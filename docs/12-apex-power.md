# 12 — Apex Power: Reaching Coercive Harm at the Top

**Status: ACTIVE (opened 2026-07-11, session `apex-power`). Addresses OBJ-027 — the
framework's #1 gap (doc 10 Finding B).**

## Purpose and the honest frame

Doc 10 Finding B is the framework's deepest problem: every enforcement mechanism it built
bites **downward** (the domestic-gated offense reaches the partner; negligent-retention
reaches the manager but not the CEO who *is* the employer; predominant-aggressor calls are
made by power-embedded prosecutors). A framework whose founding insight is that the pattern
runs *up* every scale of power cannot honor that insight if it only catches the relatively
powerless. This doc is the missing workstream.

**Disconfirming discipline up front (CLAUDE.md agreement 1; S-18).** "Let's do apex power"
is a primed push toward *triumphantly closing* the gap. The refutation test I held this doc
to: *do the candidate mechanisms actually reach apex **coercive/psychological** harm — the
framework's paradigm — or do they only reach adjacent wrongs (fraud, corruption, atrocity,
harassment-narrowly) and re-import the institution-capture problem (Finding C)?* The honest
answer is **partial**: the apex gap narrows but does not close, and the residual is named in
§6 rather than papered over. This doc **downgrades OBJ-027 from `UNRESOLVED` to
`PARTIALLY MITIGATED`, not `RESOLVED`.**

## 1. Why the domestic gate fails upward — and why the concept nonetheless travels

The offense (doc 03) is gated to intimate/family/household relationships. That gate is what
makes it unable to reach an executive or official. But the framework's *core concept* —
coercive control as an ongoing pattern that dominates, isolates, and strips autonomy — is
**already being extended beyond the domestic in the research literature** `[SUPPORTED]`:
scholarship now situates coercive control in workplaces and institutions ("Understanding
Coercive Control Within and Beyond the Domestic Environment," White Rose 2024; "Observing
coercive control beyond intimate partner violence," 2020; work-related IPV reviews 2025).
So reaching upward is **the concept traveling as its own scholars already take it**, not a
bolt-on. The mechanisms of control (domination, dependency-creation, isolation, systematic
regulation of another's life) are relationship-form-independent; the *domestic gate* is a
jurisdictional convenience, not a boundary of the phenomenon.

**Limit:** recognizing the phenomenon upward is not the same as having an *enforcement tool*
upward. §§2–4 are about tools; this section only establishes the concept is not
category-bound.

## 2. The core move — invert the liability vector

Negligent-retention (doc 05) reaches **down**: the employer is liable for *keeping* a
harmful subordinate. The apex principle is its mirror:

> **Liability should rise with effective control.** The more power a person has over a
> domain, the more they are answerable for coercive-harm patterns in it — including their
> own conduct and their failure to stop it.

The doctrinal template for this is **command / superior responsibility** (international
criminal law; Rome Statute Art. 28; *Yamashita*): a superior is liable for subordinates'
conduct they knew or should have known about and failed to prevent or punish. `[ESTABLISHED]`
as doctrine. We borrow its **structure — liability follows effective control and flows up —
NOT its content** (it is atrocity law; importing it wholesale would be exactly the
"anchor as ammunition" error, CLAUDE.md agreement 7). `[NORMATIVE]` as a transplant to
coercive-harm patterns, and **untested** — this is the doc's weakest load-bearing idea (see
end). Its value is orientation: it shows a mature legal system *can* make power itself the
basis of liability, which is the inversion Finding B says the framework lacks.

## 3. Live mechanisms that already reach up (grounded, current)

These exist now. Each reaches the apex for a *narrower* slice than the domestic offense
reaches a partner — the honest pattern is that they reach **oversight-failure and
failure-to-prevent**, not the apex actor's direct coercive conduct.

### 3a. Oversight (Caremark) duty extended to officers — *In re McDonald's* (Del. Ch. 2023) `[ESTABLISHED]`
For the first time, the Delaware Court of Chancery held that **corporate officers** (not
just directors) owe a fiduciary **duty of oversight**; a Chief People Officer who
"consciously ignored red flags" of a toxic sexual-harassment culture can breach it. This
reaches the **C-suite** for conscious blind-eye to a coercive-conduct pattern.
- **Disconfirming limits (all real):** (1) liability requires **bad faith / conscious
  disregard** — a deliberately high bar; mere negligence won't do. (2) In McDonald's the
  *directors'* claims were **dismissed** because they acted once they learned — so the tool
  bites only the *conscious* blind-eye, not the slow one. (3) It is a **shareholder
  derivative** suit routed through Delaware corporate courts, and recovery runs to the
  **corporation, not the victims** — a governance instrument, not a victim remedy (Finding C
  residue). (4) It reaches *failure to oversee*, not the executive's own coercive conduct.

### 3b. Positive duty to prevent — UK Worker Protection Act 2023; Australia Respect@Work `[ESTABLISHED]`
A structural shift from **reactive to proactive**: the UK Act (in force **26 Oct 2024**)
requires employers to take "reasonable steps" to *prevent* sexual harassment; Australia's
Respect@Work positive duty (2022, enforceable from Dec 2023) obliges employers to
*proactively eliminate* it "as far as possible." This places the obligation on
**leadership**, before harm, and — crucially for Finding C — is enforced by **independent
equality regulators (EHRC / AHRC), not prosecutors**, partially escaping the
prosecutor-capture problem. UK tribunals can uplift compensation up to 25% for breach.
- **Disconfirming limits:** harassment-*specific* (not the full coercive/psychological
  pattern); "reasonable steps" is a **soft** standard; in the UK model an individual cannot
  enforce the duty **standalone** (only the regulator can) — the individual gets the uplift
  only riding a successful harassment claim. Reaches *prevention failure*, not the apex
  actor directly.

### 3c. Misconduct / abuse of public office `[SUPPORTED]`
For the *state-power* apex: common-law misconduct in public office (England/Wales) and
analogous abuse-of-office offenses reach officials who abuse their position, **including
non-financial** abuse. This is the closest existing analogue to "coercive harm by someone
who *is* the institution" in the public sector. **Limit:** enforced by the same prosecutorial
apparatus the seed indicted (Finding C), and rarely charged for non-corruption conduct.

### 3d. Individual targeted sanctions — Global Magnitsky model `[SUPPORTED]`
Personal consequences (asset freeze, travel ban) imposed on **individual** apex human-rights
abusers, bypassing the protection of their own state/institution; **non-prosecutorial**
(executive-administrative). Anchor value: proof that a legal order *can* attach personal cost
to apex actors otherwise untouchable. **Limit — sharp:** geopolitically **selective** and
executive-discretion-driven, i.e., it is itself a **power instrument** and can be
weaponized; not a neutral tool. Cite as existence-proof, not as a model to emulate uncritically.

## 4. The independent-review requirement (answers Finding C for the apex) `[NORMATIVE]`

Finding B named a second defect: apex predominant-aggressor / power-vector determinations
(doc 02) are exactly the ones a prosecutor embedded in the power cannot credibly make. The
structural response is to **pair the reach-up liability doctrines (§§2–3) with
determinations by independent, non-prosecutorial bodies** — inspectors-general, ombudsmen,
statutory public inquiries, equality regulators (the EHRC/AHRC model in §3b is the working
example). Preconditions:
- **Whistleblower protection** as the *precondition* for apex pattern-documentation to
  surface at all (the powerful control the information environment; without protected
  disclosure there is no record to review). `[SUPPORTED]` that whistleblower regimes exist;
  `[NORMATIVE]` that they should extend to coercive-harm patterns, not only financial/safety.
- **Independence from the reviewed power** — the body cannot be one the apex actor staffs,
  funds at will, or can dissolve. This is the hardest design constraint and the one most
  often defeated in practice.
`NEEDS-LIBERTY-REVIEW` — an independent apex-review body is itself a power that can be
captured or weaponized (a standing inquisitor into executives/officials); it must pass the
same six prongs as any other mechanism (doc 07).

## 5. Power-asymmetric calibration (links OBJ-028) — proposed, NOT adopted

Finding D: the both-victims safeguard is "DARVO the powerful can afford." The apex response
is to calibrate the anti-weaponization defense to the **power differential** — the more
effective control the accused holds, the higher the bar for the "I'm being weaponized"
defense and the more weight on documented course-of-conduct. **This is liberty-fraught**
(equal protection; treating the powerful differently under law) and may be unworkable.
Retained as a *proposal*, `NEEDS-LIBERTY-REVIEW`, cross-referenced to doc 10 item 2 /
OBJ-028. Do not treat as adopted.

## 6. The irreducible residual (the disconfirming honesty)

What the mechanisms above still **cannot** reach — stated plainly so doc 09 cannot imply the
gap is closed:

1. **The true apex.** The founder-CEO with voting control and a captive board; the
   controlling shareholder; the head of state. Here the accused **is** the institution and
   **no internal body has independent power over them** — §§3a–3b presuppose a board, a
   regulator, or a corporate form that can be turned against the actor. For these, law
   largely fails, and the real checks are **political, reputational (exposure), and exit**.
   The framework should say this, not perform a legal remedy it does not have.
2. **Finding C is mitigated, not eliminated.** Every mechanism in §3 still routes through
   *some* institution power influences (Delaware courts, regulators, prosecutors, executive
   sanction-authorities). §4's independent-review requirement is the mitigation; its own
   capturability (§4 caveat) is the residual.
3. **Coverage asymmetry with the paradigm case.** The domestic offense reaches a partner's
   *direct coercive conduct*; the apex tools mostly reach **failure-to-prevent** and
   **oversight-failure** (§§3a–3b), which is a *different and narrower wrong*. The framework
   reaches the apex actor best when they **presided over** a pattern, and worst when they
   **personally are** the pattern — the inverse of what Finding B would most want.

**Net:** OBJ-027 moves from "no tool" to "a partial toolkit for the *sub-apex* powerful
(officers, senior managers, public officials in institutions with live oversight), plus an
honest admission that the *true* apex remains largely beyond law." That is real progress and
an honest ceiling in the same breath.

## Weakest load-bearing element (per CLAUDE.md agreement 1)

The **command-responsibility transplant (§2)** is the softest point: it is `[NORMATIVE]`,
untested for non-atrocity coercive harm, and carries the agreement-7 hazard of importing
atrocity law's severity by analogy. It is doing *orientational*, not *operational*, work —
and the doc must not let it read as an enacted mechanism. Second-softest: the live tools
(§3) reach **harassment / oversight-failure**, and treating them as if they reach the full
coercive/psychological pattern would repeat the "component-validation laundering
whole-claim" error (S-11). Claim the narrow reach; flag the extension as `[HYPOTHESIS]`.

## Open questions

- Can the "reach-up" liability doctrines be extended from **sexual harassment** (their
  current live domain) to the broader **coercive-control pattern** without losing the
  definiteness that got them enacted? (Bridges to doc 03 vagueness work.)
- Is an independent apex-review body designable so it is neither captured by the power it
  reviews nor a weaponizable standing inquisitor? (The §4 knot; `/liberty-review`.)
- Does the "liability rises with effective control" principle have a **civil** home
  (enhanced duty of care for high-control actors) that avoids the atrocity-law baggage of
  the command-responsibility framing?
