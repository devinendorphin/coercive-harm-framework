# 01 — Problem Statement

*Synthesis of the institutional gaps identified in the April 2024 seed conversation
(`source/seed-conversation-2024.txt`). This is descriptive: it states the problem
the framework exists to address. Claims carry epistemic-status tags; the sharpest
critiques of the problem framing itself are logged via `/steelman` in doc 08.*

---

## The core gap

Institutions — courts, police, hospitals, workplaces — are built to recognize
injury that leaves a mark. A category of serious harm leaves no visible mark: harm
inflicted through words, narratives, deception, isolation, and the sustained
manipulation of a person's sense of reality. This harm is `[SUPPORTED]` as real
and physiologically consequential (sustained coercive control produces measurable
psychological and bodily injury), yet the institutions that would remedy visible
injury systematically fail to see it, prove it, or safely process it. The project's
one-line framing: *plug the gaps in judicial, medical, and workplace treatment of
a new injury category — coercive/psychological harm — without importing stigma or
enabling new discrimination* (L4).

The harm is "recent in its development but ancient in practice" (README): the
conduct is old, but naming it as a compensable/prosecutable injury category is new,
and the institutional machinery hasn't caught up.

## The specific institutional gaps

**1. Evidentiary invisibility (restraining orders).** "A lot of emotional abuse
situations don't necessarily involve wounds that are visible[,] so... victims of
[such ab]use have a really tough time establishing that... precedent" (L8). Without
physical evidence, the threshold showing for protective orders is hard to meet.
`[SUPPORTED]`

**2. Courtroom re-traumatization.** Existing procedure can force a victim to "face
[their] perpetrator" and to "narrate over and over again... to an enforcement
apparatus [that] does not believe her" (L8, L91). The procedure is asymmetric: for
a target still "rawly trying to process their past" it is nauseating and
attritional; for a perpetrator with a different relationship to their own feelings
"it's not as nausea[ting]" — so the process filters *out* genuine victims and
filters *for* those who can endure it (L91). `[HYPOTHESIS]` on the asymmetry
mechanism; `[SUPPORTED]` that confrontation re-traumatizes.

**3. Medical-setting access control.** Hospitals and clinics "[come] from with no"
protocol to exclude family members "they deem to be not appropriate to be there,"
leaving abusers with access to isolated, vulnerable patients (L8). `[HYPOTHESIS]`
(specific-claim; needs verification of current hospital visitation/protective law).

**4. Scattered, incoherent consent law.** Consent doctrine is "very scattered and
very messy... even in the law books," sometimes not even about sexual assault — the
example given is a New York statute touching arson and "emphatic content" that
"doesn't necessarily deal with... the presence of force[,] fraud or fear" (L8).
The gap is the absence of a consolidated force/fraud/fear spine across consent
contexts. `[HYPOTHESIS]` — the specific NY statutory claim needs checking in
`/develop 03`; force/fraud/fear as organizing elements is `[ESTABLISHED]`.

**5. Prosecutorial and systemic evasion.** Perpetrators "navigate the labyrinthine
aspects of law and exploit it," "play... to the parts of us that want to believe
in the good in all people," wait out oversight, and if they fail "just pick up and
do it elsewhere... take on another persona" (L91). Enforcement personnel may
themselves have "been inflicting that type of [harm] on vulnerable communities for
decades" (L91), compounding disbelief. `[HYPOTHESIS]`/`[NORMATIVE]`.

## The cross-scale claim

Endorphin insists the pattern "exist[s] not only on the interpersonal level but in
the community[,] city[,] state[,] regional[,] institutional[,] professional[,]
governmental[,] international realms" (L36), recurring with near-identical tactics
"regardless of race[,] ethnicity[,] gender[,] age[,] disability" (L59). His own
evidence base is autobiographical and cross-strata: a five-person household with
two abusive partners (L40); an arts organization where a hired "equal" induced
stress (L40); a Flatbush mental-health agency where newly-empowered leadership of a
historically marginalized community reproduced the same abuses (L69); and
geopolitics (L276). `[HYPOTHESIS]` — structurally important and, as the seed itself
flags, the claim most in need of empirical grounding rather than pattern-matching
(L177). The Flatbush example is retained deliberately: it establishes that
power-abuse is not identity-bound, which the framework needs for its own
anti-discrimination integrity (feeds doc 07).

## The constraint that shapes everything

The seed states the project's own guardrail before the project asks for it:
"explicitly delineating characteristics could potentially resemble or enable
discriminatory practices, which must be avoided at all costs" (L107). Two design
commitments follow, and they are non-negotiable (`[NORMATIVE]`):

- **Behavior, not people.** "Focusing on patterns of behavior rather than innate
  characteristics" (L112); impacts on victims over motivations of perpetrators
  (L113). Any drift toward person-typing, diagnosis, or suspected-neurology
  classification is an automatic fail (see doc 07, `/liberty-review`).
- **Both victims.** Because false accusation ("confession through accusation") is
  itself a core tactic of the harm, any mechanism built to catch it can be turned
  by an abuser against a true victim. The framework is only as good as its
  safeguards against its own weaponization.

## Why now

Endorphin situates the project at an inflection: a period (he dates it ~2015–2017)
when decentralized media let survivors "crowdsource recognition of these behavioral
patterns" and put shared language to previously idiosyncratic experiences (L162,
L167). The claim is that awareness has outrun institutions — people can now *name*
the harm faster than courts, clinics, and workplaces can *process* it — and that
the gap between recognition and remedy is where this framework does its work. He
frames the stakes at civilizational scale: an empathy/cooperation deficit that
"might f*** us over in the most essential part of us needing to change ourselves
during climate change" (L80). `[NORMATIVE]` — motivating vision, not a load-bearing
premise; the framework must stand without it.

## What this problem statement is *not* claiming

Recorded here so later drafts don't quietly inflate scope:
- Not that a specific brain structure causes the conduct (that's a doc-02/06
  `[HYPOTHESIS]`, not a premise of the problem).
- Not that the pattern maps onto any diagnostic category (behavior-not-people).
- Not that every death behind an addiction or suicide is a homicide (doc 03 handles
  the far narrower prosecutable-subset claim).

## Weakest load-bearing element (per `CLAUDE.md` agreement 1)

The cross-scale claim is doing enormous structural work — it is what makes this "a
framework" rather than "a set of interpersonal-abuse reforms" — while resting almost
entirely on one person's pattern recognition across his own life. The seed itself
concedes the empirical base is thin (L177, L191). If the cross-scale universality
does not survive contact with sociological evidence, the interpersonal and
workplace pieces still stand on their own; the grand-unified framing does not. The
first evidence-check should pressure-test whether the framework *needs* the
cross-scale claim or merely *wants* it.
