# 05 — Accommodation Model

**Status: ACTIVE (developed 2026-07-09, session `develop-05`)**

## Purpose

Develop the ADA-inspired framing: for the non-violent part of the spectrum, structural
adjustment — role design that removes the leverage through which harm occurs (no
financial authority, no direct reports) — rather than punishment, backed by an
"honest-capacity-statement" culture and reusable administrative templates. This was
the framework's most double-edged move because "calling a behavior pattern a
disability" is exactly the person-typing docs 02/07 forbid (OBJ-004). **This session's
central result: the ADA research resolves that tension** — the ADA already separates
*status* from *conduct*, and the framework can borrow its *architecture* without
invoking its *legal disability classification*. See §0.

## 0a. Elevated role (division of labor, from the doc-03 steelman)

Doc 05 is no longer the framework's "soft, optional half." The §9 steelman established
that criminal law cannot reach the **pure-words** case (OBJ-023/019), so for the seed's
paradigm harm — invisible, words-only coercion — **the accommodation model and the
therapeutic pathways (doc 06) are the *primary* instruments.** This raises the stakes on
doc 05's own weakest element (enforceability, below): the framework is leaning its
central case on an instrument that is currently voluntary. See doc 01, "The division of
labor across instruments."

## 0. Resolution of the founding contradiction (OBJ-004)

From `research/ada-accommodation-architecture.md`:

- **The trap:** to claim ADA accommodation *as a legal right* you must establish a
  "qualified individual with a **disability**" — a diagnosis. That classifies a
  *person* by *condition*: the person-typing the framework forbids.
- **The escape:** the ADA's own doctrine is **conduct-based, not status-excusing**.
  EEOC: "improper behavior in and of itself does not constitute a disability, and
  having a disability does not excuse employees from... the same conduct standards";
  accommodation is "always prospective" (no duty to excuse past misconduct);
  reassignment/demotion is a recognized forward-looking accommodation. Borrow this
  **architecture**, applied on the basis of **documented conduct and risk**, as a
  *voluntary organizational-design practice* — **without** invoking the legal
  disability status. [ESTABLISHED architecture; `[NORMATIVE]` application]
- **Result:** no one is classified as disabled; the organization applies conduct-based,
  ADA-shaped role design. "Disability" survives only as the *design analogy* Endorphin
  himself flagged as a "framing" (L118), not a clinical or legal label. **OBJ-004
  dissolves; OBJ-012** (two-tier person-typing) is answered by sorting on documented
  conduct, never inferred capacity-to-change.

## 1. The four borrowed principles (the model's spine)

1. **Conduct standards bind everyone equally.** Not an excuse mechanism. This is the
   behavior-not-people spine *and* the primary protection for potential subordinates.
   `[NORMATIVE]`, ADA-grounded.
2. **No excusing past harm.** Accommodation is prospective; it is not a sick-note for
   damage already done. Protects victims and keeps the model honest. `[NORMATIVE]`,
   ADA-grounded.
3. **Prospective structural role design keyed to documented conduct.** The "no budget
   / no reports" move (seed L118) is doctrinally a *reassignment/role-design
   accommodation* that removes the leverage through which harm propagates, enabling
   the person to meet the conduct standard going forward. `[NORMATIVE]`
   `NEEDS-LIBERTY-REVIEW`.
4. **Honest-capacity-statement culture.** Normalizing the statement of one's
   limitations without it reading as weakness (seed L118). The cultural diagnosis is
   `[SUPPORTED]` (organizational-behavior/psychological-safety literature); the remedy
   is `[NORMATIVE]`.

## 2. The administrative template (what Endorphin asked to build, L118)

A reusable, conduct-based role-design instrument — the "administrative template for a
very unique situation that... is everywhere in every industry." Sketch, all
`[NORMATIVE]` / `NEEDS-LIBERTY-REVIEW`:

- **Trigger:** a *documented course of conduct* (not a personality read, not a
  diagnosis) meeting a defined threshold — mirrors doc 03's course-of-conduct spine.
- **Assessment:** documented, legitimate, non-pretextual; guards against a biased
  "conduct read" (a weaponization vector — see §4).
- **Design levers:** remove/limit budget authority; remove direct reports; scope to
  individual-contributor or outreach-type work the person does well; define
  escalation and review.
- **Framing to the person:** role fit and structural design, on the model of ADA
  reassignment — *not* accusation or diagnosis. Pairs with the self-directed pathways
  (doc 06), which the person must arrive at themselves.

## 3. Acknowledged incompleteness

Accommodation removes *leverage*; it does not remediate the *underlying driver*. It
must pair with the self-directed therapeutic pathways (doc 06), and the seed's
self-arrival constraint means the org can create conditions but cannot compel the
change. Accommodation is containment, not cure. `[NORMATIVE]`

## 3a. Enforceability: soft-law levers (no diagnosis) — closing the weakest-element gap

Resolving OBJ-004 by dropping the ADA disability hook also dropped the legal *duty* to
accommodate. These conduct-based levers give the model teeth **without** reintroducing a
diagnosis (`research/accommodation-enforceability-levers.md`):

1. **Negligent retention / supervision (existing tort) — the primary lever.**
   `[ESTABLISHED]` An employer is liable when it **knows or should know** an employee
   endangers others, **fails to act** (terminate, **reassign**, address), harm is
   **foreseeable**, and damages result. The model's "no reports / no budget"
   role-design **is** a recognized corrective action that *discharges* this duty;
   ignoring a **documented course of coercive conduct** and leaving the person in an
   authority role **exposes** the employer. This converts accommodation from "nice-to-
   have" into **liability-driven risk management**, entirely conduct-based, no diagnosis.
   It also fits the framework's aim exactly: it protects *potential subordinates* via the
   employer's *existing* duty of care, not a new protected class.
2. **Insurance (EPLI / D&O).** `[SUPPORTED]` Insurers already price governance and drive
   anti-harassment practice; they can require/reward conduct-based role-design as a
   condition or discount — a market lever, no legislation.
3. **Procurement / grant conditions.** `[NORMATIVE]` Funders condition grants on adopting
   the template — the operative lever at the nonprofit/agency scale (Flatbush, L69).
4. **Sector codes / accreditation.** `[NORMATIVE]` Adoption of the §2 template becomes
   **evidence of due care**, looping back into lever 1's liability calculus.

**Both-victims caution:** liability exposure can push employers to *over*-react —
defensively sidelining anyone accused. The §4 safeguard (documented, non-pretextual
assessment; review/appeal) must gate the liability-driven response, or the lever becomes
a weaponization vector. **Honest limit:** these create *employer incentives*, not a
*direct entitlement* for the person — enforceability is now **partially** answered, not
fully.

## 4. Weaponization & liberty flags (for `/liberty-review`)

- **Biased conduct-read.** "This person needs the no-authority role" can be weaponized
  to sideline, demote, or defame a targeted employee (both-victims lens; OBJ-004's
  live residue). Safeguard: the trigger is *documented course of conduct* on the same
  evidentiary discipline as doc 03, with review and appeal — never a manager's
  impression.
- **Adverse-action exposure.** Role-stripping absent a disability process or a
  disciplinary finding can itself be challenged as wrongful demotion / defamation /
  discrimination. The conduct assessment must be documented and non-pretextual.
- **Disparate impact.** Who gets read as "needs containment" vs. "high performer" can
  track race/class/gender/neurodivergence. Monitor. `NEEDS-LIBERTY-REVIEW`.

## Weakest load-bearing element (per `CLAUDE.md` agreement 1)

*Updated 2026-07-09 — partially closed.* Resolving OBJ-004 by dropping the disability
hook (§0) removed the legal *duty* to accommodate. §3a now supplies the missing teeth
**without a diagnosis** — chiefly **negligent-retention/supervision** liability (existing
tort: the role-design discharges an employer's existing duty of care; ignoring documented
conduct exposes them), plus insurance, procurement, and accreditation levers. **What
remains open:** these create *employer incentives*, not a *direct entitlement* for the
person — so the person's interest in non-stigmatizing treatment still depends on how well
the organization executes (gated by §4). The model has moved from "no teeth" to
"conduct-based incentives, no individual right." That residual — and the risk that
liability pressure drives *over*-reaction — is the standing weak point.

## Open questions

- **The enforceability frontier.** Is there a soft-law lever (D&O insurance
  incentives, grantor/procurement conduct standards, professional codes) that gives
  the model teeth *without* reintroducing a diagnosis requirement?
- **Self-request vs. imposition.** ADA accommodation is normally employee-*requested*;
  here the person often lacks the self-awareness to request it (the pattern's whole
  point). Employer-*imposed* role design is where adverse-action and both-victims
  risks concentrate (§4). Where is the defensible middle?
- **Does role-stripping stigmatize or protect?** "No reports, no budget" protects
  potential subordinates but marks the person. Framed as ADA-style reassignment (a
  recognized, non-punitive move) it reads as design, not scarlet letter — but only if
  the org culture actually treats it that way.
- **Interaction with doc 03.** Should the accommodation template be the *civil-first*
  front end for workplace-scale coercive harm (parallel to protective orders at the
  domestic scale)? This is where the bounded cross-scale claim (doc 01) cashes out.
