# 05 — Accommodation Model

**Status: STUB (seeded, undeveloped)**

## Purpose

Develop the ADA-inspired framing: for the non-violent part of the spectrum, treat
the underlying deficit as something requiring *structural accommodation* rather than
punishment — role design that removes the leverage through which harm occurs (no
financial authority, no direct reports), backed by an "honest-capacity-statement"
culture and reusable administrative templates. This is the framework's most
constructive and most double-edged move: calling a behavior pattern a "disability"
is exactly the person-typing the framework forbids elsewhere, so this doc lives in
permanent tension with docs 02 and 07 and must be developed alongside the
disability-rights objections in doc 08.

## Candidate model elements (from the seed)

1. **Accommodation-not-punishment** for non-violent cases — the ADA framing. `[NORMATIVE]`.
2. **Role design that removes leverage** — "no financial strings," "no one under
   her," matching a person to work they can do without the authority through which
   harm propagates. `[NORMATIVE]` (an administrative template).
3. **Honest-capacity-statement culture** — normalizing stating one's limitations
   without it reading as weakness. `[SUPPORTED]` cultural diagnosis, `[NORMATIVE]`
   remedy.
4. **Acknowledged incompleteness** — accommodation alone doesn't address the
   underlying driver; it must pair with self-directed therapeutic pathways (doc 06).
   `[NORMATIVE]`.

## Open questions

- **The core contradiction.** Doc 02/07 forbid person-typing; the accommodation
  model *requires* categorizing a person as needing accommodation. How is this
  reconciled without a diagnosis? (Possible resolution: accommodate the *documented
  conduct/impact*, structurally, without labeling the person — but does "disability"
  language survive that? The disability-rights persona in `/steelman` will attack
  here.)
- **Who invokes it, and who benefits?** Is accommodation requested by the person, or
  imposed by an employer? Imposition collides with the self-arrival constraint
  (doc 06) and with anti-discrimination law; self-request requires the very
  self-awareness the pattern lacks.
- **Weaponization.** Could "you need accommodation" become a tool to sideline,
  demote, or defame a targeted employee (both-victims lens)?
- **Does role-stripping stigmatize or protect?** "No reports, no budget" protects
  potential subordinates but marks the person — is that a lawful, humane
  accommodation or a scarlet letter?

## Research needed

- **ADA scope:** does/should the ADA reach behavioral patterns absent a recognized
  diagnosis? Case law on personality-disorder accommodation claims; the
  "direct threat" and "essential functions" doctrines.
- **Employment law** on demotion/role-restriction as accommodation vs. adverse
  action; disability-disclosure and confidentiality rules.
- **Organizational-behavior literature** on capacity/limitation disclosure, honest
  role-matching, and psychological safety (relevant to the "capacity-statement
  culture" claim).
- **Disability-rights critique** of pathologizing conduct — self-advocacy movement
  positions on who benefits/loses when behavior is medicalized.
