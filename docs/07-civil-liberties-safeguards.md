# 07 — Civil-Liberties Safeguards

**Status: STANDING QUALITY DOCUMENT (seeded)**

## Purpose

Hold the framework to its own founding constraint: it must not become a tool for the
very abuse it targets. This doc records due-process, anti-discrimination, and
anti-weaponization protections, and is the home base for `/liberty-review` verdicts.
The seed set this standard before proposing a single mechanism — "explicitly
delineating characteristics could potentially resemble or enable discriminatory
practices, which must be avoided at all costs" (L107) — and that sentence is the
spine of this document.

## The six review prongs (from `/liberty-review`)

Every legal mechanism (doc 03) and every accommodation (doc 05) is tested against:

1. **Vagueness** — could an ordinary person know in advance what conduct is
   prohibited?
2. **Person vs. pattern** — does any language target *who someone is* (traits,
   suspected neurology, diagnosis) rather than documented conduct? Automatic fail.
3. **Weaponization inversion** — could an abuser use this mechanism *against* their
   victim? (Confession-through-accusation makes this the signature risk.)
4. **Due-process floor** — presumption of innocence, confrontation reconciled with
   victim protection, proportional penalty, appeal path.
5. **Speech boundary** — where does it touch protected expression, and is the
   conduct/speech line defensible?
6. **Disparate impact** — how might it land unevenly across race, class, gender,
   disability, given documented enforcement disparities?

## Relevant passages from the seed

- **Founding constraint** (~L107): "explicitly delineating characteristics could
  potentially resemble or enable discriminatory practices, which must be avoided at
  all costs. Extreme care is needed in how this is defined."
- **The minefield** (~L102): "will it or will it not resemble... very problematic
  methods of discrimination that have also historically [caused harm]... a potential
  minefield of concepts that needs to be avoided."
- **Design commitments** (~L112–L115): "focusing on patterns of behavior rather than
  innate characteristics"; "highlighting impacts on victims rather than motivations
  of perpetrators"; "due process protections like specialized advocacy for both
  parties."
- **Weaponization is intrinsic** (~L80): "confession through accusation" — the
  perpetrator accuses the victim of the perpetrator's own conduct — means a
  false accuser can wear the victim's clothes. Any detection mechanism must assume
  this adversary.
- **Enforcement bias is real and cross-community** (~L69, L91): the Flatbush agency
  reproducing abuse, and police who "have been inflicting that type of [harm] on
  vulnerable communities for decades," ground the disparate-impact prong in the
  seed's own testimony.

## Second review pass (2026-11-01): the *Something Was Wrong* cross-season findings

*Source: `research/something-was-wrong-s17-s24.md` X3, X7, X10, X12, X17, built on S1–S24.
Each mechanism below is a **safeguard or a norm**, not a new offence. That lowers the stakes
on prongs 1 and 5 and raises them on prong 3: a safeguard that an abuser can turn is worse
than no safeguard, because it carries the framework's authority.*

### R2-1. Name both false-accusation channels (X3) — `CLEARED-WITH-AMENDMENTS`

**The mechanism.** Doc 07's anti-weaponization safeguards name two channels, not one:
- **fabricated reports**: S18's social worker's false assault report against an 11-year-old
  client (record); S22's admitted false accusation against a legislator;
- **legal process turned on reporters**: S9's restraining orders against the target and her
  father; S14's two harassment petitions against the mother of the child he pleaded to
  assaulting; S18's restraining order against a whistleblower; S15h's stalking statute cited
  against the target; the "#PAS" post.

**Prongs.**
1. *Vagueness.* As a principle ("watch for retaliatory process") it is vague. It clears only
   as an **enumerated screen**: (a) timing, the filing follows a report, an exit or a
   disclosure; (b) mirroring, the filing's allegation reverse-maps the original one
   (confession-through-accusation, 02 item 1); (c) the filer is the party with the documented
   course of conduct.
2. *Person vs. pattern.* Passes: every indicator is conduct or timing.
3. *Weaponization.* **The live risk is a race to file.** S9's perpetrator filed first, in the
   target's absence, and her replies then became violations. A rule keyed to "who filed
   second" would hand that move a safeguard. Amendment: **filing order is never
   dispositive**, and the screen applies to both filings.
4. *Due process.* Filing is petitioning. The rule changes **scrutiny and sequencing**
   (consolidated hearing, the documented course of conduct heard first), never access. The
   existing doctrinal model is the *Noerr–Pennington* sham exception: objectively baseless
   litigation brought for the process rather than the outcome. State anti-SLAPP statutes are
   the nearest procedural model; their coverage of reports to police varies by state and must
   be checked before any reliance.
5. *Speech.* Reports to authorities and court filings are protected petitioning. Only the
   sham category falls outside it.
6. *Disparate impact.* Credibility contests track race, gender, disability and resources;
   the target least able to file first or hire counsel is the one the race-to-file harms.
   Monitor outcomes by those axes.

**Answers in part the open question** "what is the structural safeguard against
confession-through-accusation weaponization?" A procedural design (consolidation, sequencing,
the enumerated screen), not a principle. Partly, because the screen is applied by the
power-embedded institutions the framework distrusts (Finding C residual).

### R2-2. Denial alone meets nothing; recantation alone defeats nothing (X12) — `CLEARED-WITH-AMENDMENTS`

**The mechanism.** No element of the identification rubric may be satisfied by the subject's
denial alone, and none may be defeated by the complainant's recantation alone. Both need
independent indicators. Source: S24's programs ("if you deny that, then you are lying"; five
forced false confessions) are the unfalsifiable structure a coercive-harm rubric is most at
risk of reproducing.

**Prongs.** It passes 1, 2, 5 and 6 on its face: it is a definite evidentiary rule, conduct-
neutral and protective of both sides. **Three amendments came out of prongs 3 and 4:**
- **Admissions under leverage.** The mirror of the rule: no element is met by an admission
  obtained under leverage (S24's compelled confessions; a confession as the price of food,
  calls or release). Existing involuntary-confession doctrine reaches only state action; this
  extends the same discipline to the rubric whoever extracted it.
- **"Independent indicator" is defined**: a contemporaneous record, a third-party witness or a
  documentary trail. **Demeanour is excluded.** The corpus shows tears moving a detective and a
  judge in S14 and an officer in S13o, while a Washington judge set the character letters
  against the child's photo. Demeanour does not track honesty (CODEBOOK 2c gate).
- **DARVO conflicts with the rule as drafted, so 02 item 1 is amended.** DARVO's observable
  form ("denies + attacks the confronter + claims the victim role") can be met by an innocent
  person protesting a false accusation. Amendment: the DARVO item is scored **only** where the
  reversal content is independently shown false, or the "attack" is documented retaliatory
  conduct (a counter-filing, a smear to the network, an employment attack), **never on
  protest speech alone.** Without this, the rubric would convert a falsely accused person's
  denial into evidence. That is the S24 structure under a different name.

### R2-3. One justification test for deception, whoever deceives (X17) — `CLEARED-WITH-AMENDMENTS`

**The mechanism.** The corpus assigns deception's moral valence by who deceives: S20 condemns a
catfish persona; S21E19 and S22's stings celebrate deception against a wrongdoer; S23's
institution infiltrated the survivors' group; S24's programs lied to children "for their own
good". The framework adopts one test: deception is a harm whoever uses it, and its use needs
X7's justification (a burden on the deceiver, necessity, proportionality).

**Prongs.**
1. *Vagueness.* Definite enough **as a norm**. It does not define a prohibited act.
2. *Person vs. pattern.* Passes.
3. *Weaponization.* An abuser can say "you deceived me too" of a target who recorded calls or
   kept a hidden record. **Amendment: documentation is not deception.** Lawful recording of
   one's own conversations (one-party-consent rules vary by state) and record-keeping do not
   make a false representation. Deception means a false representation made to obtain
   something.
4. *Due process.* State investigative deception (pretext calls, stings) is governed by
   existing law. S11k's pretext call recovered the videotape. This norm does not displace that
   law. It governs what this framework endorses and how it weighs privately obtained evidence.
5. *Speech.* **Most private deception is protected speech** (*United States v. Alvarez*, 2012).
   So X17 cannot be a legal prohibition. Recorded as a framework norm only.
6. *Disparate impact.* Self-help stings are what victims fall back on when institutions do not
   answer (S22 E13: "no one will confirm to you that you have actually been defrauded"; X4).
   A strict rule would burden the least-resourced. **Amendment:** the absence of an
   institutional response counts toward necessity.

### R2-4. A label assigned by the party with power is evidence of that party's method (X10) — `CLEARED-WITH-AMENDMENTS`

**The mechanism.** S24's programs read adoption as "fear of abandonment", autism as
manipulation, religious exit as illness and queerness as pathology. The WWASP founder's letter
discredits complainants by imputed "mental illness". Proposed rule: such a label is evidence
about the labeller's method, not about the person.

**Prongs.**
1. *Vagueness.* **Over-broad as drafted.** Every treating clinician holds power over a patient,
   so read literally the rule discounts all clinical diagnosis. Amendment: scope it to labels
   assigned by a party **with a stake in the outcome** (the program paid for the placement,
   the parent seeking custody, the accused, the institution defending itself) and used to
   **discount the labelled person's account**.
2. *Person vs. pattern.* Passes, and strengthens the line: it removes person-typing as a
   credibility device.
3. *Weaponization.* An adjudicated abuser could call the court's finding a "label assigned by
   power." **Amendment: adjudicated findings of conduct are excluded.** The rule concerns
   characterological and diagnostic labels, not findings of what someone did.
4. *Due process.* The remedy is **independent evaluation** (S24 E04's and E22's independent
   evaluators broke the narrative), not automatic reversal of the label.
5. *Speech.* Not engaged.
6. *Disparate impact.* Favourable: imputed pathology falls hardest on disabled, queer and
   religiously dissenting people (S24 E03, E04, E13, E20).

### R2-5. Doc 05's role restriction under the override test (X7) — `CLEARED-WITH-AMENDMENTS`

**The mechanism.** Doc 05 §1.3 and the §2 template remove budget authority and direct reports
on a documented course of conduct. X7 says every override of a person's autonomy is a harm
needing justification. A role restriction is an override.

**The distinction that saves it, and the place it was failing.** X7 is aimed at overrides **for
the person's own good**: paternalism. A role restriction justified by **protection of others**
(subordinates exposed to a documented course of conduct) rests on the harm principle, and on
the employer's existing negligent-retention duty (§3a). That justification can carry the
burden. **But the §2 template dresses the protective measure as care**: "Framing to the person:
role fit and structural design … not accusation." That is the care-register move X7 names,
and a deception X17 would have to justify. It also defeats due process: a person not told the
documented basis cannot contest it.

**Amendments (binding):**
- The measure is justified and communicated as a **conduct-based protective measure**, not as
  care.
- **The documented basis is disclosed to the person**, with a hearing and an appeal.
- **Least restrictive** design; **time-limited** with scheduled review; a stated **restoration
  path**.
- The doc 06 pairing remains an **offer**, never a condition of restoration (the self-arrival
  constraint).
- Prong 6 stands as already flagged in 05 §4: monitor who gets read as "needs containment".

### R2-6. Doc 06 under the override test (X7) — `CLEARED-WITH-AMENDMENTS`

**The mechanism.** Doc 06's self-directed-only constraint is consistent with X7: it imposes
nothing. The pressure point is §3: the improving modalities (ACT, MI) are mostly delivered
**inside court mandates**.

**Amendments (binding):**
- Treatment attached to a sentence or a civil order is labelled honestly: a **sanction**
  justified by adjudicated conduct and due process, never "help" (S24E11: "that's not an
  apology").
- Where a mandate exists, **the choice among modalities stays with the person**. That is the
  mandate-compatible form of self-arrival (S21: she chose rehab herself, and is 10.5 years
  sober; X16).
- **No compelled confession or admission script is a progress metric.** S24 E12: "When I
  left, I was not changed at all, if anything, I was just more manipulative." This ties to
  R2-2.
- Informing and referring are not overrides (X7 calibration, S23). Doc 06's outreach may tell
  people what exists; it may not decide for them.

**Reviewer's note (agreement 1).** Two of these six began as obvious goods and failed a prong on
inspection: X10 as drafted would have discounted all clinical diagnosis, and X12 collided with
the framework's own DARVO item. The doc 05 template was failing X7 in plain sight since
2026-07-09. No mechanism here cleared outright, which remains the correct posture.

## Open questions

- Can an "expedited track" (doc 03 mechanism 6) *ever* clear the weaponization and
  due-process prongs, or is speed itself the flaw?
- Does the accommodation model's "disability" framing (doc 05) survive prong 2
  (person vs. pattern), or does it inherently type people?
- What is the structural safeguard against confession-through-accusation
  weaponization — a specific procedural design, not just a principle? *(2026-11-01: partly
  answered by R2-1 and R2-2 — an enumerated screen with consolidation and sequencing, and
  the denial/recantation rule. The residual is that the screen is run by power-embedded
  institutions.)*
