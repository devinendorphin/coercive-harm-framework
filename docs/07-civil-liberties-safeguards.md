# 07 — Civil-Liberties Safeguards

**Status: STANDING QUALITY DOCUMENT (seeded)**

## Purpose

Hold the framework to its own founding constraint: it must not become a tool for the
very abuse it targets. This doc records due-process, anti-discrimination, and
anti-weaponization protections, and is the home base for `/liberty-review` verdicts.
The seed set this standard before proposing a single mechanism — "explicitly
delineating characteristics could potentially resemble or enable discriminatory
practices, which must be avoided at all costs" (L107) — and that sentence is the
spine of this document.

## The six review prongs (from `/liberty-review`)

Every legal mechanism (doc 03) and every accommodation (doc 05) is tested against:

1. **Vagueness** — could an ordinary person know in advance what conduct is
   prohibited?
2. **Person vs. pattern** — does any language target *who someone is* (traits,
   suspected neurology, diagnosis) rather than documented conduct? Automatic fail.
3. **Weaponization inversion** — could an abuser use this mechanism *against* their
   victim? (Confession-through-accusation makes this the signature risk.)
4. **Due-process floor** — presumption of innocence, confrontation reconciled with
   victim protection, proportional penalty, appeal path.
5. **Speech boundary** — where does it touch protected expression, and is the
   conduct/speech line defensible?
6. **Disparate impact** — how might it land unevenly across race, class, gender,
   disability, given documented enforcement disparities?

## Relevant passages from the seed

- **Founding constraint** (~L107): "explicitly delineating characteristics could
  potentially resemble or enable discriminatory practices, which must be avoided at
  all costs. Extreme care is needed in how this is defined."
- **The minefield** (~L102): "will it or will it not resemble... very problematic
  methods of discrimination that have also historically [caused harm]... a potential
  minefield of concepts that needs to be avoided."
- **Design commitments** (~L112–L115): "focusing on patterns of behavior rather than
  innate characteristics"; "highlighting impacts on victims rather than motivations
  of perpetrators"; "due process protections like specialized advocacy for both
  parties."
- **Weaponization is intrinsic** (~L80): "confession through accusation" — the
  perpetrator accuses the victim of the perpetrator's own conduct — means a
  false accuser can wear the victim's clothes. Any detection mechanism must assume
  this adversary.
- **Enforcement bias is real and cross-community** (~L69, L91): the Flatbush agency
  reproducing abuse, and police who "have been inflicting that type of [harm] on
  vulnerable communities for decades," ground the disparate-impact prong in the
  seed's own testimony.

## Open questions

- Can an "expedited track" (doc 03 mechanism 6) *ever* clear the weaponization and
  due-process prongs, or is speed itself the flaw?
- Does the accommodation model's "disability" framing (doc 05) survive prong 2
  (person vs. pattern), or does it inherently type people?
- What is the structural safeguard against confession-through-accusation
  weaponization — a specific procedural design, not just a principle?
