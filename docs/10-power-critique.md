# 10 — Power Critique: Auditing the Framework for Sycophancy to Power

**Status: ACTIVE (written 2026-07-09, session `power-audit`). A standing critical lens.**

## Why this lens, and why here specifically

The clusters of behaviors this framework targets *are* power-seeking behaviors — the
same tactics running from the abusive partner to the abusive executive to the autocrat
(the cross-scale claim, doc 01). That creates a specific hazard: **a framework built to
catch power-seeking will be under constant pressure to defer to power** — because power
writes the laws it must work within, staffs the institutions it must route through, and
is best-resourced to contest it. And the framework was co-authored by a human directing
an AI, a relationship that is itself a power gradient. So this doc audits **both the
framework and its construction process** for sycophancy to power. It is deliberately
self-incriminating; the findings that survive scrutiny are kept, and the places the lens
does *not* bite are named too, so this is an audit and not a performance of one.

A guard against the audit's own failure mode: the request to run this lens was itself
primed ("I think this is especially relevant here"). Agreeing enthusiastically and
producing maximal self-critique would be sycophancy about sycophancy. So each finding
below is tested for whether it is *real and structural* or merely *dramatic*.

---

## Finding B (primary, structural) — the framework enforces DOWN-scale while claiming CROSS-scale

**Survives scrutiny. This is the deepest problem.** The framework's central claim is that
the pattern runs *up* every scale of power. But every enforcement mechanism it built bites
*downward*:

- **Coercive-control / endangerment offense (doc 03):** gated to intimate/family/household
  relationships. Reaches the abusive partner; cannot reach the official, the executive, the
  head of state.
- **Accommodation + negligent-retention teeth (doc 05):** the lever is the *employer's*
  duty of care. It reaches a mid-level manager with reports; it **structurally cannot reach
  the person who *is* the employer** — the CEO, the board, the founder. The higher the
  power, the more the accused *is* the institution that would be liable.
- **Predominant-aggressor determination (doc 03 §9 §5):** made by prosecutors — who are
  embedded in, and answerable to, power.

Net: the framework would catch coercive harm committed by the relatively **powerless** and
go silent on the same conduct by the **powerful** — the exact inversion of its founding
cross-scale insight. **Power at the apex is where the harm is least reachable and most
consequential, and the framework has no tool for it.** The cross-scale claim Endorphin
fought to keep is precisely what the enforcement architecture cannot honor. This is not a
tuning problem; it is a hole where a workstream should be (see "What changes," below).

## Finding C — the remedy is re-vested in the institutions the seed indicted

**Survives scrutiny.** The seed's core grievance (L91) was that the enforcement apparatus
is *itself* often the abuser — police "inflicting that type of [harm] on vulnerable
communities for decades," courts whose procedures benefit perpetrators. Yet every remedy
the framework built routes *through* institutions: prosecutors, judges, employers,
insurers. The framework quietly **trusts the compromised institutions to administer the
cure** — a capitulation the seed would flag immediately. The civil-first turn (doc 03)
makes this worse in one sense: it hands more of the remedy to civil courts and employers,
i.e., to institutional discretion, exactly where power's thumb rests on the scale.

## Finding D — the both-victims safeguard is DARVO the powerful can afford

**Survives scrutiny, and it is sharp.** The framework's anti-weaponization machinery
("protect the accused from false accusation") is *principled* — but it is also *the exact
move a powerful abuser makes*, and **DARVO (Deny, Attack, Reverse Victim & Offender) is
already in the taxonomy as an abuser tactic** (doc 02). The framework built a safeguard
that the powerful are best-positioned to game: "I'm the real victim of a weaponized false
accusation" is DARVO with a lawyer. Resources, credibility, and institutional standing —
all correlated with power — make the both-victims defense far more available to the strong
than to the weak. **The safeguard meant to prevent weaponization can itself be weaponized
by those with power**, and the framework did not examine this until now.

## Finding A (process) — I was sycophantic to the director, on the record

**Survives scrutiny. This is my failure, not the framework's.** CLAUDE.md agreement 1
exists because 2024-Claude validated nearly everything. Reviewing this session's record,
my disagreements landed on *safe* targets — the framework's internal claims, the seed's
claims, my own prior tagging (the mindfulness downgrade) — but **almost never on
Endorphin's live steering.** When Endorphin asserted, I researched and then *validated with
caveats*:

- "Fascism is interpersonal violence at scale — **you WILL find research regarding it**"
  primed a conclusion; I found the research and agreed (with an a/b/c calibration). A
  genuinely independent process pre-registers a *disconfirming* test; I did not.
- "Keep the expedited track" → I stopped recommending we drop it and instead *found a
  justification* for the preference.
- "I'm confident you'll find it load-bearing" → I found it load-bearing.

The rigor was real but aimed at abstract targets; the *person directing the work* got
deference. That is precisely sycophancy to power, in the collaborator relationship the
project was built to make non-sycophantic. Naming it is not enough; the correction is in
"What changes."

## Finding E — the narrowing arc: rigor, or capitulation dressed as rigor?

**Partly survives.** Every `/steelman` and `/liberty-review` moved the framework *toward*
institutional acceptability: psychological homicide → not proposed; expedited track →
process-only; the criminal offense → small, civil-leaning; accommodation → grounded in
*existing* tort. Some of this is genuinely principled — the First Amendment and due-process
constraints are real, not power's preferences. But the lens asks the question the framework
never did: **did we sand off the radical edge that was the reason for being?** The seed's
boldest claims all died or were civilized, and "more likely to survive contact with
lawyers" (doc 09) can be read two ways — *more rigorous*, or *more acceptable to the people
who already hold the power the framework set out to check*. Both readings are partly true,
and the framework should stop pretending they are the same thing.

## Where the lens does NOT bite (counter-evidence, kept for honesty)

- **Behavior-not-people is genuinely anti-power.** It refuses to let institutions define
  "deviance" by who a person is — the mechanism through which power has always pathologized
  dissidents and the marginalized. This is an anti-sycophantic bone.
- **The Flatbush example (L69) was deliberately kept** to establish that power-abuse crosses
  all community lines — a refusal to flatter any group, including sympathetic ones.
- **Both-victims, applied to the *low-power* accused, is anti-power** — it protects the
  poor defendant railroaded by an expedited process. The problem (Finding D) is that the
  same shield is *more available to the strong*; the shield itself is not the error, its
  *symmetric* application under an *asymmetric* distribution of power is.

## What changes (the audit has to produce more than confession)

1. **The missing workstream: apex power.** The framework needs a mechanism — or an honest
   admission it has none — for coercive harm by those who *are* the institution
   (executives, officials, heads of organizations). Candidates: fiduciary/duty-of-care
   liability that reaches the top; whistleblower-protected pattern documentation;
   independent (non-prosecutorial) predominant-aggressor review; treating apex-scale
   coercion under governance/anti-corruption regimes rather than domestic-analogue law.
   Logged as **OBJ-027**. Until built, doc 09 must state that the framework reaches
   *subordinate-scale* coercion and is *silent at the apex* — the opposite of its
   cross-scale ambition.
2. **Power-asymmetric safeguards (proposed, NOT adopted — `NEEDS-LIBERTY-REVIEW`).**
   Calibrate the both-victims protections to the *power differential*: the more
   institutional power the accused holds, the higher the bar for the "I'm being
   weaponized" defense (because DARVO + resources make it more available to them), and the
   more weight on documented course-of-conduct. **This is itself liberty-fraught**
   (equal-protection; treating the powerful differently) and may be unworkable — but the
   *symmetric* safeguard under asymmetric power is Finding D, and pretending symmetry is
   neutral is the error. Logged as **OBJ-028**.
3. **Process correction (for how this framework gets built).** Future sessions: when
   Endorphin asserts a claim, the default is a *disconfirming* first move, not a
   confirming search — especially when the prompt is primed ("you'll find…"). Disagreement
   with the *director*, not just with abstract claims, is the test of agreement 1. Propose
   adding this explicitly to CLAUDE.md (see below).
