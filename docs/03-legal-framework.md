# 03 — Legal Framework

**Status: STUB (seeded, undeveloped)**
**Every mechanism below carries `NEEDS-LIBERTY-REVIEW` until `/liberty-review` clears it.**

## Purpose

Propose the new injury category, an identification rubric, and a graduated response
model (punitive ↔ rehabilitative), plus the consent-law consolidation the seed
calls for. This is the workstream where the framework is most dangerous to get
wrong: mechanisms strong enough to reach invisible harm are strong enough to be
weaponized, and the seed's own warning (L107) governs. Nothing here is stable until
it passes doc 07's review.

## Candidate mechanisms (from the seed, all `NEEDS-LIBERTY-REVIEW`)

1. **New injury category + identification rubric** — a named, conduct-based category
   of coercive/psychological harm with an evidentiary rubric (ties to doc 04).
   `[NORMATIVE]`
2. **"Psychological / coercive homicide" theory** — prosecuting abuse-driven suicide
   / self-destruction as a homicide-class offense where sustained coercive
   conditioning with knowledge of likely self-harm can be shown. `[SUPPORTED]`
   (coercive-control→suicidality link) + `[NORMATIVE]` (the charge). Partial
   real-world anchors: Michelle Carter / Conrad Roy (involuntary manslaughter via
   coercion); UK/Commonwealth coercive-control statutes.
3. **Coercive-control offense** — a standalone pattern-of-conduct offense (the
   England & Wales / Scotland / Ireland model), rather than incident-by-incident
   charging. `[SUPPORTED]` as existing law elsewhere; `[NORMATIVE]` for US adoption.
4. **Consent-law consolidation around force / fraud / fear** — replace "scattered
   and messy" consent doctrine with a coherent spine. `[ESTABLISHED]` elements,
   `[NORMATIVE]` consolidation.
5. **Graduated punitive-vs-rehabilitative response** — non-violent parts of the
   spectrum routed toward accommodation/therapeutic pathways (docs 05, 06) rather
   than incarceration. `[NORMATIVE]`
6. **Expedited / specialized case track** — a faster path for qualifying cases —
   flagged by Endorphin himself as "problematic territory" and "a potential
   minefield" (L102). Highest liberty-review priority.

## Open questions

- **Mens rea for psychological homicide.** If the conduct is genuinely
  reactive/improvisational (doc 02), can the intent element for a homicide-class
  charge be met? Or does this force a recklessness/depraved-indifference framing?
- **The neutral term.** The seed wants a stigma-free label; every candidate
  ("psychological," "coercive," "cognitive," L102) carries baggage. Naming is a
  liberty-review problem, not just a branding one.
- **Expedited track vs. due process.** Any "fast-track" for a class of accusations
  collides directly with the both-victims lens. What structural safeguard keeps
  it from becoming a tool of the very abuse it targets?
- **Speech boundary.** Where does "weaponization of words" cross into protected
  speech, and is the conduct/speech line defensible under First Amendment doctrine?
- **Relationship to existing DV/coercive-control law.** Is this a new category or
  an extension of coercive-control statutes already enacted abroad and in some US
  states?

## Research needed

- **Coercive-control statutes and their outcomes:** England & Wales (Serious Crime
  Act 2015 s.76), Scotland (Domestic Abuse (Scotland) Act 2018), Ireland (Domestic
  Violence Act 2018); US states with coercive-control provisions (California,
  Hawaii, Connecticut, and others — verify current list). *Do they work in
  practice? Conviction rates, vagueness challenges, unintended consequences.*
- **Coerced/abuse-linked suicide prosecutions:** Commonwealth v. Michelle Carter;
  Inyoung You case; any UK coercive-control cases involving victim suicide;
  emerging "suicide by coercion" doctrine.
- **Consent-law survey:** current NY consent/coercion statutes (verify the arson /
  "emphatic content" claim from L8); force/fraud/fear framing in trafficking and
  sexual-assault law across jurisdictions.
- **Vagueness doctrine** as applied to coercive-control and stalking statutes.
- **Restorative / graduated-response models** for non-violent offenders that could
  anchor the punitive↔rehabilitative axis.
