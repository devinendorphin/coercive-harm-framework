# 11 — Cross-Cultural & Historical Anchors

## Purpose

The behavioral cluster this framework addresses is not new — most cultures have named
it, and many developed intervention landscapes for it: identification norms,
containment, exile, ritual, and (rarely) reintegration. This workstream builds a
global sampling of (a) the terms, (b) the culturally specific interventions —
especially for incorrigibility and the performance of contrition — and (c) the modern
research record on implementing anything resembling those interventions. These serve
as anchor points for the legal, accommodation, and therapeutic workstreams, and as a
comparative record of how different "landscapes of disincentivizing" have worked
across the ages.

Deep research happens via `/anthropology`. What follows is the verified starter set.

## Part A — Term inventory (starter set)

Format per entry: term · culture/source · definition as documented · the associated

### Kunlangeta (Siberian Yupik, Bering Sea island — likely St. Lawrence I.) [SUPPORTED — single-sourced]
*Verified against Murphy 1976 primary text; see research/anthro-kunlangeta.md for the
full audit, verbatim quotations, and the corrections below.*

Documented by psychiatric anthropologist Jane M. Murphy: an "abstract term for the
breaking of many rules when awareness of the rules is not in question" — glossed
verbatim as "his mind knows what to do but he does not do it." The framework-relevant
core is the **conduct/illness boundary**: Murphy's key find is that kunlangeta (like the
Yoruba *arankan*) was held distinct from *nuthkavihak* ("being crazy" — mind/soul out of
order), which shamans *did* treat. Kunlangeta was **not** classed as illness and **not**
believed curable by healers — the culture drew exactly the conduct-vs-illness line this
framework draws. This distinction is the entry's durable anchor value.

**Corrections applied after primary-source check (do not revert):**
- *Geography.* These are **Siberian Yupik** (kin in Chukotka), not mainland "northwest
  Alaska" (a popularization error from Stout 2005). Murphy names only "an island in the
  Bering Sea"; St. Lawrence Island is the likely site but unconfirmed in this paper.
- *The "pushed off the ice" line is not documented practice.* It is one informant's
  **hypothetical** answer to "what *would have* happened ... traditionally," hedged with
  "*probably*." Only **one** person among the 499-person census was ever called
  kunlangeta (n=1), and much of the Eskimo data runs through a **single key informant**.
  Treat the terminal-exclusion "intervention" as speculative reconstruction, not record.
- *Attestation gap.* No independent Yupik lexicon entry located; the term effectively
  lives through Murphy 1976 + the downstream pop-psychology chain. [RESEARCH NEEDED]
- Source: Murphy, J.M. (1976). "Psychiatric Labeling in Cross-Cultural Perspective."
  Science 191(4231): 1019–1028. doi:10.1126/science.1251213 → research/anthro-kunlangeta.md
- Anchor value (revised): a small-scale society independently drawing (1) a distinct
  named **conduct** category with (2) intact-awareness as its hallmark and (3) explicit
  exclusion from the "treatable/illness" category. The **incorrigibility→terminal-
  exclusion** response is a *negative* anchor (see docs/07), not a precedent — and rests
  on n=1 + one informant's speculation, so claim it modestly.

### Arankan / aranakan (Yoruba, Nigeria) [ESTABLISHED]
From the same Murphy paper (drawing on the Leighton/Lambo Cornell–Aro studies):
"a person who always goes his own way regardless of others, who is uncooperative,
full of malice, and bullheaded." Like kunlangeta, categorized by the Yoruba
themselves as distinct from ordinary madness (were) — and similarly regarded as
not amenable to the healing repertoire.
- Source: Murphy 1976; background in Leighton et al. (1963), Psychiatric Disorder
  Among the Yoruba. → research/murphy-1976.md
- RESEARCH NEEDED: primary Yoruba-language sources on community response; how Ifa
  divination framed such persons; spelling/usage verification beyond Murphy.

### Wolf in sheep's clothing (Hebrew Bible / New Testament tradition) [ESTABLISHED as text]
Matthew 7:15 — "false prophets... in sheep's clothing, but inwardly they are
ravening wolves." The key structural insight: the warning targets *disguised*
predation — performed virtue masking exploitation — and the diagnostic offered is
behavioral-pattern observation over time: "by their fruits ye shall know them"
(7:16–20). This is a conduct-over-claims evidentiary standard, directly relevant
to workstream 04.
- RESEARCH NEEDED: rabbinic and patristic commentary on detection and communal
  response; the related OT strand (e.g., Ezekiel 22:27, Zephaniah 3:3 — wolves as
  predatory elites, i.e., the pattern at institutional scale, matching the seed
  conversation's cross-scale claim).

### Candidate entries requiring verification via /anthropology [HYPOTHESIS until sourced]
- **Herem** (Jewish law): graduated communal exclusion/excommunication — a formal,
  reversible, procedure-bound ostracism. Spinoza's herem as case study.
- **Meidung / shunning** (Amish/Anabaptist): explicitly rehabilitative-intent
  ostracism — exclusion designed to induce genuine (not performed) contrition, with
  a defined reintegration path. Directly on point for "performance of contrition."
- **Skóggangr / full outlawry** (medieval Icelandic/Norse law): legal personhood
  withdrawn after adjudication; the community-scale terminal exclusion, but reached
  through legal process rather than informal action — a due-process contrast to
  the kunlangeta response.
- **Ostrakismos** (classical Athens): ten-year exile by anonymous popular vote, no
  charge or trial required — historically aimed at dangerously powerful/charismatic
  figures. Anchor for the framework's institutional-scale concern AND a cautionary
  case (weaponization by faction).
- **Munafiq / nifaq** (Islamic tradition): the hypocrite whose performed piety
  masks bad faith; extensive theological literature on why this figure is treated
  as more dangerous than the open opponent, and on the limits of judging inner
  states — a civil-liberties-relevant restraint tradition.
- **Xiaoren 小人** (Confucian): the "small person" — self-interested, incapable of
  ren (humaneness) — managed through role design and exclusion from positions of
  trust rather than punishment: an accommodation-model precedent for workstream 05.
- **Icchantika** (Mahayana Buddhism): the being "incapable" of awakening — and the
  major doctrinal dispute over whether such incapacity can exist at all. A rich
  tradition-internal debate about incorrigibility itself.
- **Windigo / wetiko** (Algonquian peoples): the cannibal-spirit condition — greed/
  consumption of others as possession. NOTE WITH CARE: the historical "windigo
  psychosis" literature is heavily contested and entangled with colonial framing;
  also relevant is the documented danger of execution-after-accusation. Handle as
  both anchor and cautionary tale.
- **Witchcraft accusation systems** (multiple cultures): the essential negative
  anchor. Systems for naming hidden malicious harm-doers have repeatedly become
  machinery for persecuting the innocent and the marginal — the historical proof
  of the weaponization-inversion risk that /liberty-review tests for. Every entry
  in this inventory must be read against this failure mode.

## Part B — Intervention landscape taxonomy (to be developed)

Classify each documented intervention along these axes:
1. **Trigger** — informal consensus vs. formal adjudication vs. accusation alone
2. **Reversibility** — permanent (ice, outlawry) vs. conditional (meidung, herem)
3. **Theory of change** — none/containment vs. induced contrition vs. structural
   role-restriction vs. transformation
4. **Contrition test** — how did the culture distinguish performed from genuine
   contrition? (The meidung reintegration process is the key case to study.)
5. **Abuse safeguards** — what, if anything, protected against false accusation
6. **Scale** — interpersonal / village / institutional / polity
7. **Documented failure modes** — weaponization, faction capture, scapegoating

## Part C — Modern implementation research (starter set)

### Mendota Juvenile Treatment Center — decompression model [ESTABLISHED]
The strongest empirical counterpoint to blanket incorrigibility claims. Wisconsin
facility (est. 1995) for youth deemed too aggressive or "treatment refractory" for
ordinary corrections. In the Caldwell et al. 2006 study, youth scoring high on the
Psychopathy Checklist: Youth Version who got treatment-as-usual were more than
twice as likely to violently recidivate within two years as MJTC-treated youth;
follow-up research through 2024 shows violent-arrest rates cut nearly in half
4.5 years post-release. The model explicitly targets the antagonistic-defiance
cycle rather than only skill deficits — i.e., it works on the relational pattern
itself. → research/mendota-decompression.md
- Key citations: Caldwell & Van Rybroek (2001) IJOTCC 45:469–477; Caldwell, Skeem,
  Salekin & Van Rybroek (2006) Criminal Justice and Behavior 33(5); Caldwell &
  Van Rybroek (2005) IJLP 28:622–636; Caldwell et al. (2012) CJB 39:144–155.
- Framework implication: incorrigibility judgments hardened into law (the
  kunlangeta answer) may foreclose change that intensive, correctly designed
  intervention can produce — at least in youth. Adult evidence is much weaker:
  distinguish sharply. [Tag adult-generalization as HYPOTHESIS.]

## Open questions

1. Does any documented culture have a validated method for distinguishing performed
   from genuine contrition — or is time-under-observation the only known test?
2. Small-scale societies could afford terminal exclusion; mass societies cannot.
   Which pre-modern mechanisms scale, and which depended on everyone knowing
   everyone (i.e., on reputation systems modern anonymity destroys)?
3. The two independently documented traditions (Inuit, Yoruba) both excluded this
   pattern from the "healable" category — yet Mendota shows treatment effects in
   youth. Reconcile: is the traditional incorrigibility judgment about adults,
   about available technology, or about the pattern itself?
4. Every naming system studied so far carries a witch-trial shadow. What structural
   features separated the systems that stayed calibrated from those that ran amok?
