Target: $ARGUMENTS (default: every item marked NEEDS-LIBERTY-REVIEW across docs/).

The seed conversation itself set this standard: "explicitly delineating
characteristics could potentially resemble or enable discriminatory practices, which
must be avoided at all costs." This command enforces it.

For each mechanism under review, test against:

1. **Vagueness** — could a person of ordinary intelligence know in advance what
   conduct is prohibited? Coercive-control statutes have faced exactly this
   challenge; check how enacted statutes drew their lines.
2. **Person vs. pattern** — does any language target who someone is (traits,
   suspected neurology, diagnosis) rather than documented conduct over time?
   Automatic fail if yes; propose conduct-based rewording.
3. **Weaponization inversion** — the seed identifies false accusation ("confession
   through accusation") as a core abuser tactic. Could an abuser use THIS mechanism
   against their victim? What structural safeguard prevents it?
4. **Due process floor** — presumption of innocence, right to confront evidence
   (reconciled with victim-protective procedure), proportionality of penalty,
   appeal path.
5. **Speech boundary** — where does the mechanism touch protected expression, and is
   the conduct/speech line defensible under prevailing First Amendment doctrine?
6. **Disparate impact** — given documented enforcement disparities in the existing
   system, how might this mechanism land unevenly across race, class, gender,
   disability? The seed's Flatbush chapter is a reminder that power abuse and
   enforcement bias cross all community lines.

Verdict per mechanism: `CLEARED`, `CLEARED-WITH-AMENDMENTS` (specify), or `BLOCKED`
(specify what would unblock). Record verdicts inline in the doc and summarize in
docs/07-civil-liberties-safeguards.md.
