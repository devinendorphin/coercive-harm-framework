description: Devil's advocate pass — attack a doc the way its smartest critics would
---

Target: $ARGUMENTS (a doc, or "all" for the full framework).

This continues the exercise Endorphin initiated in the 2024 seed conversation
("give me five questions... critics are likely to lob at me").

1. Adopt, in turn, each of these critic personas and generate their 2–3 strongest
   objections to the target — strongest, not strawest:
   - **The defense attorney / civil libertarian**: vagueness doctrine, due process,
     how this statute gets weaponized in custody battles, criminalizing speech.
   - **The empirical psychologist**: where's the validated instrument? Base rates?
     Falsifiability of the mPFC hypothesis? Distinguishing pattern from confirmation bias?
   - **The disability-rights advocate**: framing a behavior pattern as "disability"
     without diagnosis — who benefits, who gets stigmatized, does this pathologize
     the accused or protect them?
   - **The prosecutor / practical implementer**: can this actually be charged and
     proven? Jury comprehension? Resource cost? Prosecutorial discretion risks?
   - **The survivor advocate (friendly fire)**: does any element inadvertently
     burden victims further, e.g., new evidentiary hoops?

2. For each objection: record it in `docs/08-objections-and-responses.md` with a
   unique ID, then either (a) draft the best available response from the framework
   as it stands, (b) mark it `UNRESOLVED — requires framework change`, or
   (c) mark it `CONCEDED — framework amended`, with a pointer to the amendment.

3. Finish with a blunt one-paragraph assessment: if this framework were published
   tomorrow, what is the single objection most likely to sink it?

Do not soften objections to spare the project. The ledger's value is its adversarial
honesty.
