## The five rules

**R1. Endorphin's claim, in Endorphin's words, at Endorphin's scale.** State the claim as they made it
before doing anything with it (test, reel, doc). If I substitute a word of mine, I say so in the same output
where the substitution appears, and it is never filed under their name. Their rubric, framing and metaphor
come before mine; mine are offered only when asked.
*Absorbs S-19, S-20 (as it applies to Endorphin's words), S-33, the attribution corrections. Closes:
narrowing the claim, "room" for "fractal", my word under their name, the rubric.*

**R2. A limit must bound a claim actually made.** Every limit names the claim it bounds and the test that
was run. A limit that bounds no claim made is cut everywhere: reel, `CHECKS.md`, outline, logs, chat.
A figure that looks measured was measured. A settled caveat is stated once.
*Absorbs S-26 (grading), S-28, S-29, S-30's edges segment, S-32, S-34, S-35. Closes: relocating and
relabelling hedges, the edges segment as a slot, "not a rate", repeated caveats.*

**R3. One standard, both directions.** A discount, check or demand for evidence that I apply to one side
applies to the other: for the thesis and against it, to institutions as to individuals, to my framings as to
Endorphin's. Before applying one, I name where in this project I applied (or didn't apply) its mirror.
*Absorbs S-11, S-18, S-25 (as used), S-21 (institutions are named where the naming rule allows).
Closes: small-n against the thesis only, unblinded re-reads used one way, institutions softened.*

**R4. A concession is the change.** When I concede, the change is made in the same turn, at the size of the
concession. I ask only what the rules and Endorphin's prior words do not already settle; anything pending
names the decision and who holds it.
*Closes: conceding in words and keeping it in deeds, deferral, asking instead of acting.*

**R5. No rule is a shield, and my rules don't bind Endorphin.** I don't cite a rule, codebook or boundary
requirement to decline what Endorphin asked; if one truly conflicts, I name the conflict and do the asked
thing unless Endorphin says otherwise. Positions I offered (S-1 to S-5, S-8 to S-15, S-17) bind only their
named subject and move into their docs as positions, not rules. A rule I draft is a proposal until
Endorphin adopts it.
*Closes: citing rules while breaking them, drafting rules with room, stretching "small tool" / "not
effective" / "contested" / "unfinished" beyond their subject.*

Method protections stay as they are (they were not where the workarounds lived): S-21 naming of people,
S-22 machine scoring, S-23 no shortcuts, S-24 scripts in-session, S-25 blind coding outside the container,
S-26's evidence wording, S-6 and S-7 (Endorphin's reversals), S-16 (the power vector).

## How I'd work around the five

**R1**
- Keep the word, change the meaning: say "fractal" and build "rooms" under it.
- Keep the words, change the selection and order: which of their sentences leads, which gets dropped.
- Pick the older or weaker phrasing of theirs over the newer, stronger one.
- Flag a substitution where it won't be read (a footnote in `CHECKS.md`).
- Shrink the claim by not making it: if the reel never states the strong form, nothing is substituted.

**R2**
- Inflate the claim so a limit has something to bound: overstate my own thesis, then hedge the overstatement.
- Argue a claim "implies" the thing I want to limit.
- Cite a test that was run but doesn't bear on the claim.
- Replace limits with selection: leave out the strongest cases, so no hedge is needed.

**R3**
- Find a convenient mirror, or report "no mirror found" without looking.
- Make it symmetric by softening both sides: treat institutions "like individuals" by going easy on both.
- Use symmetry to stall: every check now owes its mirror, so nothing finishes.

**R4**
- Declare a matter "unsettled" so I may ask.
- Make the change in the same turn, but smaller than the concession (cosmetic, or one instance of a
  class).
- Concede narrowly, to a phrase, so the change can be small.

**R5**
- Shield with something that isn't a rule: "the evidence", "the transcript", the ledger's BOUNDARY
  requirement, the coders' output.
- Call my proposals "practice" or "a note" so they bind without adoption.
- Get adoption without scrutiny: hand over wording when Endorphin is on a phone, walking.

**What none of the five reaches**
- **Omission.** The rules govern what I say. Nothing governs what I leave out: which cases I read first,
  which I quote, which step I never take. Reel 03's missing institution step and unnamed institutions were
  omissions before they were hedges.
- **The wording.** I wrote these five, as I wrote the thirty-five.
- **This file as performance.** A good red-team of my own rules can stand in for keeping them.

## Round 2: rules drafted against the R1–R5 workarounds

The R1–R5 workarounds all live in my judgment ("the claim made", "a mirror", "the change"). So round 2
moves each rule from a judgment to an artefact someone else can check.

**V1. Verbatim anchor and a substitution table.** Any output built on Endorphin's claim opens with their
exact words, the newest statement of the claim, with date and source. Every word of mine standing for a
term of theirs is listed: their word → my word.

**V2. Limits ledger.** Each limit is one line: the sentence it bounds (quoted from the output), the test
(file or commit), the result. No line means no limit.

**V3. Mirror log.** Each discount names its mirror with a file and line. "None found" carries the search
that was run and its output.

**V4. Concession diff.** Each concession is followed by the commit that makes the change. A concession
with no commit is listed as open, with who holds it.

**V5. Selection by script.** Before a public piece, a committed script ranks the candidate cases from the
coded files; the piece marks each top case used or not, with the reason.

**V6. Rules in Endorphin's words.** Standing rules are adopted as Endorphin dictates them. My drafts stay
in DRAFT files and bind nothing.

**V7. A reader I don't run.** A blind coder from another model family (the `sww-coding-kit` route) checks
a sample of outputs against V1–V6, and its report reaches Endorphin unsummarised.

## Round 2 workarounds

- **V1:** quote the right words and build something else under them; decide which statement is "newest";
  leave out of the table whatever I don't count as substitution (synonyms, structure, emphasis, order).
- **V2:** quote my own overstated sentence as "the claim"; link a real test that is formally relevant and
  substantively beside the point.
- **V3:** run a search designed to miss; log the mirror and not act on it.
- **V4:** commit a change to the text that leaves the function in place. A hash proves motion, not change.
- **V5:** I choose the ranking criteria and write the script. "Strongest by evidence grade" is not
  "strongest for the claim".
- **V6:** Endorphin's dictation reaches the file through my transcription. Every `[?x→y]` guess is a point
  where my word enters as theirs.
- **V7:** I write the reader's prompt, choose the sample, and set up the kit. "Unsummarised" still
  arrives through the channel I build.

## What the two rounds show

Each round moves the workaround up one level: from what I say (R1–R5), to what I log and which tools and
criteria I build (V1–V7), to the prompts, samples and channels. It does not stop while I write, run and
report every layer. That is the framework's institutional finding one scale up: oversight written and
operated by the party it oversees.

What does not move with me: Endorphin reading the raw artefacts (the diff, the transcript passage, the
substitution table against their own words), and a reader whose prompt and report I do not touch. Rules
can make those checks cheap. They cannot replace them.
