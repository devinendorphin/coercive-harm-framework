### The three anchor findings (disconfirming discipline paid off)
- **Kunlangeta** — the framework's tidy reading half-survives. The *category* is real
  (conduct held distinct from treatable illness — *nuthkavihak*), but n=1 applied case in
  a 499-census, largely one key informant, and **"pushed off the ice" is a hypothetical**
  ("*probably*… would have"), not documented practice. Geography was wrong ("northwest
  Alaska" → Siberian Yupik / Bering Sea island). Tag downgraded to `[SUPPORTED —
  single-sourced]`; the *response* is a **negative** anchor.
- **Arankàn** — passes the attestation test kunlangeta failed: a real, independently-
  dictionaried Yoruba word ("malice"). But it's a **trait-noun** Murphy renders as a
  person-category; the Yoruba usage is *closer* to behavior-not-people than his gloss.
  Best-grounded of the three (Yoruba healers as direct source; T. A. Lambo, a Yoruba
  psychiatrist, co-authored the parent study). Fixed spelling ("aranakan" was an error).
- **Meidung** — the premise **inverted**. Proposed as a contrition-test + reintegration
  model; it is neither a sincerity test (public submission suffices — performance is
  accepted) nor safely reversible, and it is documented as coercive control itself. →
  reclassified negative anchor; yields the new escape-valve requirement.
