# Research: Dissent, Strategic Ignorance, and Lateral Violence

*Compiled 2026-07-09 from Endorphin's dissent/agnotology riff. Tests whether "survival
tactics of the oppressed turn lateral over time" tracks in research. Verdict: it tracks
strongly, with three honest caveats.*

## Part 1 — Strategic/performed ignorance as resistance `[ESTABLISHED]`

- **James C. Scott**, *Weapons of the Weak* (1985) and *Domination and the Arts of
  Resistance: Hidden Transcripts* (1990): "everyday forms of resistance" by the
  dominated — foot-dragging, feigned ignorance, gossip, ambiguity, anonymity. **"Feigning
  ignorance is a potent weapon... provides the resister with plausible deniability."**
  The **public transcript** (what's safe to show power) vs. the **hidden transcript**
  (the offstage critique). This is Endorphin's "claim not to know anything to avoid the
  punishment of the master/slaver/policeman" — canonical, well-grounded.
- **Terminology caveat:** Endorphin says "agnotology." Proctor's *agnotology* (2008) is
  usually **ignorance manufactured *by* the powerful** (tobacco/climate doubt). The
  *subaltern* strategic-ignorance concept is closer to **Scott** and to **Charles Mills'**
  "white ignorance" / epistemologies of ignorance. Both matter to this framework (below).

## Part 2 — The lateral turn: oppression displaced onto peers `[SUPPORTED]` (strong theory, growing empirics)

- **Frantz Fanon**, *The Wretched of the Earth* (1961): colonized populations, **barred
  from confronting the oppressor directly, displace aggression horizontally onto peers**
  within their own group — intra-community violence as displaced anti-colonial rage.
- **Paulo Freire**, *Pedagogy of the Oppressed* (1970): **"horizontal violence"** as a
  byproduct of **internalized oppression** — the oppressed **mimic the dominators'
  tactics against each other** rather than uniting against the systemic cause.
  "Sub-oppressors" act from low self-esteem, powerlessness, and fear of reprisal, lashing
  out at their own group **"because that feels safer and allows them to feel more
  powerful."** — This is almost verbatim Endorphin's claim.
- **Empirical literature:** "lateral/horizontal violence" is studied in Indigenous
  communities (CANZUS scoping reviews; systematic review of Indigenous women's
  experiences), nursing ("nurses eat their young"), and feminist movements. Widely
  recognized as **a product of internalized historical and contemporary oppression.**

## The three honest caveats (disconfirming pass)

1. **Structural, not merely temporal.** Fanon/Freire locate the mechanism in *blocked
   upward confrontation* (can't hit the oppressor → hit sideways), more than in the
   *passage of time*. Endorphin's "once enough time passes / unresolved" is compatible
   (chronicity deepens it) but the literature's load-bearing variable is the *power
   barrier*, not the clock. Frame as structural-with-temporal-aggravation.
2. **Explains ≠ excuses.** Freire is explicit: the "sub-oppressor" still oppresses. The
   peer harmed by lateral violence **is really harmed** — usually also low-power. Using
   "displaced oppression" to minimize that harm silences the peer — the *same* silencing
   move the framework exists to fight. Both-victims applies *within* the oppressed
   stratum.
3. **Not all cluster conduct is displaced oppression.** Some is apex power-seeking, full
   stop. The lateral-violence frame must not launder genuine top-down predation as
   "everyone's just traumatized."

## Why this is a major deepening of the power critique (doc 10)

- **The identical-conduct / opposite-meaning problem.** The *same observable behaviors*
  (deception, strategic ignorance, inversion, aggression) can be: **(a)** apex
  power-seeking; **(b)** resistance aimed *up* at power (Scott — adaptive, legitimate,
  **not harm**); or **(c)** a survival tactic turned *sideways* onto a peer (Fanon/Freire
  — harm, but displaced). A purely **conduct-based** framework (behavior-not-people)
  **cannot distinguish these** without the missing dimension: **the power vector — who
  holds power over whom, and which way the conduct is aimed** (up / sideways / down).
- **"Enforcement bites the wounded."** Finding B (doc 10) said the framework enforces
  down-scale. This sharpens it: the down/sideways conduct it would catch is
  disproportionately **displaced lateral violence — the scar tissue of unaddressed
  oppression** — while the apex actor and the structural cause go untouched. A framework
  that disciplines the oppressed's displaced rage and cannot reach the original
  domination **functions as a tool of the oppressor.** This is the deepest form of the
  sycophancy-to-power failure.
- **The two agnotologies mirror the power vector:** the powerful *manufacture* ignorance
  (Proctor — climate/tobacco = institutional gaslighting at scale, kin to the
  fascism-as-coercion-at-scale thread); the oppressed *perform* ignorance to survive
  (Scott). Same surface ("we don't know"), opposite power vector.

## Constructive fix logged to the docs

Add an explicit **power-vector dimension** (up / lateral / down) to the taxonomy (doc 02)
and the offense's context analysis:
- **Up (resistance):** never the offense — extend the §4 protective-response exclusion to
  cover resistance to domination.
- **Lateral (displaced):** real harm to the peer, but the response should be
  restorative + address the upstream structural cause (doc 06 + structural), not purely
  punitive.
- **Down (subordinate), incl. apex:** the core coercive harm — and the apex version is
  exactly what the framework can't reach (OBJ-027).
