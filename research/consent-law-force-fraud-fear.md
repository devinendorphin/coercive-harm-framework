## What NY law actually says

- **Coercion (Penal Law Article 135):** NY grades coercion by degree.
  - **First degree (§135.65):** felony (class D, up to 7 yrs). One route
    (§135.65(1)) is committing coercion **by instilling a fear that the actor will
    cause physical injury to a person OR cause damage to property.** — This
    "damage to property" element is almost certainly the "arson" Endorphin
    half-remembered: NY coercion is anchored in **fear of physical injury or
    property damage**, i.e., threat-of-harm, not fraud or psychological
    manipulation. [ESTABLISHED]
  - Coercion generally = compelling someone to do what they have a legal right not
    to do (or abstain from what they may lawfully do) by instilling fear of
    enumerated harms. [ESTABLISHED]
- **Upshot:** NY coercion is built on **fear/force** (threat of injury or property
  damage). **Fraud** and **psychological/coercive manipulation absent a threat of
  physical/property harm** are largely *outside* it. Endorphin's core point — that
  the doctrine doesn't cleanly capture "force, fraud OR fear" as a unified test —
  is **substantially correct**, even though his specific recollection was garbled.
  [SUPPORTED]

## Force / fraud / fear as an organizing test

- The triad is **established** in adjacent bodies of law — notably federal
  **trafficking** law (force, fraud, or coercion) and various sexual-offense
  statutes. As *organizing elements*, they are well-precedented. [ESTABLISHED]
- What the seed proposes is **consolidation**: making force/fraud/fear a coherent
  spine *across* consent contexts (sexual, financial, coercive-control) rather than
  scattered per-offense definitions. That consolidation is a **NORMATIVE / policy
  design** move, not a description of current law. [NORMATIVE]
