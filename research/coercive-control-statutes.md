# Research: Coercive-Control Statutes (current state of law)

*Compiled 2026-07-09 for `/develop 03`. Verify links before publication; law moves.*

## Bottom line up front

1. **The offense is definitionally domestic everywhere it exists.** Every enacted
   criminal coercive-control statute requires the parties be "personally connected"
   — intimate partners, former partners who cohabit, or family members. None reaches
   workplace, institutional, or governmental relationships. **This is the single
   most important finding for our cross-scale question.** [ESTABLISHED]
   - **Topology sub-finding:** the gate is not only scale-limited but *dyad*-limited.
     Under the **Domestic Abuse Act 2021 §2**, "personally connected" is a closed
     list — intimate partners, ex-partners, family members, co-parents of a child;
     no cohabitation required, but **metamours** (partners of a shared partner, not
     themselves partnered/related) are **excluded**. Networked/multi-partner
     household abuse — the seed's founding L40 case — is outside the offense.
     [ESTABLISHED — confirmed against DA Act 2021 §2] (→ OBJ-008, doc 03 §1a)
2. **US criminalization is nearly nonexistent.** Hawaii is the *only* US state that
   has made coercive control a standalone *crime* (a **petty misdemeanor**, enacted
   2021 as a 5-year pilot). Every other US state that addresses it does so
   **civilly** — expanded protective-order definitions and child-custody "best
   interest" factors — not criminally. [ESTABLISHED]
3. **The flagship criminal model has a severe enforcement gap.** England & Wales'
   s.76 offense charged only **3.4% of 41,626 recorded CCB cases** in the year to
   March 2022. The binding constraint is proving the statutory "serious effect" on
   the victim. [ESTABLISHED]

## England & Wales — Serious Crime Act 2015, s.76

- In force **29 Dec 2015**. Created "controlling or coercive behaviour in an intimate
  or family relationship." Aim: "close a gap in the law around patterns of coercive
  and controlling behaviour" — i.e., a *course of conduct* offense, not
  incident-by-incident. [ESTABLISHED]
- **Relationship gate:** applies only to intimate partners, former partners who live
  together, or family members ("personally connected"). [ESTABLISHED]
- **"Serious effect" element:** prosecution must prove the behavior had a serious
  effect on the victim (fear of violence on ≥2 occasions, or serious alarm/distress
  with substantial adverse effect on day-to-day activities). This evidentiary
  requirement is repeatedly identified as the main barrier to conviction. [ESTABLISHED]
- **Outcomes:** low charge/conviction rates; year to March 2022 — 3.4% of recorded
  cases charged. Out-of-court disposals near 0% (treated as serious). Home Office
  *Review of the Controlling or Coercive Behaviour Offence* (March 2021) is the
  primary evaluation. Updated Statutory Guidance April 2023. [ESTABLISHED]
- Sources: legislation.gov.uk (s.76); gov.uk Home Office review (2021); CPS
  prosecution guidance; penalties.uk sentencing data.

## United States

- **Hawaii (2021):** only US *criminal* standalone coercive-control offense; **petty
  misdemeanor**; 5-year pilot. Enumerates isolation, economic abuse, monitoring,
  degradation/verbal abuse, threats (harm, releasing private info), property damage,
  forcing criminal activity/child abuse. [ESTABLISHED]
- **Connecticut — "Jennifer's Law" (2021):** *civil*. Expands the definition of
  domestic violence for civil protective orders to include coercive control; guides
  "best interest of the child" custody analysis. [ESTABLISHED]
- **California:** *civil/family*. "Disturbing the peace of the other party" (Family
  Code) construed to include coercive control; rebuttable presumption against
  custody for a coercive-control perpetrator. [ESTABLISHED]
- **General pattern:** most state definitions gate to "household members" / "family
  members" / "personally connected." Movement in 2025 (e.g., South Carolina debate —
  Marshall Project, June 2025) is still within the domestic-abuse frame. [SUPPORTED]
- Reference resources: BWJP 2024 Coercive Control Statutory Matrix; ABA Family Law
  Quarterly, "Redefining Domestic Abuse: Coercive Control" (Dec 2025); Marshall
  Project (June 2025).

## Implications for doc 03

- A **standalone criminal coercive-control offense** is a real, enacted model — but
  (a) it is domestic-only, (b) US criminalization is a single petty-misdemeanor
  pilot, and (c) the flagship UK version barely convicts. Our doc must not write as
  if criminalization is the obvious, proven lever.
- The **civil** route (protective orders, custody factors) is where US law actually
  operates. This is a live alternative to the criminal framing and lowers several
  liberty-review risks (lower stakes, existing due-process scaffolding).
- The **"serious effect on victim"** design is the enacted answer to "how do you
  prove invisible injury" — it centers *impact on the victim*, matching the seed's
  L113 instinct ("impacts on victims rather than motivations of perpetrators"). It
  also happens to be the conviction bottleneck. That tension is real and should be
  named, not smoothed.
