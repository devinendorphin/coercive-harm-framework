*Added 2026-11-01 after Endorphin's correction: "the data is the thing that is the outside
world… you don't have prevalence data… well here's the data." The correction is right. The
expert and Data Points episodes read population figures on air; they are the base rates the
corpus test could not supply, and they belong in the answer to "do institutions fail
victims?"*

pairs a number with an institution word was extracted (119 passages) and read. The ones about
how institutions respond are below. Where a figure was checked this session, the source is
given; otherwise it is marked "as read on air".*

## What this does to the institutions result

**The pre-registered verdict does not change**: in the podcast's own scenes, when an
institution is shown responding, the response is appropriate about 6 times in 10. That is
what the test measured, and the rule forbids rewriting it after the fact.

**The conclusion drawn from it does change.** The outside data measure the step before and
around those scenes:

1. **Most harm never reaches an institution.** About 69 % of sexual assaults and 71 % of
   stalking go unreported to police.
2. **Most of what does reach one drops out before an outcome.** Of sexual assaults reported
   to police, roughly 1 in 6 leads to an arrest and fewer than 1 in 10 to a felony
   conviction (RAINN; independent studies agree on the shape). Title IX shows the same funnel
   (expert, unverified).
3. **So "institutions mostly respond appropriately" is true only of the responses the
   podcast shows.** Across the population, the commonest institutional outcome is no outcome.
   The podcast's scenes over-represent the cases that got far enough to have one (court
   dates, sentencings, update episodes).

**What the outside data support, and what they do not:**
- They support the **inaction** part of the claim strongly, at population scale.
- They do **not** show *why* cases drop out. Attrition includes thin evidence, victims'
  own choices not to proceed, and lawful acquittals, as well as disbelief and neglect. So
  they do not establish the **disbelief, blame-reversal or pathologizing** parts. Those
  remain present-and-nameable in the corpus, not measured as rates.
- The false-report range (2–10 %) is the base rate doc 07 needs: it is low, and it is not
  zero (S18, S21a, S22 are instances).

**Restated finding, in plain terms:** most harm never gets to an institution, and most of
what does falls out before anything happens. When the podcast shows an institution actually
acting, the action is usually reasonable. Whether the falling-out is mostly disbelief and
blame, or mostly the limits of proof and victims' own choices, neither the corpus nor these
figures can say.
