## 3. How the counting itself hides the harm (Endorphin's statement of purpose)

The episodes say this about their own data, in the researchers' or hosts' words:

- **Stalking disappears into homicide.** Stalking triples homicide risk, but "when there is a homicide,
  the perpetrator almost never receives an additional stalking charge", so the stalking behind a
  killing is not counted as stalking.
- **False-report rates are inflated by police practice.** NSVRC: false-reporting data inflates because
  of law enforcement's failure to follow guidelines and "weak understanding of sexual assault". The
  IACP and FBI say a report is false only if evidence shows no crime occurred; insufficient evidence,
  delay, non-cooperation and inconsistency are not grounds.
- **Child-abuse counts depend on study design:** adult-to-child only vs peer abuse included; children
  interviewed vs adults looking back; contact abuse only vs non-contact too.
- **Hostility suppresses reporting:** for LGBTQ+ people, "all-encompassing statistics of victimhood are
  impossible to fully obtain because of this lack of safe reporting and safe recipients".
- **Old data used to deny abuse in court:** a defunct British study on "overdiagnosis" of medical child
  abuse still cited in 2024 by a Pennsylvania city official; experts say it is under-diagnosed.
- **What isn't diagnosed isn't counted:** "because of a lack of research, victim reporting, and/or
  perpetrator diagnosis, it's impossible to know how many people have been victims of narcissistic abuse."
- **Mislabelling:** Black youth "oftentimes assessed as having a behavioral problem rather than a mental
  health concern"; Black men's emotions misread as psychosis.
- **Diagnostic thresholds:** symptoms below the PTSD criteria "should never be minimized".

**Use:** these are institutions describing their own blind spots, which is direct evidence for the
project's claim that data collection can render harm invisible, and material for reel arc 6.

## 4. Both victims: the falsely accused, and the false-claim pretext

- False sexual-assault reports estimated at **2–8 %** (LAPD 2008: 4.5 %; the eight-community MAD study:
  7 %, 140 of 2,059); possibly an overestimate (NSVRC, above).
- The falsely accused suffer real harm (a 2021 study: loss of self, reputation, PTSD and depression);
  Brian Banks's exoneration is the episode's case.
- 47 % of US exonerations (National Registry of Exonerations, 2017) are of Black defendants.
- **A structural parallel the episodes make without naming it:** a small rate of false claims is used to
  justify suspicion of everyone who comes forward (false reports, 2–8 %, "leaves some responders highly
  critical when actual survivors come forward"), as rare voter fraud ("extremely rare", Brennan Center)
  is used to justify restricting access to voting. *Observation, not tested; a candidate laundering
  move for the ledger ("the false-claim pretext"); filed as veriticide ledger Pattern Registry Entry 19.*

## 5. At scale: the episodes' own bridge from the person to the polity

- **"A cult of one, a cult of few, a cult of many."** The narcissistic-abuse episode (citing Robert J.
  Lifton's three marks of a cult leader) says the same mechanisms run in a relationship, a family or
  team, and "many political scenarios where millions of people can be coercively controlled": love
  bombing, gaslighting, isolation, devaluation, a closed system of logic and "an authoritarian
  structure that doesn't permit feedback". *A guest's thesis, stated on the show; it matches standing
  note S-6, and it is not evidence for it.*
- **The voting episodes** (S21 E17, rebroadcast in S26) tell the history of the state and its deputies
  using violence against people seeking a civic institution: the 1964 murders of Chaney, Schwerner and
  Goodman with the aid of a deputy sheriff (seven convicted, none of murder); Bloody Sunday at Selma;
  Susan B. Anthony arrested for voting; 34 restrictive voting laws in 19 states in 2021. The episode
  frames voting as a survivors' issue because elected officials set the laws and fund victim services
  (VOCA stood to lose over $700 million in 2024).
- **VAWA:** reported IPV against women fell 53 % from 1993 to 2008 (54 % against men); intimate-partner
  homicides of women fell 26 %, of men 36 %; the hotline has taken over 7 million calls. *These are
  declines that followed the law, not proof the law caused them.*

## 6. The harm itself

- PTSD after sexual assault **49 %**; physical assault 32 %; serious injury 16.8 %; natural disaster
  3.8 % (PTSD Alliance); lifetime PTSD about 6 % (men 5–6 %, women 10–12 %).
- 37 % of stalking victims meet PTSD criteria (University of Kent).
- Lifetime cost per child of non-fatal abuse about **$831,000** (CDC); $428 billion a year in the US.
- Munchausen by proxy: about a **9 %** death rate; perpetrators "understand right from wrong"; treatment
  rarely possible (APSAC conditions: full admission, empathy for the child, new strategies, a long
  monitored period). *Bears on doc 06: an imposed treatment path fails where admission never comes.*

## Figures to treat with care

- "The CDC states that one billion children … experience violence each year … considering only the cases
  that are reported": the one-billion estimate comes from population surveys (WHO-cited), not reported
  cases; the episode's framing is probably wrong.
- "79 % of abuse inflicted by a female abuser will remain unreported indefinitely": no source given.
- "Seven times to leave": widely repeated, weakly sourced.
- Regional child-violence percentages (64 % Asia … 12 % Europe): as read; not traced.

# The long expert episodes (read in full, 2026-11-12)

## S19 E12 — Jennifer Khalifa (CPEDV), part 1: "the absence of trauma is choice"

Her own story (already coded by the S19 coders as "Jennifer (first boyfriend)"); read here for what it
adds to the project's questions.

- **Opportunity is manufactured, not only given.** He asked her to help with his college applications,
  got into her school, moved into her apartment complex, dropped his classes and enrolled in hers,
  later had her renting a room in his family's house while she helped support them. Endorphin's point
  that the moves track opportunity needs this refinement: *perpetrators build access*; the test should
  code access as it stands *and* the moves that create it (enmeshment), or it will treat built access
  as background.
- **Institutions, in order:** a church leader told her he knew "there were multiple women who had had
  experiences like this with him", and wished they would come forward (knowledge without action); the
  campus counselor's advice was "it is okay to end a friendship. And that was it"; the school's honor
  code (curfew; no going down a hallway) meant "if I had come forward, there was a chance I could have
  been expelled. They told me that." *An institution's own rule converted the assault into the
  victim's violation: the blame-reversal move, built into a code of conduct.*
- **Why she did not report** after the rape: her brother was in prison ("if I call the cops … he's going
  to be arrested. This is going to crush him"), shame and guilt under purity teaching. Coded reasons
  `protecting_others` and the institution's moral framing.
- **Her frame:** "the opposite of trauma is not the absence of trauma … the absence of trauma is choice";
  a childhood without choice "teed up" the adult relationship. *Her account, not a population claim.*

## S19 E13 — Jennifer Khalifa, part 2: "prevention is synonymous with healing"

**The institutions, in her words:**
- **Church.** On reporting: she "would be facing excommunication, basically reprimanded for my part in
  this", while for him "everything was just swept under the rug": he was given a calling "to work with
  the young men ages 14 to 16 … he needs a calling and he doesn't need to be reprimanded because that
  would make him feel further from the church." *The victim tried, the perpetrator placed with minors:
  blame reversal and self-protection in one decision.* Later she heard that on his mission he had been
  "going into rooms with 16 and 17 year old girls and paying his mission companion to stay outside":
  "a long history of abuses and nothing being done about it."
- **Police interview:** "why did you invite him over?"; "they're also questioning the validity of it and
  your role in it." A covert call nine months later: he denied it; the investigator: "that man knew
  exactly what to say to cover his ass. I can't arrest him", but went to his house; result: "this will
  be on record and on file for him forever." *The untested record (S19 divergence 12) comes from here.*
- **University (public health):** professors told her "this isn't a public health issue. This is a
  criminal justice issue." *A discipline defining a harm out of its remit, which is one way data
  collection never starts.*
- **Second abuser (her husband):** explosive anger excused by a medical condition; financial control
  (draining the account), then physical assault while she held their newborn. She called the police she
  had trained; her **employer (a domestic violence agency) protected her**: "there are so many of us who
  are survivors … We got you." Restraining order, supervised visits; even with insider knowledge,
  "navigating these systems was not easy."
- **A system punishing the victim:** Survived and Punished's case of a mother charged with "failure to
  protect" after her partner murdered their son when he found her packed bag.

**Opportunity and silencing:** her status as a trainer of police and judges kept her silent ("who am I
going to tell? Because I felt like the biggest fraud"). *A role can silence a victim as well as shield a
perpetrator.*

**Figures (as read):** 1 in 4 women and 1 in 7 men experience domestic violence (about 1 in 4 men with
other forms included); "only 6 % of cases ever make it to a courtroom … only 2 % … tried and charged"
(see `EXTERNAL_DATA.md`); California faced an almost $200 million VOCA shortfall, with legal-advocacy
grants (the ones that obtain restraining orders for stalking victims) already cut; California's Healthy
Youth Act (2016) added healthy-relationship teaching in 2018.

**Her frames:** "what happened to you" instead of "what is it that you're doing" (a disruptive
four-year-old "historically, we've punished"); "the communities and the individual who has been
impacted is the holder of the answer." She also counts infidelity and stalking without contact as
domestic violence. *A broad, field-practice definition; the framework's both-victims lens would not
carry it into any legal mechanism.*

## S25 E4 — Dr. Nicole Bedera, *On the Wrong Side* (Title IX), a year of fieldwork inside one office

*Findings from a year of fieldwork inside "Western University's" Title IX office (observation; interviews
with survivors, administrators and perpetrators), published as* On the Wrong Side. `[SUPPORTED]`*: expert
field research, consistent with the institutional-betrayal literature (Freyd) the framework already cites;
the Michigan figures come from that university's own public report.*

**Moves this project already names, found by an institutional researcher:**
- **"Fragmenting violence"** (her concept; filed in the veriticide ledger as Pattern Registry Entry 18, a named sub-form of Protocol move 3, Fragmentation): when an office received several concerns about one
  perpetrator, from one victim or many, it "would separate them all off individually and say that any
  evidence given to one case probably couldn't be used in another case … into these little tiny, easily
  dismissable pieces, which led to these huge benefits for some of the most prolific perpetrators." *The
  institutional form of the shortcut this project's self-audit banned (judging three-sentence pieces), and
  the reason the whole-story test found what the segment test missed.*
- **"Symbolic compliance"** (Lauren Edelman): effort spent convincing regulators and the public that the
  school complies with civil-rights law, "but that's all it ever was, was symbols." Her example: rape
  whistles then, consent trainings now, with "no one at the school who has been trained in how to really
  help you" when someone reports.
- **"Orchestrating complexity"** (Jackie Cruz): complexity created "where there is none to justify their
  inaction." *Compare the referral loop (S23) in the self-audit.*
- **The maze:** a "report" and a "complaint" use near-identical forms, but only a complaint can lead to
  action; even she needed six months to find the right form. **That one hurdle knocked out 80 % of
  reports**; about 20 % pass. At the University of Michigan one year, four cases ended in any sanction,
  none an expulsion (its own public report). "It turns reporting … into a war of attrition."
- **Informal resolution:** voluntary for both parties (the perpetrator can quit at any time) and "can't be
  punitive"; at her site a library schedule keeping a stalking perpetrator away from his victim was ruled
  "too punitive". "We don't have an effective educational remedy to change perpetrators' behavior."
- **Cross-examination and the absent perpetrator:** under the 2020 rules, if a party does not attend the
  hearing, all their statements are excluded, including a perpetrator's confession (and "perpetrators
  confess all the time … in most cases", from her interviews), so perpetrators gain by staying away and victims
  must attend. Perpetrators she interviewed "enjoyed the hearing process … it allowed them to exert
  dominance."
- **Retaliatory cross-complaints:** a perpetrator files a counter-complaint "to try to confuse
  investigators into thinking that they don't know who the true victim is"; the school still sides with
  the man. *The system as a weapon, which the both-victims lens (OBJ-001 family) anticipates.*

**On power (bears on the refuted power-position test, P1/P2):** schools "use gender as a proxy for
power"; they protect men generally, not only star athletes or donors' children: survivors who were
themselves donors' or famous professors' children were not protected either. *This fits the corpus
result that perpetrator power position did not predict impunity: the protection attaches to a broad
category, not to the apex.*

**Who works there, and what they believe:** schools hired administrators who believed rape myths in
preference to experts; "himpathy" (Kate Manne): "one life [is] already ruined, why ruin two"; "we all
think rape is wrong until we know the perpetrator." Safe havens existed (victim advocates, some faculty)
but "none of those people worked in Title IX."

**Where the trauma comes from:** "a lot of that enduring trauma … comes from institutional betrayal …
going to ask for help and being told no"; with support, survivors "can recover very quickly." `[SUPPORTED]` *(institutional-betrayal
literature).*

**How the counting was controlled (Endorphin's purpose, directly):**
- Her own sample: the university's offices were supposed to pass every student case to her study, but
  "they were not recruiting for cases involving faculty", the most common type on campus by other
  research: "a glaring omission from the book." *The institution being studied shaped what could be
  counted.*
- Access: told she would never be allowed to study her own university; she got in elsewhere only because
  the Title IX coordinator was newly hired from government, then was quietly dropped from meetings.

**Settings that make opportunity (Endorphin's refinement):** unsafe spaces are "male-dominated,
competitive, and hierarchical", including ones with many women but men at the top (theater, music,
marching band). One theater department: "women were a dime a dozen, but men were diamonds in the rough";
a professor known for harassment submitted character statements for a star male student facing several
complaints.

**Comparisons and remedies:** "the Title IX system produces more institutional betrayal and fewer
accountability actions than our criminal justice system"; the fix she proposes is an independent body
without the school's conflicts of interest; California made campus victim advocates state employees
(staffing ratios, independence to help survivors sue their schools). On the 2020 regulation: when commenters asked
whether the cross-examination rule was meant to work this way, "the Trump administration doubled down and
said, no, that's exactly how we mean it to function"; Holland's co-authored analysis of the regulation's
preamble (next section) found its rationale rests on treating complaints as not credible.

## S25 E7 — Dr. Katherine Holland, mandatory reporting and Title IX processes

- **"A mandatory reporting policy is only as effective as the system that you were reporting into."** The
  Title IX office is "not a victim advocacy office … They are there to ensure that the institution is
  compliant"; survivors "frequently describe it as more traumatic than the assault." `[SUPPORTED]`
  *(her published survivor studies).*
- **The override:** a "university-driven complaint" can go ahead without the survivor, "taking a wrecking
  ball to that person's life": investigators interview the friends, colleagues and housemates who are
  her whole support system. *Standing note S-20 ("the override is the harm") in an institutional setting.*
- **Delay that outlasts the harm's victim:** cases "drawn out over years until the perpetrator just
  graduated or left on their own." *Endorphin's thesis that delay is used to keep extracting, in the
  researcher's own description.*
- **Outcomes:** findings of responsibility are rare, removals rarer; one survivor's assailant, found
  responsible, was suspended for the rest of the semester because "the assault could have been worse."
  No practitioner she has spoken to thinks the 2020 hearing process "work[s] well for anyone."
- **Mutual no-contact orders as a weapon:** the order binds both parties; perpetrators claim the survivor
  broke it, while survivors' reports of violations rarely lead to anything. *The both-victims mechanism,
  run in reverse against the victim.*
- **Retaliation unrecognised:** offices look for formal acts ("were you denied a promotion?"), not the
  forms retaliation actually takes; survivors drop classes, change majors, leave.
- **Power without stated threats:** an advisor who controls funding, training and the only specialty in
  the department holds coercive power "even if there are not those explicit coercive threats";
  investigators "looking for … evidence of coercion" miss it. *Opportunity built into the role.*
- **Star value:** a faculty member who brings in large grants, or a beloved coach, gets "the benefit of the
  doubt": a "cost-benefit analysis". Nassar "had been investigated by Title IX. People had already
  reported. It did not solve that problem."
- **Symbolic compliance again:** a training module sold to universities is called "report equal support!"
- **A comparison study she cites:** supportive measures in Title IX policies mostly *reduce* access to
  education (easier withdrawal); those for sports concussions keep students in school (remote work, a slow
  return).
- **Bias in the room:** an LGBTQ+ student told by an investigator, "I'm not going to hold your lifestyle
  preferences against you."

## What the four long episodes add, together

1. **The institutional names for what this project found.** Fragmenting violence (Bedera) is the segment
   shortcut; symbolic compliance and "report equals support" are the advertised safety the whole-story
   test measured against outcomes; orchestrated complexity and the report/complaint maze are the referral
   loop; delay until the perpetrator graduates is extraction by delay.
2. **Opportunity is built into roles and settings and also manufactured.** Hierarchical, competitive,
   male-topped settings (Bedera); advisors and stars whose value buys the benefit of the doubt (Holland);
   a perpetrator who moves into the victim's building, classes and job (Khalifa).
3. **Protection follows a broad category, not the apex.** Bedera's finding that schools protect men as a
   class, not only powerful men, is one explanation for the corpus result that perpetrator power did not
   predict impunity.
4. **The counting is controlled by the counted.** The university decided which cases reached Bedera's
   study and quietly closed its meetings to her; a public-health faculty defined the harm out of its field
   (Khalifa). This is Endorphin's point about data collection rendering harm invisible, documented by the
   researchers who ran into it.
5. **Rules that turn the victim into the violator.** An honor code (Khalifa), a church court (Khalifa),
   mutual no-contact orders and retaliatory cross-complaints (Holland, Bedera), a "failure to protect"
   charge (Survived and Punished).
