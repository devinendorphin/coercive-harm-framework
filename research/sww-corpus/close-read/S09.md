## Target responses (T-fields; recorded, not tests)

- **Danielle stayed because she foresaw the separation danger.** "If I left… he would make
  my life a living hell… use Carrie… or show up at my parents' with a gun." She fantasized
  about his accidental death "and then being free," and told her therapist she would wait
  for "a breaking point." She left only with private security, the rifles removed, new
  devices, and a pre-emptive call to her boss.
- **Kenji documented**: car GPS, iCloud, the watch, a 75-page timeline, a private
  investigator, the crime-watch organiser's police contacts. He hesitated to warn Danielle
  for months for fear of what Arty would do.
- **Darcy, both target and co-actor.** She fed information to Arty, put a car in her name for
  him, wrestled Kenji for his keys, and repeated Arty's lines about Danielle in an online
  group. Danielle and Kenji call her "both a victim and an abuser." Recorded as their reading.
  Her own words: "**you don't know what it's like to be controlled by another person**."
- **The children's response.** Kenji's son shut his laptop on his first day of school during
  a fight. At 8 he calls himself his mother's "bodyguard."

## The documentation burden in this season

- **Records turned against the people who made them.** Danielle's notes were read as
  rehearsal and cost her credibility. Kenji's organised evidence was read as edited. The
  perpetrator's counter-filed restraining order turned his victim into an unreliable witness.
- **The perpetrator manufactures records.** Police reports, a dashcam claim, fabricated
  texts, a template cease-and-desist and subpoena, a U-visa victim narrative. The system
  weighs record against record without screening for who started it (compare DARVO,
  `../../taxonomy-external-grounding.md`).
- **Danielle's legal costs** went on for two years before custody was settled: "you just
  get screwed if you can't pay for a lawyer."

## Divergences (recorded, not smoothed)

1. **The widest documented victim set of one perpetrator in the corpus** (≥10 women over
   about 15 years), with direct testimony of **escalation** (Stacey: controlling but not
   violent c. 2008; Darcy: choking, threats to shoot, 2020).
2. **Weaponization of protections built for victims** (U-visa, restraining orders, police
   reports, dual arrest) is systematic here, not incidental.
3. **A male target**: Kenji is targeted through his ex-wife, through his job and through
   restraining orders. The host stresses that men can be victims.
4. **A target who also acts for the perpetrator** (Darcy). This is not new (compare S3's Ted
   and S5c's mother), but here two co-survivors assert it.
5. **The judge's reasoning as a counter-instance to the literature**: an institution
   applying the "why didn't she leave" heuristic the clinical research refutes.

## What this season adds

- **The strongest corpus support for the operator's specification of T2**, together with S8:
  tailored personas, explicit walls against comparing notes, and a collapse brought about by
  cross-victim comparison. Comparing notes was then **used deliberately as the intervention
  method** for later targets.
- **Your separation thesis, shown from both sides.** The survivor stayed because she correctly
  foresaw the risk; the court penalised her for staying. The host read the Hotline and NCADV
  statement on air: "leaving is often the most dangerous period."
- **Evidence for low variance in perpetrator behaviour across ≥10 targets of a single
  perpetrator**: the same devices (name tattoos, cars in her name, attacks on jobs, abduction
  threats, legal counter-filings) recur over a decade.
