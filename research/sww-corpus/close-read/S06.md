## The season, honestly characterized

This season is **not** a set of relationship-coercion narratives. It has two
person-to-person deception cases (Kaelyn, Jez), one predatory lure (the host and her
husband), one hate crime followed by years of targeted stalking (J.E.), and a panel
discussion on trolling. That matters for the method. It means **the C1–C6 cycle should be
absent where no relationship exists**, and that is what this season shows. The cycle did
not get applied to everything. A codebook that found "love bombing" in a stranger's
threatening phone calls would be measuring the coder, not the corpus. Here the cycle
appears only where there is a sustained relationship (Kaelyn). What recurs across *all*
the cases is a smaller set of **devices**.

## The documentation burden in this season

- **J.E.'s case was made by the victims' own record.** The mother installed call-recording
  software "to build a cache of evidence that was forensically irrefutable." That produced
  **238 recorded calls** in discovery, "a fraction" of the total. Phase one (2019), without
  recordings, went nowhere. Phase two, with recordings, produced a conviction. This is the
  cleanest before-and-after instance in the corpus of the claim in
  `../../documentation-labor-burden.md`. The family was carrying the labor while the stalking
  was still happening: a parent with Parkinson's, a target who was having panic attacks
  daily and later had a suspected heart failure.
- **Legal process as a second isolation.** During discovery J.E. could not write, post or
  email about the case: "it paralyzed me and isolated me." The process that vindicated them
  also silenced them for months.
- **Retraumatization by the record.** At sentencing the prosecution played a 5–8 minute
  montage of the threats. The mother's impact statement quoted them "with the exact same
  cadence," "in my mother's voice."

## Divergences (recorded, not smoothed)

1. **This season tests scope, and the codebook passes.** The cycle is absent in the
   stranger, lure and hate-crime cases. Recurring *devices* (fabricated personas, staged
   vulnerability, threats routed through family, victim-blaming, erasure) cross the
   relationship boundary. The cycle does not.
2. **J.E.'s own correction of their 2020 hypothesis.** They assumed a white supremacist and
   declined to speculate in public. The caller turned out to be a Black woman with untreated
   mental illness. J.E. reads this as "white supremacy working… creating infighting among
   minorities." J.E. supported her psychiatric assessment and asked that she be imprisoned
   near her family: "I don't hate her, but I can't forgive her… she stalked me." This was
   the survivor running a disconfirming test on their own hypothesis. It is recorded here as
   an instance of the discipline this repo asks of the analyst.
3. **Erasure as the final move.** The caller's courtroom apology named the mother and
   stepfather and left out J.E., the target of 80–90% of the calls. The press then erased
   J.E. again. Compare S5b, where Kelly's departure was erased ("we do not talk about it").
4. **Female perpetrators** in both deception cases that were solved (Kristen; J.E.'s
   caller). Combined with S2 and S3, the perpetrator's gender is not constant in this
   corpus.

## What this season adds

- **Specificity evidence for the codebook.** The cycle does not appear where it should
  not, which is evidence against the worry that "love bombing → devaluation → discard" is
  vocabulary laid over any harm.
- **The documentation burden, demonstrated.** One case went from unprovable to convicted
  because the victims' family built the record themselves, under threat.
- **The macro end of the fractal.** A national-scale hate crime turned into one stranger's
  four-year campaign against one family, using the private name, threats routed through a
  relative, and erasure. These are the same devices seen in intimate cases, carried by a
  different motive.
