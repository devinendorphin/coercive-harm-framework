# Season 14 close-read: one alleged serial perpetrator, many women (serialized, 12 episodes + 2 updates)

*Read in full and in order: twelve episodes plus the two "What Came Next" update episodes (about
146k words). Coded under amendments 1, 1a and 2, including the operator's 2c ruling after S13.*

**The show used the man's real name.** It said that this was deliberate and rested on the volume
of corroboration. This file calls him **"Jake"**, the first name used on air, and leaves out his
surname. Almost everything alleged against him has not been adjudicated. A research corpus is not
the place to republish an accusation, and leaving the name out costs nothing analytically.
(Behaviour, not people; the both-victims lens.)

- *P2 for Kaylen, Melissa and Sarah (the three cycle target-cases). Each is corroborated by
  co-witnesses: Kaylen's mother, her friends Hana and Jennifer, Melissa's friend Amy, and Melissa's
  ex-husband Chris as witness to the car attack.*

  anonymous women relayed by the host or the survivors.*
- *Mimi, his second wife, did not participate, "out of caution for her personal safety." Her
  account reaches us only second-hand and through one public post. She is **not** coded as a
  target-case (as with S12's wife).*
- *Documents read on air: his court declarations (2022), the employer's two public statements,
  his texts and emails, and a sentencing victim-impact statement.*
- *The host stated on air that Jake had not responded to requests for comment. His attorney asked
  that contact go through counsel, and a 15-page list of allegations got no reply.*

## Divergences (recorded, not smoothed)

1. **The largest single-perpetrator sample in the corpus.** About 45 women over 15 years by the
   survivors' count, three of them coded on the cycle. The forms are tailored to each woman's need
   (a crisis, grief, a cause, safety reassurance). The function repeats. Where the replication is
   observable, it supports the operator's low-variance claim at the level of one perpetrator.
2. **C3 n/d for Sarah, ~ for Melissa.** Long distance and a separate life. Recorded, not inferred.
3. **The rubric's signal was selective by audience** (above). It points one way only.
4. **Borrowed fluency through a co-target** (Mimi). Outside 2c's form, and recorded as a boundary.
5. **The doc 07 weaponization risk, run by the perpetrator.** Two harassment petitions against the
   mother of the child he assaulted. A #PAS ("parental alienation") post after he had agreed to no
   contact. A claim to a band's camp that he was blackmailed into his plea.
6. **Demeanour cut both ways in court.** Tears moved a detective and a California judge; a
   Washington judge looked from the character letters to the child's photo. This bears on the 2c
   both-victims gate: demeanour is not evidence of honesty.
7. **An institution as credential.** A cause-aligned employer lent trust it had not checked. Its
   first response protected the institution: delay, comments turned off, the footage credit
   altered, "we don't know him." It later apologised.
8. **Comparing notes blocked from outside, again.** S11d (a therapist), S13e (police), and S14 (a
   platform takedown on a report the creators could not appeal).
9. **Performed consent-checking.** Four short-contact accounts describe constant consent questions
   that they later read as cover, alongside alleged covert recording. Recorded as a **candidate
   device only**. Consent-checking is good practice, and treating it as a red flag would penalise
   it. The device, if real, is the pairing with covert recording, never the questions alone.
