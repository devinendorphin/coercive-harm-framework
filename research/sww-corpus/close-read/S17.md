### The same conduct, eight years apart (trial evidence)

- **DA's closing, "a carbon copy"**: both mothers were living with family, had low self-esteem,
  and wanted a father for their child; both relationships were serious fast and lasted about
  four months; **both had cohabited about three weeks before the injuries**; both women
  believed him at first; "he doesn't start blaming us until they start blaming him", and then
  he blamed each mother.
- **The child-abuse paediatrician** compared the two children's injuries: "absolutely,
  they're the same", and "one individual responding to other kids is going to respond… in a
  similar manner."
- **The defence's own expert**, shown both sets of photographs, agreed they "look very
  similar", and that Jace died of blunt-force trauma ruled a homicide.

This is the corpus's clearest within-perpetrator replication across **two separate
families**, admitted for the limited purposes of common scheme, identity and absence of
accident.

## Target responses (T-fields)

- **Doubt absorbed by explanation.** "If I ever confronted him about anything… by the end of
  the conversation I would feel guilty about ever even doubting him." She went to one of his
  protective-order hearings in support; the threats were shown to come from his phone, and
  that night he blamed his "brother".
- **Checked, and reassured by the institutions.** She took Jace to the ER, a paediatrician and an ENT
  about the ear bruising; each found another explanation (below).
- **An exit plan, delayed by money and fear.** A mortgage co-owned with her sister, credit-card
  debt, his likely suicide threat, and "I hate confrontation."
- **Documentation for vindication.** She printed every text for the prosecutors, to show "how
  he controlled me." The prosecutor questioned her motive.
- **Self-blame, then reassignment.** Her impact statement: "It was me who believed you over my
  own son." Later: "I didn't kill Jace. Cody did."

## Institutional response

| Institution | What happened |
|---|---|
| Payne County DA (2002) | No charges after the first baby's injuries; the mother pleaded to failure to protect and did a treatment plan |
| Courts (2002–10) | **Four final protective orders** granted on testimony. His explanation, to Leslie: "it's so easy to get a protective order… judges will always grant it to a woman against a man" |
| **ER physician (April 2010)** | Charted that it "would appear like the patient was slapped simultaneously on bilateral sides of the head" and **made no referral**: "at 1:30 a.m. it's difficult to engage the Department of Human Services." Offered an optional CT. Leslie: there is always a social worker on call |
| Paediatrician, ENT | Tested for a bleeding disorder (negative); "maybe he was sleeping on his ears wrong." No one raised abuse. The medical examiner testified that **about 95% of ear bruising at this age is abuse** |
| Leslie's family | Parents: "that's concerning, but it's your sister's life." Stacey, a social worker, did not call DHS; the prosecutor read her dilemma aloud: "call us back when you get something more" |
| **Tulsa Child Justice Center** | Co-located DA, police, DHS, medical staff and forensic interviewers. Protective custody (Leslie excluded from the hospital until a hearing two days later); a warrant and arrest within ten days. The detective: "if just one of those medical doctors… would have said something, Jace may still be alive" |
| Trial | Prior-act evidence admitted with a limiting instruction; side-by-side injury photographs. The defence had two witnesses (one limited to fact testimony after a discovery failure). The prosecutor's closing: "**How could somebody so smart be so dumb?** … Be mad at her all you want… but do justice for Jace." The judge **barred Stacey's and the first child's impact statements from being read** |
| Public | Online trolls ("horrible mother"; protests threatened); a friend leaked Leslie's private Facebook message |

## Divergences (recorded, not smoothed)

1. **A test of S15e's prediction (divergence 47), partly met.** S15e predicted that where the
   partner is the route to someone else, C1 toward her is sustained and C2 is absent. Here C1
   was sustained and centred on the child, and C2 toward Leslie was thin ("never yelled at
   me"). **But the harm to Jace was not grooming for access; it was violence, by the
   prosecution's account in rage at an interrupted video game.** So S17 fits the shape of the
   prediction without its mechanism. Recorded as *partial*, not as confirmation.
2. **R-SEP ran through the perpetrator's family**, for thirteen years: online accusations
   against the sister, and the sister's 2023 messages to Leslie. Compare S9's custody
   litigation and S7's parole campaign: the response to exposure outlives the relationship,
   here by proxy.
3. **The network named the danger and was overruled by the target's attachment and by the
   institutions' reassurance.** Stacey found the protective orders and reached the earlier
   mother; three clinicians found other explanations. This is the inverse of S12, where the
   friends' evidence ended the relationship.
4. **An apology extracted by another inmate.** After a fellow inmate told him to "man up", he
   told Leslie's aunt in a prison visiting room "tell her I'm sorry", and repeated it for each
   family member when asked. He has never admitted the act. Recorded as the only
   acknowledgement, not as remorse.
5. **The network's own control, named by itself.** Stacey: "I was super controlling of my
   sister… our dynamics were not super healthy." Recorded at the same weight (both-victims
   lens); it is not coded as perpetration.
