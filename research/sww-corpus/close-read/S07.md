# Season 7 close-read: Amy (Feb – Mar 2021), plus the What Came Next updates (2022–2023)

*Read in full and in order: E1–E6 and both What Came Next update episodes (≈ 58k words).
This is the first season coded under codebook amendment 1, so P-fields (perpetrator
behaviour) are kept separate from T-fields (target response) and O-fields (outcome).
Sources: the survivor's testimony (P2); two family friends (Lauren and her mother Cynthia);
the aunt's letter. Two matters are on the public court record: a **plea to second-degree
murder (15-to-life)** for the mother's killing, and a **2023 plea** to five counts over an
attack on a correctional officer plus death threats against Amy. The perpetrator is called
"Rory" here, the pseudonym the season used. The update episodes use his real first name,
which is not repeated here. Diagnoses he received are recorded only as the survivor reports
them and how he used them. This file codes behaviour, not diagnosis (working agreement 3).*

**Overlap with the clinical literature** (`../../emotional-abuse-clinical-literature.md`). The
host read the 2011 *J. Aggression, Maltreatment & Trauma* passage on adolescent-to-parent
violence on air: **"violent incidents are often the result of a parent who decides to impose
consequences, not the parent who gives in."** This is the separation-danger finding (Wilson &
Daly; Campbell; Monckton Smith stage 4) at child-to-parent scale. The mother herself foresaw
it: after a psychic's warning she put her will and life insurance in order about a year
before the killing (Amy's account).

## Target responses (T-fields; recorded, not tests)

- **Fawning and absorbing.** Amy: "I never matched any energy. I was always trying to absorb
  theirs… I was definitely an enabler." She became the buffer, parentified (put in charge,
  babysitting) and then a surrogate spouse.
- **Secrecy.** Amy never told her mother about the sexual abuse: "biggest regret… she would
  never have let him back in." The mother kept the violence from her sister.
- **Self-blame in the victim parent.** The mother's 1980 diary: "the nicer you are… the more
  you get taken advantage of… you get what you deserve." Her teacher's hope never stopped:
  "she could never give up on him."
- **Building outside life.** Dance, stained glass, 3,000+ volunteer hours, work, a sorority.
  Each success made him angrier ("my wins were his losses").

## The documentation burden in this season

- **Amy's case was carried by her own paperwork for 16 years**: notification lists, finding
  his transfers by searching online, a $4 court-record search walked through by a
  victim-services chief, keeping his hitman letters, the two-year wait for the parole
  transcript, filing her own police report on the death threat. "The onus of responsibility
  lies on me and victims."
- **Contemporaneous recording mattered.** The 2023 conviction relied on the recorded parole
  hearing, the prison's video of the officer attack, and Amy's retained letters. The 2007
  plea had no comparable record of the 10 years of escalation, and was charged as
  unpremeditated.
- **The burden is lifelong.** "26 years of abuse… that continued while he was in jail,
  that continued while he was in prison." Freedom came only when three strikes made release
  practically impossible.

## Divergences (recorded, not smoothed)

1. **New relationship form: sibling-to-sibling and child-to-parent abuse.** The power
   vector is upward against the parent (compare S2's upward vector at work) and downward
   against the younger sibling. The P-field set is unchanged.
2. **Non-contact sexual abuse as a deliberate deniability strategy.** Amy took years to name
   it because "his hands never touched my body."
3. **Lethal R-SEP against the parent, then a campaign against the surviving target that
   lasted 16 years from prison**, using the parole system itself as an instrument.
4. **Family split over the perpetrator.** One aunt supports the victim; another aunt and
   relatives wrote support letters for his parole.
5. **Uneven institutional protection.** The same system that called a death threat against
   a civilian "not a threat" moved within weeks when a correctional officer was attacked.
   Recorded as Amy's reading, and consistent with the sequence.

## What this season adds

- **The separation-risk finding inside the family.** A parent imposing a limit is the
  trigger, read on air from the parent-abuse literature and enacted in the case.
- **Diagnoses as one more vehicle.** The behaviours match S1–S6. The excuse layer
  ("it was my bipolar") is new, and Amy names it as interchangeable with religion or
  children.
- **The longest R-SEP in the corpus**: 16 years of post-exit abuse conducted through the
  justice system. A direct instance of "manipulation of systems" in post-separation abuse
  (Spearman et al. 2022).
