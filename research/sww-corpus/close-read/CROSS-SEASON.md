**Headline on the perpetrator-only basis.** C4 and **R-SEP are present in all 9 coded
target-cases**. C2 and C3 are present in 8 of 9; in S6a the persona's devaluation was thin
and the isolation happened inside the fiction. C1 holds in function across 9 different
forms. C5 is present wherever it was observable. The cases where it was not observable
were all cases where the target exited, the pattern was exposed, or someone died first.
**No case so far shows a P-field absent while the perpetrator had the opportunity to
perform it.**

**Disconfirming check on this headline (revised after the operator's challenge, "so the host
should have interviewed some dead people?").** The first version of this check said R-SEP's
universality was "partly a selection effect" because stories reach a survivor podcast only
when the target got out. That pointed the bias the wrong way. The cases a survivor podcast
cannot hold fall into three classes:
1. **Killed at or after exit.** Their R-SEP was the most severe form of the same response.
   Adding them would strengthen the pattern, not weaken it. They are also not voiceless:
   the literature reaches them through proxy informants and bereaved families (Campbell
   2003; Monckton Smith's 372 homicides; Domestic Homicide Reviews), and so does this corpus
   (S3 was told by the bereaved; S4 by survivors of a mass death).
2. **Still inside.** No exit has happened, so R-SEP has not been triggered. That makes it
   unobservable, not absent.
3. **Left without retaliation.** This is the only class that could disconfirm R-SEP. Its
   members are alive and able to speak. The residual bias is that a quiet exit may not seem
   like a story worth telling, so these people may under-submit.

So the corpus cannot estimate *how often* perpetrators retaliate at exit; that number has to
come from population studies. It can test the *kind* of perpetrator behaviour once the
trigger occurs, and every kind observed so far matches the clinical construct of
post-separation abuse. Watch for class 3 in later seasons. The "not checked" cells mark T1/T2
information I did not code on the first reading. They are to be filled on re-read, not
assumed.

## Divergences that matter for the framework

1. **The power vector is not fixed.** S2 ran *upward* (a subordinate coercing a senior,
   on obligation); S3 ran laterally. The function set appears regardless. This bears on
   doc 02's power vector.
2. **Co-perpetration and transmission.** S3's husband "adopted her habits." The coercion
   recruited from inside the target's household.
3. **Self-harm and illness in the perpetrator** (S2) change how the harm is understood,
   not what was done to others.
4. **Susceptibility varies.** Women with earlier abusive partners recognised it sooner (S1).
5. **Institutions:** child protective services did not investigate (S2); an ER knew but
   could not say (S2); police acted and produced a record (S3); the nursing board's outcome
   was never told to the reporter (S2). The record is made by the target, or by the
   bereaved, in their worst condition (S2, S3).

6. **Scale (S4).** The same move-set runs at group and institutional scale, with state
   actors captured or used: a deputy serving an eviction order, a district attorney who was
   a member, political protection. This is the strongest evidence so far for the "fractal"
   claim (C2 in the quantitative study).
7. **Survivor testimony on variance (S4, Tom Bogue):** "a lot of variations to every
   story… but they all have a common foundation."
8. **Ideology as vehicle (S5).** The same cycle appears in a youth ministry, a marriage and
   a parental household. Rachel: "It wasn't the belief system. It was the abusive people
   who always use whatever they can to control people." The host: "the abuse is so linear."
9. **Independence (S5).** S5a and S5b describe one church. They count as one institution
   with two witnesses, and as two perpetrators (Lance; his adopted son, raised into the
   role). Within-institution transmission (father → adopted son) is a divergence in its own
   right.
10. **What finally moved institutions (S5c).** Several converging reports (a mandated
    reporter, a sibling's disclosure to a counsellor, ex-members) moved the authorities after
    single reports had failed. The charges covered only what had contemporaneous evidence:
    the youngest child's recent abuse. This is a direct instance of the documentation
    burden.
11. **Scope test (S6).** Cases with no relationship (a stranger lure, a predatory landlord,
    hate-crime stalking) show **none** of the C1–C6 cycle, but several of the devices.
    This is specificity evidence: the codebook did not find the cycle everywhere, which
    weighs against reading it as vocabulary laid over any harm.
12. **The documentation burden, before and after (S6, J.E.).** The 2019 calls without
    recordings went nowhere. The 2022–23 calls, recorded by the victims' family, produced a
    guilty plea and a sentence. The family built the record themselves, under threat.
13. **Codebook amendment 1.** Outcomes and target responses (exit, exposure, death,
    conviction) are no longer counted as variance against the claim. Separation is the
    point of highest danger in the clinical literature, so outcome variance is expected
    (`../../emotional-abuse-clinical-literature.md` §2). The C5 "✗" entries in S1–S3 were
    a coding error of this kind and are recoded above.
