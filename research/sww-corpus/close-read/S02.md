# Season 2 close-read: "T" and "Sylvia" (Aug–Oct 2019)

*Read in full and in order: episodes 1–8, including the Q&A (≈ 37k words). Codebook:
`CODEBOOK.md`. All content is the testimony of the survivor and witnesses as broadcast
(P2). The show frames the case as factitious disorder (and, for the son, factitious
disorder imposed on another). That framing is the show's. This file codes **conduct**.*

## The case

| Field | Content |

## Similarities with Season 1 (a different perpetrator, a different setting, a different gender)

- **The same binding device: a fabricated cancer.** S1's Dick told his ex-fiancée for years
  that he had cancer, with fake "lawyer" calls, and told Lindsay 2 on a first date. Sylvia
  built four years on it. Both used medical paperwork or props to make it real: mailed
  bills; real self-inflicted incisions.
- **A suicide performance as a leash.** S1: "Brian had to walk Bubba off a ledge." S2: an
  overdose scene with the children's photos and **T's own gifts and notes** laid on the bed,
  after which T carried lasting guilt (E3).
- **Doubters are discredited.** S1: the sister's and parents' doubts were suppressed to keep
  the peace. S2: the ex-partner was shouted down and the care system turned on him.
- **Animal harm.** S1: Sarah's dog kicked. S2: a cat brought in emaciated with skin gone
  from its back ("locked in an attic"), which recovered fully under clinic care; a dog
  "hit by a car" that was never brought in (E4).
- **Contrition without remorse**, then reversal. S1: the formal "there is much to confess"
  text. S2: an email blaming a disorder ("I really believed my treatments were true"), then
  self-pity at being "abandoned" (E5).
- **Children used.** The daughter was parentified: at eight, she counselled other children
  on an art-therapy DVD ("life isn't always fair"). The children were underfed. The son's
  illnesses were never witnessed.
- **The helper is exhausted.** "I'm starting to be emotionally and physically exhausted",
  just before the overdose call (E3). This is debility produced by obligation rather than by
  force.

## Divergences (recorded, not smoothed)

1. **No romance; female perpetrator; workplace setting.** The function set still appears.
2. **The power vector runs upward or sideways in formal terms.** Sylvia was T's
   subordinate. Her leverage was **moral obligation** (a dying friend), not authority. This
   bears directly on doc 02's power vector: the coercion here does not track formal
   hierarchy. It runs on a manufactured claim on others' care.
3. **Gain was mostly attention, care and status**, not money. A house, a "chemo implant"
   paid for by her father, nursing-school admission on letters of sympathy and gifts are
   the material gains. The show distinguishes factitious disorder from malingering on
   exactly this point. The conduct converges anyway.
4. **The target recruited the community herself.** T: "I led the troops." T carries guilt
   for enrolling her coworkers and her daughter. In S1 the perpetrator built the network
   (sockpuppets). In S2 the target's own virtue built it.
5. **The perpetrator was also self-harming**, by self-inflicted surgery and medication. T's
   anger is tempered ("you almost can't be too mad because she harmed herself"), and the
   host asks "evil or unwell? Can you be both?" This is a real divergence in how the harm
   is understood, though not in what was done to others.
6. **The audience as secondary harm.** T was judged by listeners online for her grief letter
   and her anger (E8).

| Institution | What happened | Reading |

| **Child protective services** | "Did nothing… didn't even investigate." The children went back to Sylvia within a month | a protective institution failed to act on a credible report |
| **Mental-health access** | a six-week wait for therapy; T got in only by writing her doctor a personal letter | the remedy was available only to someone who could navigate it |
| **Her family** | after her death: "I've moved on, you need to move on too" | closure denied to the helper |

## What this season adds

- **Your claim holds at the level of function, across a sharp change of setting.**
  - The same binding device (fabricated cancer), the same suicide-leash, the same
    discrediting of doubters, the same compartmentalization, and the same return attempt.
  - Unlike S1, this is a different person: a woman, a coworker, not a romantic partner.
- **It diverges in form at C1 and C5.** Need-bombing replaces flattery. Exposure replaces
  discard.
- **It diverges in the power vector.** The coercion ran up the formal hierarchy, on
  obligation. This is the most important new finding for the framework.
