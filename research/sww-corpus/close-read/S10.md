# Season 10 close-read: the anthology season (Oct – Dec 2021)

*Read in full and in order: twelve episodes, ≈ 97k words. Eleven survivors' stories, plus E7,
told by a niece on behalf of her late aunt. Coded under amendment 1. Evidence grade: P2
(first-person survivor testimony) except E7 (P3: family memory plus public record as the host
summarised it). Where a conviction or charge is mentioned it is stated as such; everything
else is allegation. **The episodes run 4–14k words, against 50–200k for a full season, so a
P-field that is "not described" (n/d) here is weaker evidence of absence than in a full season.***

## Target responses (T-fields; recorded, not tests)

- **Fawning as survival** at both ends of the corpus: Bailey in 2006 and Annette (Lindahl,
  1979) both escaped by flattery. The host names the fawn response on air.
- **Pathologized targets, accommodating targets**: Ava (an eating disorder, OCD), Angie
  (bulimia, a 72-hour hold), Emma (a PTSD freeze) all describe their own conditions as making
  "no" harder. Recorded as their reading. The codebook codes the perpetrator's use of it
  ("it's just your depression," "fix yourself or I'll leave"), not the condition itself.
- **Collapse by comparing notes** recurs: Maribel and Evelyn; Mia and a second woman; Emily
  and Vanessa. Emily and Vanessa then **institutionalised it**: they check whether he is
  telling each a different story, they file a **joint custody suit served the same day**,
  and they agreed to stage an intervention with any future serious partner.

## Divergences (recorded, not smoothed)

1. **C3 is inverted in the economic-exploitation case (S10j).** Cody courted Emily's friends
   and family after his first theft instead of isolating her. **This is a counter-instance
   to the claim that no P-field is absent where the perpetrator had the opportunity.** The
   network was used as a credibility resource and a source of money. Coded ✗, not rescued.
2. **C4 is not described in two cycle cases (S10d, S10e).** Given the short episodes this
   is weaker evidence than in a full season, but it is recorded as n/d and **counted against
   the headline** (see `CROSS-SEASON.md`).
3. **Lethality turned inward.** Jeff's sequence matches the separation and exposure risk
   profile: firearms acquired, suicidality, threats against officials, loss of status. It
   ended in his own death. This is outcome variance (an O-field), not perpetrator variance.
4. **S10h is the first case in the corpus with binding and deception but none of the
   control sequence.** It belongs on the boundary, not in the count.
5. **Male perpetrators predominate (8 of 10 perpetrators across the 11 cases); two are women**
   (Katie, Janet), and neither shows the control sequence. **Two cases are too few to say
   anything about sex**, and are recorded only.

## What this season adds

- **Specificity evidence from inside the deception class.** S10h, S10b and S10i show that
  lying, gifts and intense early attachment occur without C2–C4 and R-SEP. Since the
  discriminating features are in the control sequence and the response to exit, this
  sharpens what "low variance" must refer to.
- **R-SEP holds in all six cycle cases**, in forms ranging from a guilt hoover (Emma) to
  stalking and assault (Maribel) to an armed confrontation with police (Jeff).
- **Two honest dents in the headline**: C3 inverted (S10j); C4 not described (S10d, S10e).
- **Economic abuse as a full variant**: identity theft, coerced debt, a post-nup as
  punishment, and a "cost of extraction" — the operator's point about the lodger who
  won't leave, here as a co-signed lease demanded as the price of moving out (S10c).
