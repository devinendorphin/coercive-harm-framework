**The embedded case in S11e: Heidi's report about "Peter" is undetermined (U).** Heidi told Sasha
that Peter, an older and wealthy businessman, had hit her, and Sasha saw bruises. Heidi had
recorded his apartment without consent and posted his texts online, tagging his company, and
received a cease-and-desist from the company's lawyers. Peter had earlier appeared at the back
of their church, "following her schedule." Years later Sasha found a news report from the same
year: **an employee of Peter's had accused him of harassment, recorded him, and described a
physical altercation, then withdrew her statement about a month later.** Sasha wonders whether
Heidi retold the employee's story as her own.

That is Sasha's reading. The other reading is that two women made allegations of the same kind
about the same powerful man at the same time, and one withdrew under the legal pressure the
other was also receiving. The host broke into the episode to state the false-report base rate
(2–6 %). The corpus cannot decide between the readings. Heidi's own conduct (recording him,
posting his texts) is recorded at the same weight, under the both-victims lens. **No P-field
is coded for Peter, and Heidi's report is not coded as false.**

## Divergences (recorded, not smoothed)

1. **S11h: the direct aggressor is a child.** The coercive *system* (minimise, blame the
   reporter, economic leverage, love-bombing after incidents, forced forgiveness) sits in the
   employing adults.
   - Molly reads the household as a chain: the father to the mother (observed: "that was
     really stupid of you," cowering apologies), the mother to the nanny, the child to everyone.
     That is her reading.
   - **Behaviour, not people.** The child is not typed and is not coded as a perpetrator. The
     child is plausibly also on the receiving end of the household's pattern. The cycle is
     coded on the household as employer.
   - **Resolved by the operator's ruling (`CODEBOOK.md` amendment 2a).** The child's conduct is
     coded **CP-minor (conduct pattern, minor)**, the way a minor gets "conduct disorder," not
     "antisocial." It is never coded as a P-field. Exposure: the household chain above. Same
     exposure, different role: the younger sibling Blair took the **buffer** role. "Primed" is
     held as risk, not trajectory `[SUPPORTED]`: about a third of children with conduct
     disorder progress to the adult diagnosis.
2. **S11c: C4 to third parties not described.** The poisoning ran toward the target ("no one
   else can be trusted"), which is C3. R-SEP is thin and opportunity was limited. Counted as
   C4 n/d against the headline.
3. **Three near-class-3 exits.** The cross-season file asked to watch for targets who left
   without further active retaliation. **S11c, S11g and S11h come close.** Each saw a single act
   at exit (an obscene message; a re-recruitment call that signalled knowledge; a rant) and
   nothing after. They are coded R-SEP "~". **The "~" threshold is low: one hostile message
   qualifies.** That matters for how the headline is read.
4. **Persisting C3/C4 effects split by whether the network was smeared.**
   - Where the target alone was isolated (S11c), relationships "mended almost overnight" on
     exit: "like a light switch turned back on."
   - Where the network itself was given an account (S11i, S11l), the damage outlasted contact.
     Vera got apologies "years and years later."
   - Hypothesis, offered as a refinement of amendment 1a and untested: **the persisting effect
     tracks C4 (what the network was told), not C3.**
5. **Lethality moved to the next partner (S11d).** The separation danger landed on whoever was
   leaving most recently, and on her children. This is outcome variance, not perpetrator
   variance. But it shows that R-SEP is a property of the perpetrator, not of one relationship.
6. **The perpetrator framed innocents, and the institution punished them (S11i).** False
   accusation used as a coercive tactic, run *through* the school's disciplinary process. This
   is the doc 07 weaponization risk, observed with the perpetrator as the weaponizer.
7. **Sex of perpetrators** (recorded only):
   - male: S11a, b, c, d, i, k, and Matt in S11g;
   - female: Heidi (S11e), Tori (S11j), Patricia (S11l, with Steve), and Matt's mother and
     sister (S11g);
   - S11h: both parents, plus a child.
   Three of the four non-intimate cycle cases include women perpetrators. **Too few to read.**
8. **Clinical person-labels in the material.** A therapist said "narcissist" (S11d); a
   counsellor suggested "histrionic personality disorder" (S11l); the S11h introduction says
