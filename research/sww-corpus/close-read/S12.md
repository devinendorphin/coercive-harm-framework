## His admissions (as Kenzie reports them; she says she has them in writing)

- "I don't really like myself and I'm addicted to being other people. It is the best high."
- "**You are not the first person… I have done this to many women before, and I just get
  better each time.** COVID really incubated the whole situation."
- No job: "I was his full-time job" (Kenzie's phrasing of what he said).
- "Ruth" was **his wife's friend's daughter, with photos pulled from the internet.** "Ruth's
  mother" was **a real woman he had been married to for six weeks in his early twenties.** He
  told Kenzie six years and a child.
- The confession email to his wife: "I would go onto internet sites. I would post ads for nanny
  positions… telling them this rand story about how successful I was." He left a stage
  direction in it: "[tears]." "Then she replied and my darkness was hooked."

*Analytic note, not a finding:* the email repeats to the wife the device used on Kenzie:
"you were the first person I found myself being honest about who I was… you were it for me, the
end of the road." Kenzie: "how many times you painted pictures of loving me, of saying these same
words to me."

## Target responses (T-fields)

- **Due diligence, absorbed by the cover story.** She ran a reverse image search in month one and
  found a half-matching profile. The explanation was WITSEC. Later, a page with his real name
  went down after friends found it, and Sophia: it "**added validity again to his witness
  protection.**" The cover story was built to turn disconfirming evidence into confirmation.
- **The network validated the plausibility.** A friend in the news ("not that far-fetched") and a
  former murder-trial juror ("aligning") endorsed the story. Kenzie's father was a detective.
  Plausibility from people with relevant expertise did the cover story's work.
- **Caretaking as the trap's last stage.** She nursed him through fake radiation, and stayed
  after learning he was married because "**I cannot leave a man with cancer.**"
- **Persisting effect of C1** (new, see divergence 3): "an absolutely unachievable high… Now
  when I date people I feel normal, which is the correct way to feel, and that feels low and
  wrong."
- **A trigger long after:** her own tan lines on holiday recalled his unexplained tan, from the
  vacation with his wife.
- Refused an NDA and payment. Asked only to be reimbursed for the child's room. Told the story
  publicly because "people should know that that exists."

## Divergences (recorded, not smoothed)

1. **The controlling apparatus is fictional state power, not personal threat.** Joe never
   threatened Kenzie. The constraints (secrecy, permission to leave the house, surveillance,
   "don't Google him") were attributed to a third party: the federal government. **This is
   coercion by proxy authority**, a different mechanism from S1–S11, where the perpetrator
   enforced the rules himself.
2. **C3 is contested by the target.** Kenzie says he never tried to isolate her. Her friends saw
   the Minnesota move as exactly that. **He also cultivated her network** (S10j's inverse C3).
   Recorded as ~, with both accounts.
3. **A persisting effect of C1, not C4.** In S11 the effects that outlasted contact tracked C4
   (what the network was told). S12's lasting harm is a **recalibrated baseline**: normal
   intimacy now "feels low and wrong." **This qualifies the S11 hypothesis:** persisting
   effects can come from C1 as well as from C4.
4. **R-SEP is ~ and near class 3.** Exposure brought an offer of silence money and narrative
   management, not pursuit. With S11c, S11g and S11h, that is the fourth near-class-3 exit.
   Unlike them, the perpetrator was exposed rather than left. He was the one who had something
   to lose (marriage, wife's money, reputation), and offered to pay.
5. **The second target is silent (U).** The wife's experience is unknown. Joe's account of her
   ("cold," "business") is a perpetrator's statement about a partner to another partner, and is
   not coded.
6. **Operator's fluency rubric (CODEBOOK 2c), applied.** Joe described his wife to Kenzie,
   fluently, as cold and transactional while deceiving both. That is **fluent denigration of a
   partner to an audience during the relationship**, which is the refined form the rubric kept.
   **One instance in its favour.**
7. **Story appropriation (CODEBOOK 2b): related, not the same.**
   - **Confirmed: identity and image appropriation.** A real child's photos, and a real
     ex-wife repurposed as "Ruth's mother," confirmed by his admission and by the
     non-existence of the child.
   - **Built on his own kernel.** The WITSEC story grew from a real local-news interview in
     which he described a crime he witnessed. That is his own story inflated, not someone
     else's.
   - **No evidence of absorbing a prior victim's account.** The coworker who had witnessed a
     gang shooting was met afterward, and nothing shows he took her details.
   - **2b's specific claim (retelling a prior victim's story) still has no confirmed instance.**

## What this season adds

- **The deception class taken to its limit, with the control sequence present only in
  function.** The rules were real in effect: permission to move, secrecy, a wall against
  checking, relocation. They were attributed to an invented authority. S10h, S10i and S11j
  showed deception without control. **S12 shows control routed entirely through deception.**
- **A serial perpetrator by his own account** ("many women… I just get better each time"), with
  one confirmed earlier marriage of six weeks and a possible earlier target ("Stephanie," who
  sent real photos of herself).
- **The protective case for the network.** Kenzie: "if he was able to isolate me… this would be
  a different story with a different ending." That is the counterfactual C3 exists to produce.
- **A gap in the law** (doc 03): two years of fraud that touched the target's money, body and
  family plans, with no civil remedy because there was no physical injury.
