## Divergences (recorded, not smoothed)

1. **Police pattern-recognition protected a falsely accused partner (S18c).** The officers
   disbelieved Braun's sexual-assault report against Amy W. *because of the reporter's
   history*. This is the opposite of S9 and S14, where a false report succeeded. It shows
   that a reporter's documented pattern can be used against a false allegation. It does not
   establish how often that happens. **X3 bears on it**: the same pattern-based disbelief
   would harm a true reporter with a contested history.
2. **A false accusation against a trans 13-year-old, recanted (S18g).** Tyler was questioned
   at school about molesting children on a bus after a classmate's parents told her to say
   it; she apologised years later: "I made up that lie about you because my parents told
   me I should." Tyler: "I take this stuff seriously on both ends." **The false-accusation
   channel ran against a member of a targeted minority, at the direction of parental and
   religious authority.** Recorded at full weight under the both-victims lens.
3. **The whistleblower's methods produced a court order against him (S18h).** Jimmy I. read
   Kevin's phone without permission, published his private messages, posted his father's
   funeral details ("we'll see you at your dad's funeral"), was blamed for a bomb threat to
   that funeral (he denies it and suggests Kevin made it himself: **U**, and his inference is
   recorded as such), and was served a restraining order he did not contest. The anonymous
   women's blog then shut down under threat of suit. **This is the weaponisation risk in
   the other direction**: exposure carried out as retaliation hands the exposed person a
   legitimate grievance and silences the people the exposure was meant to help. The
   messages he found are not in doubt; what they establish (contempt, misogyny, a stated
   wish to kill the band, sexualised talk about a niece) is **speech**, not the conduct the
   women alleged, which remains **U** here.
4. **Counting witnesses is evidence only to the degree they are independent (S18f).**
   Connelly: "Why do you believe one person and not 12?" The S18f accounts are partly
   independent: graffiti before the disclosure, an artist's year of warnings, and idiosyncratic
   details (morphine, ketamine, Polaroids) that matched what Tyler had seen in the shop. The
   ~300 messages after the public video are not independent of the video. The episode
   supports the pattern; the arithmetic in the quotation does not.
5. **Written and spoken versions diverge, both ways.** Archie agreed in writing that he hated
   strip clubs, after pressuring Tyler into one in person (Tyler: "it was in writing"); after
   the assault he asked Shane to talk in person, not by text. Gaby: "that never happened",
   two weeks after an admission. Wife #3 had forgotten a punch her own messages recorded.
   **The record contradicts the account, and the record is usually the target's** (compare
   S15).
6. **Credibility depends on the kind of harm (S18g).** Tyler confronted Archie about the
   toolbox but not about his girlfriend: "no one questions whether he fucked me over with
   the toolbox… people will question if he assaulted my girlfriend."
7. **A diagnosis claimed as a shield (S18f, S18g).** "Autistic and from Russia, so he doesn't
   know our culture." Shane, who is on the spectrum: "you can't wear our thing as your badge."
   This is amendment 4b's self-diminishment used as a defence, with one witness saying it was
   knowingly false. **Behaviour, not people**: the field codes the *claim made as an
   excuse*, never the diagnosis.
8. **A non-harm case under the same banner (S18e).** Chris Williams's date vanished after a
   run of updates on her way to the trip (airport surveillance later showed she did get into a
   car) and never answered. There was no extraction, no control and no pattern.
   The show runs it as a cautionary tale. It belongs to no class this study codes, which
   supports the standing note: the podcast's banner is not a definition.
9. **Selection for tolerance, the target's inference (S18g).** On Tyler's resignation:
   "if I can't keep you here, being as tolerant as you are…" Tyler: "did you handpick me
   because you thought I would tolerate more?" **Recorded as an inference from one remark.**
   Compare the doulas' "she targeted highly empathetic people" (also an inference).
10. **The state's most effective tool was a non-state one (doulas, S18f).** In both serial
    cases the stopping mechanism was a platform (TikTok, Instagram) and a professional
    community (doulas, tattoo conventions); the police and the regulator moved after
    virality, or not at all. The S17–S24 file already notes that in S18 the state moved within a week of
    the case going viral. This sharpens the speech-boundary and due-process questions in doc 07.
