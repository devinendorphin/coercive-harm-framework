## The disconfirming side: personas congruent with the conduct

- **R. Kelly.** Convicted in 2021 of federal racketeering and sex trafficking; 30 years, upheld
  on appeal in 2025. His public persona and catalogue were overtly sexual. That is **not** an
  inverse persona.
- **Weinstein (partly).** The bullying was public; only the cause-championing was inverse.

**So inversion is not universal among exposed celebrity offenders.** A refined hypothesis, which
fits S14 as well:

> **The inverse persona tracks access-by-trust.** Where access to victims runs through being
> trusted (children's hosts, family spokesmen, preachers, hospital volunteers, a cause-aligned
> ally in S14), the persona is the inverse of the conduct, because the persona *is* the key.
> Where access runs through power or gatekeeping (a producer's casting power, a star's
> industry power), the persona can be congruent, and the power does the work.
> `[HYPOTHESIS]`

*Upgrade if* sorting a broad sample of convicted public figures by access route predicts persona
type above chance. *Downgrade if* inverse personas are as common among power-access cases.

## Two biases to correct for

1. **Selection.** "The hypocrite" is the better news story, so inverse cases are
   over-represented in what anyone remembers. A count drawn from memory or headlines
   overstates the pattern. A real test needs a sampling frame such as all convictions of
   public figures in a period, not the famous ones.
2. **The inference runs one way.** These cases go **from documented conduct back to the
   persona**. They do **not** license suspicion **from** a wholesome persona to hidden abuse.
   The base rate forbids it, since most family entertainers are not abusers. Reading
   wholesomeness as a red flag would be the doc 07 weaponization failure, aimed at anyone
   likeable. The same rule as consent-checking (CODEBOOK 3b) applies: **the persona is never
   coded alone**, only against contradicting conduct.

## For the consent-law question (doc 03 §4; CODEBOOK 3f)

- Savile is the cleanest legal illustration. The **role** was the deception, and it bought
  **physical access** (keys to wards). Institutional safeguarding law reaches that through
  access controls and vetting, without any fraud-in-consent theory at all.
- Cosby shows the persona as **evidence**. The sworn admission about quaaludes is a concealed
  material fact (**drugging**, i.e. incapacitation), not the persona.
- Whedon shows the **outer boundary**. A feminist persona contradicted by marital infidelity is
  deceptive, but it is exactly the general relational dishonesty the fraud prong must exclude.
- **The persona earns its place in law as proof of pattern and intent, never as the offence.**
