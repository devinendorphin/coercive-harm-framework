# Research: Foreseeability & Risk-Assessment — untying the "serious effect" knot

*Compiled 2026-07-09 for `/develop 04`, at Endorphin's direction. Tests his insight:
"foreseeability = demonstrating the clusters of behaviors are predictable; if so
that's easy." Springboards off validated risk instruments and the survivor-narrative
corpus ("Something Was Wrong").*

## The knot this addresses

OBJ-009/011/015 (doc 08): the offense element "serious effect on the victim" is at
once the vagueness cure, the 3.4% conviction bottleneck, a re-traumatization vector,
and a penalty on resilient victims. Escape = shift the offense from *proving harm to a
specific victim* to *the objectively foreseeable harmfulness of the conduct pattern*.

## Finding 1 — the foreseeability limb ALREADY EXISTS in enacted law `[ESTABLISHED]`

England & Wales s.76 requires the perpetrator **"knows or ought to know"** the
behaviour will have a serious effect. **"Ought to know" = "that which a reasonable
person in possession of the same information would know"** — an **objective
foreseeability test**, already on the books, focused on *effect/foreseeability* rather
than specific intent. Endorphin's reframing is therefore not an invention; it is a
**re-weighting** onto a limb the flagship statute already has. (CPS guidance;
legislation.gov.uk s.76.)

## Finding 2 — the pattern's harm is professionally predictable (validated instruments) `[ESTABLISHED]`

The field has built and **validated actuarial instruments that forecast serious harm —
up to homicide — from documented coercive-control patterns**:
- **Danger Assessment (Campbell et al.)** — validated to predict intimate-partner
  femicide / near-lethality. (PMC validation study.)
- **SARA** (Spousal Assault Risk Assessment) — predicts reassault.
- **ODARA** (Ontario Domestic Assault Risk Assessment) — predicts DV recidivism;
  correlates with DA and SARA.
- **DASH**, **MOSAIC-20** — risk/lethality screening.
- Systematic review: Graham, Sahay, Rizo, Messing, Macy (2021), *Trauma, Violence, &
  Abuse* — validity/reliability of IPV homicide & reassault risk tools.

**Legal significance:** if a profession has *validated* tools that predict the harm
from the pattern, then the harm is **foreseeable as a matter of established
knowledge** — a reasonable person "ought to know" it. This is the legal-grade anchor
for the foreseeability limb. Endorphin's "that's an easy thing" is substantially
right: the predictability is already demonstrated, just not yet imported into this
framing.

### CRITICAL GUARDRAIL on how to use the instruments (do not skip)

Foreseeability must be established **generically**, not **individually**:
- **DO** cite the *existence and validation* of these instruments as *legislative /
  adjudicative fact* that *this kind of conduct* foreseeably causes serious harm —
  grounding a generic "ought to know" standard once, in the statute's reasoning.
- **DO NOT** score an individual defendant on the Danger Assessment and offer the
  score as evidence they "should have foreseen" harm. Using group base-rates to prove
  individual culpability is the **"actuarial justice" trap** (cf. *State v. Loomis*,
  Wis. 2016, on risk scores in sentencing) — a due-process and disparate-impact
  problem. These tools were built for **victim safety-planning** (err toward
  over-prediction), NOT for proving criminal elements beyond reasonable doubt (which
  must err toward the defendant). Repurposing them as prosecution evidence inverts
  their error-tolerance. Keep foreseeability generic; keep the individual case on the
  documented *conduct*, not a risk *score*.

## Finding 3 — the structural payoff: result-crime → endangerment-crime `[NORMATIVE]`

If harm is objectively foreseeable from the pattern, the offense can be built as
**engaging in the foreseeably-harmful pattern** (an *endangerment* offense), with
*actual* serious effect demoted from **element** to **sentencing aggravator**.
Analogy: **DUI** — no victim required; the foreseeably-dangerous conduct is itself the
crime. This dissolves all four horns of the knot: vagueness (cured by defining the
conduct + objective-foreseeability, not the victim's psychology), bottleneck (no
individual harm-causation to prove), re-traumatization (victim needn't perform
damage), resilience-penalty (crime complete regardless of coping).

**The honest tradeoff (no free lunch):** endangerment *broadens* liability — no harm
required — which raises the *opposite* both-victims risk: a false accuser need only
allege the *conduct pattern*, not prove *harm*. Whether this is the better tradeoff
turns on whether the **conduct pattern is more objectively documentable than
subjective harm** (plausibly yes: messages, financial records, isolation evidence,
third-party witnesses are more provable and less re-traumatizing than psychological
injury). **Must go to `/liberty-review`** — "no harm required" is a real expansion.
