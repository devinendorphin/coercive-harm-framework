# Research: Apex-power mechanisms — reaching coercive harm at the top

*Source of record for docs/12. Session `apex-power`, 2026-07-11. Discipline: the task was
primed ("let's do apex power"); the research was run as a refutation test — do these
mechanisms reach apex **coercive/psychological** harm, or only adjacent wrongs? Findings
recorded with limits, not just capabilities.*

---

## 1. Coercive control travels beyond the domestic (concept, not tool yet) [SUPPORTED]

- White Rose eTheses (2024), "Understanding Coercive Control Within and Beyond the Domestic
  Environment" — explicitly aims to situate coercive control in workplaces/institutions.
- "Observing coercive control beyond intimate partner violence" (2020; PsycNet
  2020-75424-001).
- Kauzlarich, "Work-Related Intimate Partner Violence (WIPV): A Systematic Review and
  Feminist Conceptual Analysis," *Human Resource Management* (2025).
- Takeaway: the domination/isolation/dependency mechanism is relationship-form-independent;
  the domestic gate in doc 03 is jurisdictional convenience, not a boundary of the
  phenomenon. **But**: recognizing the phenomenon upward ≠ having an enforcement tool upward.

## 2. Command / superior responsibility — conceptual template only [ESTABLISHED doctrine / NORMATIVE transplant]

- Rome Statute Art. 28; *In re Yamashita* (1946). A superior is liable for subordinates'
  crimes they knew/should have known of and failed to prevent or punish.
- Borrow the **structure** (liability follows effective control, flows *up*), NOT the
  content (atrocity law). Importing its severity would be the "anchor as ammunition" error
  (CLAUDE.md agreement 7). Value is orientational: a mature legal system *can* make power
  itself the basis of liability — the inversion Finding B says the framework lacks.
- **Untested** as a transplant to coercive-harm patterns; doc 12's weakest load-bearing idea.

## 3. Live reach-up mechanisms

### 3a. Caremark oversight duty extended to officers — In re McDonald's Corp. (Del. Ch. 2023) [ESTABLISHED]
- Jan 25 2023, Del. Court of Chancery (VC Laster): **first** holding that corporate
  **officers** owe a fiduciary duty of oversight (previously only directors, since
  *Caremark* 1996 / *Marchand v. Barnhill* 2019). CPO David Fairhurst adequately alleged to
  have breached it by consciously ignoring red flags of a toxic sexual-harassment culture.
- Reaches the **C-suite** for conscious blind-eye to a coercive-conduct pattern.
- **Limits (all real):** (1) requires **bad faith / conscious disregard** (a loyalty claim),
  not mere negligence — high bar. (2) The companion claims against McDonald's **directors
  were dismissed** because they acted once informed — the tool bites the *conscious* blind
  eye only. (3) **Shareholder derivative** suit; recovery runs to the **corporation, not
  victims** (Finding C residue) — governance instrument, not victim remedy. (4) Reaches
  *failure to oversee*, not the executive's own coercive conduct.
- Sources: Del. Ch. opinion coverage — Covington, DLA Piper, K&L Gates, Cleary Gottlieb,
  Mintz, McDermott (mwe.com), D&O Diary (all Jan–Apr 2023).

### 3b. Positive duty to prevent — UK Worker Protection Act 2023; Australia Respect@Work [ESTABLISHED]
- **UK:** Worker Protection (Amendment of Equality Act 2010) Act 2023; new preventative duty
  **in force 26 Oct 2024** — employers must take "reasonable steps" to prevent sexual
  harassment. Enforced by **EHRC** (independent regulator, not prosecutor). Tribunals may
  uplift compensation **up to 25%** for breach. EHRC technical guidance updated Sep 2024.
- **Australia:** Anti-Discrimination and Human Rights Legislation Amendment (Respect at
  Work) Act 2022; positive duty to **proactively eliminate** sexual harassment "as far as
  possible"; AHRC enforcement powers from **Dec 2023**.
- Structural significance: **reactive → proactive**, obligation on **leadership**, enforced
  by **independent equality regulators** — partially escapes Finding C (prosecutor capture).
- **Limits:** harassment-*specific* (not the full coercive/psychological pattern);
  "reasonable steps" is soft; in the UK an individual **cannot enforce the duty standalone**
  (only EHRC can) — the uplift rides a successful harassment claim.
- Sources: Morgan Lewis (Sep/Oct 2024), Cooley, Reed Smith, BDO; Ashurst, MinterEllison,
  KPMG AU; *Industrial Law Journal* 53(3):505 (2024).

### 3c. Misconduct / abuse of public office [SUPPORTED]
- Common-law misconduct in public office (England/Wales) + analogous abuse-of-office
  offenses reach officials abusing their position, **including non-financial** abuse — the
  closest public-sector analogue to "coercive harm by one who *is* the institution."
- **Limit:** enforced by the prosecutorial apparatus the seed indicted (Finding C); rarely
  charged for non-corruption conduct. (Primary-source verification still needed.)

### 3d. Global Magnitsky individual sanctions [SUPPORTED]
- Personal consequences (asset freeze, travel ban) on **individual** apex human-rights
  abusers, bypassing their institution's protection; **non-prosecutorial** (executive).
- Anchor value: existence-proof that a legal order can attach personal cost to otherwise
  untouchable apex actors.
- **Limit (sharp):** geopolitically **selective**, executive-discretion-driven — itself a
  power instrument, weaponizable. Existence-proof, not a model to emulate uncritically.

## 4. Escaping Finding C — independent, non-prosecutorial review [NORMATIVE]

- Pair reach-up liability (§§2–3) with determinations by **independent bodies**
  (inspectors-general, ombudsmen, statutory inquiries, equality regulators — EHRC/AHRC is
  the working case).
- **Whistleblower protection** is the precondition for apex pattern-documentation to surface
  (the powerful control the information environment).
- Hardest constraint: **independence from the reviewed power** (cannot be staffed/funded/
  dissolved at the apex actor's will). Most often defeated in practice.
- `NEEDS-LIBERTY-REVIEW`: a standing apex-inquisitor is itself capturable/weaponizable.

## 5. The residual (what still can't be reached)

1. **True apex** — founder-CEO with voting control + captive board; controlling shareholder;
   head of state. The accused **is** the institution; no internal body has independent power
   over them. §§3a–3b presuppose a board/regulator/corporate form to turn against the actor.
   Real checks here are **political, reputational (exposure), exit** — not law.
2. **Finding C mitigated, not eliminated** — every §3 mechanism still routes through some
   institution power influences.
3. **Coverage asymmetry** — the domestic offense reaches a partner's *direct* coercive
   conduct; apex tools mostly reach **failure-to-prevent / oversight-failure**, a narrower
   and different wrong. The framework reaches the apex actor best when they *presided over* a
   pattern, worst when they *personally are* it — the inverse of Finding B's wish.

## 6. Net + status change

OBJ-027: `UNRESOLVED` → **`PARTIALLY MITIGATED`**. A partial toolkit for the **sub-apex**
powerful (officers, senior managers, public officials inside institutions with live
oversight) + honest admission the **true apex** stays largely beyond law. Progress and an
honest ceiling in the same breath.
