# Research: Arankan / aránkàn (Yoruba) — anthropology deep-dive

*Source of record for docs/11 Part A "Arankan" entry. Session: /anthropology arankan,
2026-07-10. Disconfirming discipline: Yoruba is a major world language (~45M speakers)
with real lexicography, so — unlike kunlangeta — the "is the word even attested
independently of Murphy?" test is decisive here. It passes.*

---

## 1. Independent attestation — the test kunlangeta failed, arankan passes [ESTABLISHED]

**arankàn is a genuine, independently-attested Yoruba word.** It appears in Yoruba–English
dictionaries (Glosbe; yoruba.english-dictionary.help; and general Yoruba lexicography)
with the gloss **"malice."** This is a materially stronger evidentiary footing than
kunlangeta, which is effectively single-sourced through Murphy. The word does not depend
on Murphy 1976 for its existence.

**Spelling correction:** the attested form is **arankan / aránkàn** (with tonal
diacritics). Murphy renders it "arankan." The variant **"aranakan"** carried in the
original docs/11 candidate list is **not** attested and appears to be a transcription
error — drop it.

## 2. The semantic catch: trait-noun vs. person-category

Here the disconfirming look pays off in an unexpected direction. The dictionaries gloss
arankàn as an **abstract quality — "malice"** (a trait), whereas Murphy renders it as a
**person-category**: "a person who always goes his own way regardless of others, who is
uncooperative, full of malice, and bullheaded" (*Science* 191:1021, verbatim). So Murphy
(or her rendering) converts a Yoruba *quality-word* into an English *person-taxon* and
then draws the "parallel with our concept 'psychopath.'"

Two implications:
- **For the framework's core commitment (behavior, not people):** the *Yoruba* usage —
  arankàn as a nameable *trait/quality* rather than a kind-of-person — is actually
  *closer* to the framework's "conduct, not category" discipline than Murphy's
  psychopath-parallel gloss. This is a point in the anchor's favor, but it also means the
  entry should attribute the *person-typing* to Murphy's framing, not to the Yoruba.
- **Caution on the "psychopath parallel":** Murphy's cross-cultural-psychopathy argument
  may over-read a trait word as a diagnostic person-category. Cite arankàn as evidence
  that Yoruba names the *malice/uncooperativeness trait*, not as evidence that Yoruba has
  a folk-psychopath taxon equivalent to a DSM personality disorder.

## 3. Not-illness classification — well grounded here [ESTABLISHED, for the source claim]

Per Murphy's method (Science 191:1020): for the Yoruba she worked "with a group of three
native healers and a member of an indigenous cult," and — this is the key line — "when I
pressed this point with the Yoruba healers they specifically denied that these patterns
are illness." So the "arankan is not treated as illness / not the healers' domain"
classification comes **directly from Yoruba healers**, not from an outside inference. That
is a stronger provenance than the Eskimo single-key-informant route.

## 4. Colonial / exoticization check — the strongest of the three entries

The Yoruba material is **not** a pure outside-observer artifact. The parent study —
Leighton, Lambo, Hughes, Leighton, Murphy & Macklin, *Psychiatric Disorder Among the
Yoruba* (Cornell Univ. Press, 1963) — was **co-authored by T. Adeoye Lambo**, a Yoruba
psychiatrist (widely described as the first Western-trained psychiatrist in Africa),
whose life's work was the *decolonization* of psychiatry: he integrated Yoruba
traditional healers into the Aro therapeutic-village model. A prominent cultural insider
co-produced the source. The Cornell-Aro project itself was framed as a rebuttal to
racist "African mind" theorizing. This meaningfully raises confidence in the Yoruba
categories relative to the Eskimo ones — while still warranting the standard caution that
the categories were rendered into English within a mid-century transcultural-psychiatry
program with its own aims.

## 5. What is still missing [RESEARCH NEEDED]

- **No documented community intervention/response for arankan.** Murphy gives the term and
  the not-illness classification, but — unlike kunlangeta's "ice" — records *no* specific
  traditional response to an arankàn person. The docs/11 Part B axes cannot yet be filled
  for arankan; do not invent them.
- Primary Yoruba-language sources on how such a person was handled communally; whether/how
  **Ifá divination** framed persistent malice; proverbs (*òwe*) about the arankàn type;
  any relation to concepts like *ìkà* (wickedness/cruelty) or *àìníwà* (bad character /
  lack of *ìwà*). Yoruba ethics centers *ìwà* (character); situating arankàn within the
  *ìwà* framework is the highest-value next step and would connect to workstream 02.
