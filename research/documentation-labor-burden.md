# Research: the documentation burden — does the beginning of evidence require the victim to overstretch?

*Compiled 2026-10-04 at Endorphin's direction, from the operator's account below. Feeds doc
04 (evidentiary standards) and the *Something Was Wrong* corpus study
(`sww-corpus/README.md`). It was searched, not assumed; disconfirming evidence was sought
and is reported.*

## The claim (operator's account, verbatim in substance)

> "It takes a lot of labor to identify and write it down for oneself, let alone for record.
> And it is done in relative exhaustion. If this happens in the workplace and you have an
> adversarial manager who has their target on you … you have to do your job at the work
> hours, and usually the manager makes it so that you're in chaos, and that means that you
> have to wait until you're home or alone in the office to begin the work of writing things
> down before your memory starts to revise it, or to assemble an evidentiary body. For a
> victim, the beginnings of evidence require one to already overstretch themselves."

*Status of the account itself:* first-person testimony from the operator's own experience
(P2-equivalent). It is recorded as testimony, and assessed below against independent
evidence.

## Assessment, component by component

| # | Component | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Documenting abuse takes substantial labour | `[SUPPORTED]` | **Stephenson et al., CSCW 2025** (19 legal support providers, Wisconsin; honorable mention for best paper): capturing evidence of technology-facilitated abuse is "difficult and time-consuming," especially "when there are years of harassing texts to document." Survivors must supply heavy context for others to see the harm. Evidence of covert monitoring is hard to collect and hard to tie to the abuser. **Vera-Gray & Kelly (2020)**, *"safety work"*: the invisible, habitual labour women do against the threat of violence. It comes from public-harassment research, so it is an analogous frame, not a direct measure of documentation |
| 2 | Done in exhaustion | `[SUPPORTED]` | **Nielsen & Einarsen (2012)** meta-analysis (*Work & Stress* 26(4); 66 samples, N = 77,721): workplace bullying is associated with mental-health problems, **post-traumatic stress symptoms and burnout**. Prospectively, bullying predicts later mental-health problems. **Arnsten (2009)**, *Nat. Rev. Neurosci.* 10: "even quite mild acute uncontrollable stress can cause a rapid and dramatic loss of prefrontal cognitive abilities" (working memory, executive function). Those are the faculties that assembling a record requires. *Counter-evidence kept:* Nielsen & Einarsen found only weak or non-significant associations with **sleep** and **job performance** |
| 3 | The record must be made before memory revises it | `[ESTABLISHED]`, in the law's own design | **FRE 803(5)** (recorded recollection) admits a record only if it was "made or adopted by the witness **when the matter was fresh** in the witness's memory." The law privileges the contemporaneous record, and so places the timing burden on whoever experienced the event. That memory is reconstructive and open to later revision is mainstream memory science (Loftus; *gen.*, not verified this session) |
| 4 | In the workplace, the structure places documentation on the target | `[SUPPORTED]` (US) | **Faragher v. City of Boca Raton / Burlington Industries v. Ellerth (1998):** absent a tangible employment action, the employer escapes liability for supervisor harassment if it took reasonable care **and the employee "unreasonably failed to take advantage of any preventive or corrective opportunities."** Courts have held that fear of retaliation, stated in "conclusionary" terms, does not excuse a failure to report. Harassment that is not tied to a protected class has **no general US cause of action.** The Healthy Workplace Bill has been introduced in many states. Massachusetts S.1316 was reported favourably in Oct 2025, but as of this search **no state has enacted it** (advocacy-sourced summary; S1-pending) |
| 5 | "The manager makes it so that you're in chaos" | `[SUPPORTED]` as mechanism; intent not claimed | Biderman's *induced debility/exhaustion* and doc 02's *preemptive seeding of chaos* name this function. **Analytic link (`[HYPOTHESIS]`):** the debility function degrades the capacity that evidence-making needs (time, working memory, composure). So the coercion attacks the record before the record exists. This is a claim about **structural effect**, not about any individual's intent |
| 6 | Overall: "the beginnings of evidence require one to already overstretch" | **`[SUPPORTED]` for victim-initiated forums** (US workplace, civil protective orders, custody). **Not universal** | **Disconfirming evidence found:** *evidence-led* ("victimless") prosecution of domestic abuse in England and Wales. Police and prosecutors build the case from 999 calls, body-worn video, digital evidence and third-party witnesses, without requiring the victim. So the overstretch is **a design choice of the forum, not a law of nature.** Even there, HM CPS Inspectorate (Oct 2025) found the CPS must improve its decisions where evidence-led prosecution is viable, and set a Dec 2026 deadline for case reviews to assess it. The burden-shifting design exists and is under-used |

## What this does to the framework

- **doc 04:** the evidentiary standard should be judged by **who must produce the
  evidence, and in what condition**, not only by what counts. A standard that is
  neutral on paper, but requires contemporaneous records from a person whose capacity is
  being degraded by the conduct at issue, is not neutral in effect. Candidate design
  responses (`[NORMATIVE]`, `NEEDS-LIBERTY-REVIEW`):
  - an **evidence-led / offender-centric** default for coercive-control matters;
  - **institutional duties to preserve records** (the employer holds email, scheduling and
    HR files);
  - **late-record tolerance**: a delay in documentation is not, by itself, an
    impeachment ground where the conduct included debility tactics.
- **Corpus study:** see `sww-corpus/README.md`, A9. A survivor-run, single-curator
  documentation project is what this burden predicts at scale.
