## Season summaries

**S17 — the murder of a 3-year-old by a partner with a known record (Tulsa, 2010)**
- **Warning signs existed, and were public.** Before the relationship he had **four final
  protective orders** on Oklahoma's public court database. In 2002 a 6-month-old suffered
  subdural and retinal hemorrhages in his care.
  - The county DA declined to charge, because "they couldn't determine which one did it".
  - **The infant's mother pleaded to misdemeanor failure-to-protect** and did a treatment
    plan. He walked (narr.; the prosecutor's opening statement as read).
- **He turned the false-accusation narrative into a weapon:** "it's so easy to get a
  protective order … judges will always grant it to a woman against a man" (narr., E2).
- **Mandated reporters failed.**
  - An ER physician charted ear bruising "as if slapped simultaneously on bilateral sides
    of the head". He made no referral, because "at 1:30 a.m. it's difficult to engage DHS".
  - Pediatricians saw the bruising too; nobody reported it (narr., E4).
- **What worked:**
  - Tulsa's co-located Child Justice Center (DA, police, DHS, forensic interviewers,
    medical staff in one building).
  - Prior-act evidence admitted with a limiting instruction (common scheme, absence of
    accident), shown as side-by-side injury photos.
- **The process was manipulated, and the parent was blamed.**
  - He feigned seizures and used a wheelchair to provoke a mistrial; the judge ordered
    daily medical clearance from the jail.
  - The prosecutor's closing said of the mother: "How could somebody so smart be so
    dumb?" She feared a failure-to-protect charge.
  - The judge did not allow the aunt's victim-impact statement, or the earlier child's,
    to be read aloud.

**S20 — the "Brody" catfish (Iowa, Tennessee, North Carolina; 2007–2023) — READ IN FULL**
*All 12 episodes read in full on 2026-10-04 (E01b in the feed duplicates E01). Grade:
narr. throughout unless stated. The narration is unusually well documented: texts read on

    shared home, a "dog camera" she had given away and still had access to, and control of

  persona then demanded she explain it, and called it "an out" when she would not. It also

    him.

    flew in and stayed at her home.

    The fiction defeated the intake form.
  - **Day two:** the victim brought the investigation she had done herself (texting-app

    in that state.

  misdemeanor cyberstalking. The charge was **dismissed at the complainants' election** and
  she was **not convicted**. The earlier word "uncharged" was wrong.
- **The both-victims record (doc 07), stated alongside the strength of the evidence, not

- **X7 inversion.** Every parent and friend who correctly suspected the truth *chose not to

## Cross-season findings (the part that matters for the framework)

**X1 — Licensing boards are the slowest instrument in the corpus** (doc 05; strengthens
OBJ-030) `[SUPPORTED]`, from multiple independent instances.
- **The instances:**
  - Braun kept her social-work registration about 3.5 years after the first complaints
    (record).
  - A Texas midwife was suspended about 3 years after two deaths (record; comparator).
  - The Origins owner got probation in 2015 and later ran the center where S23's harms
    occurred (narr. on the docket).
  - A convicted rapist obtained a nursing license through a registry misspelling (narr.).
  - Closed troubled-teen programs reopen under new names (narr.).
  - The father in S16 ran a business through nominee fronts while on probation (record).
- **Doc 05 relies on employers and licensing to enforce role restrictions.** The corpus
  shows those are the levers that lag furthest and are easiest to evade.
- **OBJ-030 should broaden** from "nominee fronts" to "**role restriction depends on the
  slowest and most evadable institutions**".
- *Disconfirming check:* this is a curated corpus, selected for failure. Board
  response-time base rates are needed before this becomes more than a pattern among
  instances.

**X2 — Pattern evidence works when it is admitted, and it cuts both ways** (doc 04, doc 07)
`[SUPPORTED]`.
- S17 (prior-act injury photos) and S16 (multi-victim corroboration) convicted.
- In S18, police **disbelieved a false sexual-assault report *because of the reporter's
  pattern***. That is the both-victims discriminator working: judge the report by the
  individual's pattern and the corroboration, not by the category of report.

**X3 — Both poles of the false-accusation problem are in the corpus** (doc 07; OBJ-028)
`[SUPPORTED]`.
- **False accusations used as a tactic** (record and narr.):
  - S18: Braun's false sexual-assault report;
  - S22: Stoner's alleged fabricated pastor accusation;
  - S18E12: a recanted childhood accusation.
- **The *claim* of false accusation used by abusers:**
  - S17: "judges always grant POs to women";
  - S16: "I've been accused falsely";
  - S18: the tattoo artist's "autism/culture" defence;
  - S18E13–14: a restraining order taken out against a whistleblower.
- **A survivor-centred podcast holds both**, which argues against either pole's
  generalization.
- Doc 07's anti-weaponization safeguards should name **both** channels explicitly:
  fabricated reports, *and* the legal process used against reporters.

**X4 — The burden of investigation shifts to victims; virality is the trigger** (doc 01,
new gap candidate) `[SUPPORTED]`.
- **Victims or their families did the investigating** in S16 (the host obtaining records),
  S17 (the sister finding the protective orders), S18 (the doulas), S19 (Amy's family),
  S20 and S23 (the mothers).
- **In S18 the state moved within a week of the case going viral.**
- **A justice system that responds to virality is unequal by design.** It favours victims
  with platforms, and it invites the extra-legal campaigns flagged in S20.
- Candidate doc 01 **gap 7**: investigative burden-shifting.

**X5 — Sentencing ceilings fall below the harm's duration** (doc 03; consistent with
OBJ-024 and S-13) `[SUPPORTED]`.
- **The instances:**
  - S19: the maximum is 4 years for 5 years of stalking, and the judge said so;
  - S18E9: a misdemeanor for a stated intent to kill followed by a beating;
  - S18: house arrest for a first offender, under a joint submission the judge called
    "distasteful".
- **These are victim-side confirmations** of the framework's conclusion that the criminal
  tier is small. They also show that the remedies victims most want (permanent protective
  orders, licensing bars) sit in **civil and administrative** channels, which is S-10's
  division of labour seen from the ground.

**X6 — Failure-to-protect liability lands on the coerced parent** (doc 03, doc 07)
`[SUPPORTED]` in one case, twice.

**X7 — Overriding a person's autonomy "for their own good" is coercive harm** (doc 02, doc 06,
doc 10 Finding C) `[NORMATIVE]` for the definition; `[ESTABLISHED]` for the documented
abuses. *Rewritten 2026-10-04 on an operator challenge, conceded.*

- **The earlier version was wrong.** It treated "imposition" as a possibly neutral baseline
  and "abuse" as a separate thing added on top. It then warned that the troubled-teen
  evidence "cannot separate imposition from abuse."
- **That split is the care-register move the framework exists to name.** Taking a child by
  force at night, isolating them, controlling their communication, and calling it treatment
  is not a neutral act that sometimes turns abusive. **The override is the coercion, and
  coercion is the harm this framework names.** Attack therapy, sleep and food deprivation,
  and restraint are the same move made more severe. They are not a different category.
- **The same structure runs through the institutional record:**
  - Packard, Nottidge, Lowe and Bulwer-Lytton were committed by husbands and families;
  - Willowbrook's consent was extracted as the price of a bed;
  - Dully was lobotomized at 12 on a stepmother's account;
  - the S16 crisis call was answered with guns "for his safety".

  In every case, someone with power over a person overrode that person's will and called it
  care. (Sources: veriticide-general-ledger
  `docs/institutionalization-coercive-treatment-history-2026-10-03.md`, revised the same day.)
- **The corrected structure, which is what doc 07 has to work with:** every override is a
  harm. Whether a *particular* override can be **justified** is a separate question, in the
  way self-defence justifies force without making force harmless. The candidates are
  incapacity and imminent lethal danger. The burden sits with the person doing the
  overriding. The historical record shows that burden is usually claimed by those the
  override benefits.
- **For doc 06.** The self-directed-only constraint is not one design preference among
  others. It follows from this definition: an "intervention" imposed on someone is an
  instance of the harm the framework targets.
- **For doc 05.** The accommodation model must be checked against this too. Role
  restrictions imposed on a documented-conduct basis are overrides of the restricted
  person's autonomy, so they need the same justification test. Flagged for `/liberty-review`.
