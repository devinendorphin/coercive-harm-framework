# Research: Soft-Law Enforceability Levers for the Accommodation Model (doc 05)

*Compiled 2026-07-09 for the doc-05 enforceability frontier. Doc 05's weakest element:
dropping the ADA disability hook (to resolve OBJ-004) also dropped the legal *duty* to
accommodate, leaving voluntary org design. These levers give it teeth **without** a
diagnosis mandate — all conduct-based.*

## Lever 1 (primary) — Negligent retention / supervision (EXISTING tort) `[ESTABLISHED]`

- **Doctrine:** an employer is liable when it **knows or should know** an employee poses
  a danger to others, **fails to act** (terminate, **reassign**, or otherwise address),
  the harm is **foreseeable**, and **damages** result. Classic trigger: "keep employees
  who sexually harass coworkers despite complaints."
- **Why it is the teeth:** the accommodation model's "no reports / no budget"
  **reassignment/role-design is explicitly a recognized corrective action** that
  *discharges* this duty. Conversely, an employer who ignores a **documented course of
  coercive conduct** and leaves the person with authority over others is **exposed** to
  negligent-retention/supervision liability if harm follows. So the role-design move
  converts from "nice-to-have" to **liability-driven risk management** — and it is
  **conduct-based** (documented awareness + foreseeability), requiring **no diagnosis**.
- This is the cleanest fit with the whole framework: it protects *potential
  subordinates* (the point of the role-design) via the employer's *existing* duty of
  care, not via a new protected class.
- **Both-victims caution:** liability exposure can push employers to *over*-react —
  defensively sidelining anyone accused. The doc 05 §4 safeguard (documented,
  non-pretextual conduct assessment; review/appeal) must gate the liability-driven
  response, or the mechanism becomes a weaponization vector.

## Lever 2 — Insurance (EPLI / D&O) `[SUPPORTED]`

Employment Practices Liability and Directors & Officers insurers already price premiums
on governance practices and drive anti-harassment adoption. They can **require or reward**
conduct-based role-design + documented-conduct processes as a condition/discount — a
market lever that spreads the template without legislation.

## Lever 3 — Procurement / grant conditions `[NORMATIVE]`

Public funders and grantors condition funding on adopting conduct-based role-design and
honest-capacity practices (as they already do with anti-harassment training). Directly
relevant to the nonprofit/agency scale (the seed's Flatbush example, L69) where funding
is the operative lever.

## Lever 4 — Sector codes / accreditation `[NORMATIVE]`

Professional bodies, accreditors, and standards regimes (e.g., B-Corp-style) embed the
administrative template (doc 05 §2) as a best practice; **adoption becomes evidence of
due care** (which loops back to Lever 1's liability calculus).

## The honest limit

None of these creates a **direct legal entitlement to accommodation for the person.**
They create **employer incentives** to adopt conduct-based role design. That is
consistent with the framework's aim (protect potential subordinates + honest
role-matching, not a new protected class) — but it means the person's interest in
*non-stigmatizing* treatment is protected only by how well the organization executes,
gated by the §4 safeguards. Enforceability is now **partially** answered (real
incentives exist), not fully (no individual right).
