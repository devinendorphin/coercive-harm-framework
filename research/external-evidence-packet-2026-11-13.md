# Coercive Harm: An External Evidence Packet (Durvasula Cluster and Outward)

Outside research establishes most of these eight background claims well enough to cite as settled. Coercive control is a named statutory offence with thousands of convictions in the UK. Psychological abuse is linked to PTSD and depression independent of physical violence. Lethality risk can be forecast, although only moderately well. Abusers' denial and counter-attack tactics measurably shift what bystanders believe. Three claims are weaker than they are usually stated: that the repertoire was derived independently, that it transfers across settings, and that perpetrators are incorrigible. Ramani Durvasula's own peer-reviewed record is in HIV and personality-disorder health psychology. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramani_Durvasula) [grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/Ramani_Durvasula) Her "narcissistic abuse" framing is clinical and popular, not empirical. The empirical base for narcissism-linked partner harm comes from other groups, mainly Grenyer and Day at Wollongong.

## TL;DR
- **Settled enough to cite:**
  - England and Wales police recorded 49,557 controlling or coercive behaviour offences in the year ending March 2025.
  - Scotland's course-of-conduct offence produced 897 convictions in 2023-24, with conviction rates of 84–91%.
  - A 194-study meta-analysis (Dokkedahl et al., 2022) found psychological IPV most strongly associated with PTSD.
  - The Danger Assessment discriminated attempted femicide at AUC .90 in development. Pooled independent AUCs are lower (~.62).
- **Settled with boundary conditions:**
  - Trait prevalence is single-digit. NPD lifetime prevalence is 6.2% in NESARC, but a systematic review of seven interview-based community studies (Dhawan et al., 2010) found a mean of 1.06% (range 0–6.2%). Psychopathy is 1.2% when PCL-measured, against a 4.5% pooled estimate across all instruments.
  - DARVO measurably lowers observers' belief in victims, and education reduces that effect.
  - Batterer programmes show small effects: d ≈ 0.18 by police report.
- **Not settled:**
  - Truly independent derivations of the repertoire. Herman and Stark both built on Biderman.
  - Measurement-invariant transfer of one instrument across all settings.
  - Peer-reviewed validation of Hassan's BITE model.
  - "Narcissistic abuse" as a distinct construct.
  - Whether coercive control law reduces harm, as opposed to increasing case counts.

**Interpretation in one paragraph.** Three findings are robust and replicated: coercive and psychological abuse is common, it is measurable, and it is associated with clinical injury. They rest on national surveys, statutory data and meta-analyses. They can be cited as background. Several things are weaker:
- Predictive tools work above chance but not precisely.
- Personality-trait prevalence depends heavily on the instrument used. [frontiersin](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.661044/xml)
- Perpetrator programmes produce small average change.

Neither "incorrigible" nor "readily treatable" is supported. The data show small, heterogeneous effects. Finally, "narcissistic abuse" is a clinical-popular label. The underlying behaviours are documented, but the label has not been validated as a distinct construct.

## (b) Claim-by-Claim Evidence

### Claim 1 — Coercion runs a finite, recognisable repertoire

**Core derivations**
- **Biderman (1957).** He analysed USAF POWs held in Korea and set out methods the captors used to induce compliance:
  - isolation
  - monopolisation of perception
  - induced debility and exhaustion
  - threats
  - occasional indulgences
  - demonstrating omnipotence
  - degradation
  - enforcing trivial demands

  Amnesty International later reproduced it as the "chart of coercion." Tier: clinical-theoretical / observational.
- **Lifton (1961).** He interviewed Western and Chinese subjects of Chinese "thought reform" and set out eight criteria of ideological totalism, among them milieu control, loading the language, and doctrine over person. [dareassociation](https://dareassociation.org/bdev/bdb_archive/BDB_24.1/BDB_2019.24.1_97-107.pdf) Tier: clinical-qualitative.
- **Stark (2007), *Coercive Control*.** He reframed partner abuse as a "liberty crime" built from intimidation, isolation, degradation and control, the last including micro-regulation of everyday life. Tier: clinical-theoretical, drawing on forensic casework.

**Further derivations from separate data**
- **Duluth Power and Control Wheel (Pence & Paymar, 1980s).** Built from focus groups with battered women in Duluth, Minnesota. Its eight spokes include isolation, economic abuse, intimidation, and using children. Tier: practitioner.
- **Tolman (1989), Psychological Maltreatment of Women Inventory.** A psychometric item pool on dominance-isolation and emotional-verbal abuse. Tier: peer-reviewed psychometric.
- **Dutton & Goodman (2005), *Sex Roles*.** They model coercion as a demand, plus a credible threat, plus surveillance, plus delivery of consequences. This is a mechanism-level account rather than a list. Tier: theoretical, peer-reviewed.
- **Rodríguez-Carballeira and colleagues (2005 onward).** A taxonomy of psychological-abuse strategies built by comparing partner, workplace and manipulative-group contexts. It later became the basis for the PAEGS scale (see Claim 2). [behavioralpsycho](https://www.behavioralpsycho.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/02._Saldana_26-3oa.pdf) Tier: peer-reviewed.
- **Lehmann, Simmons & Pillai (2012), Checklist of Controlling Behaviors.** Tier: peer-reviewed psychometric.

**Evidence that complicates the claim**
- **The derivations are not fully independent.** Herman (1992) explicitly draws on Biderman, and Stark cites Biderman and the hostage literature. Their convergence is partly shared lineage, not independent discovery. Duluth (survivor focus groups), Tolman (psychometric), Rodríguez-Carballeira (cross-context comparison) and Einarsen's workplace NAQ-R (see Claim 2) are more independent.
- **Typology dispute.** Michael Johnson (2008) distinguishes "intimate terrorism," which is coercive-control-driven, from "situational couple violence," which is conflict-driven and not organised around control. On this view, much partner aggression does not follow the coercive repertoire.
- **Gender-symmetry dispute.** Archer's (2000) meta-analysis found near-parity in acts of physical aggression. Coercive-control scholars respond that act counts miss the pattern.

The repertoire is recognisable, but it is not universal across all abusive relationships.

**Status.** There is a strong case for a recurring, recognisable cluster of tactics. There is a weaker case for a closed or finite list. No study has tested whether the repertoire is exhaustive.

### Claim 2 — The repertoire transfers across scales and settings

**Partner and family**
- Day, Townsend & Grenyer (2020) used qualitative thematic analysis of 436 people close to someone with pathological narcissistic traits: 56.2% current partners, 19.7% former partners and 21.3% family members. [medium](https://medium.com/the-reflective-mindset/living-with-narcissism-a-study-of-the-impact-on-partners-and-family-members-narcissism-research-9cefd8a33c71)
- Respondents described the same themes in romantic and family relationships: control, emotional abuse, and switching between idealisation and devaluation.
- A 2022 analysis by the same group (*Personality and Mental Health*) catalogued physical, verbal, emotional, sexual and financial abuse themes. [wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/pmh.1532) Tier: peer-reviewed qualitative.

**Children.** Katz (2016, 2022) showed that coercive-control regimes directly constrain children, not only their mothers. This extends the repertoire to the household as a whole. Tier: peer-reviewed qualitative.

**High-control groups**
- **Rodríguez-Carballeira et al.** built one taxonomy explicitly across partner, workplace and group contexts. This is the most direct cross-setting test available, although it is conceptual rather than measurement-invariant.
- **PAEGS (Saldaña et al., 2017, *European Journal of Psychology Applied to Legal Context*).** A 31-item scale for psychological abuse experienced in groups. The English validation used n = 138 ex-members and found a single factor. [researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/316716833_Development_and_validation_of_the_Psychological_Abuse_Experienced_in_Groups_Scale) [semanticscholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/evelopment-and-validation-of-the-Psychological-n-Salda%C3%B1aa-Rodr%C3%ADguez-Carballeiraa/c2d194b883cee734f3b4a0e9c0a59b8b35ea197e) The Spanish version had α = .97, and Japanese and Korean validations exist. [researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/316716833_Development_and_validation_of_the_Psychological_Abuse_Experienced_in_Groups_Scale) [wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jpr.12166) [behavioralpsycho](https://www.behavioralpsycho.com/product/the-psychological-abuse-experienced-in-groups-scale-psychometric-properties-of-the-spanish-version/?lang=en) At a cut-off above 39 it showed sensitivity of 94.1% and specificity of 95.3% (reported in a later Saldaña et al. paper). [tandfonline](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/20008198.2021.1954776) Tier: peer-reviewed psychometric.
- **Group Psychological Abuse Scale (Chambers, Langone et al., 1994, *Cultic Studies Journal*).** It started from 308 former members of 101 groups. The final scale has 28 items in four factors (Compliance, Exploitation, Mind Control, Anxious Dependency), with α = .81. [psychiatrictimes](https://www.psychiatrictimes.com/view/clinical-update-cults) [icsahome](https://articles2.icsahome.com/articles/psychological-abuse-theory-measurement-langone-en5-1) The journal is non-indexed and the sample was recruited through an ex-member network. Tier: psychometric, lower-visibility venue.
- **Hassan's BITE model** (Behaviour, Information, Thought, Emotional control):
  - It has no peer-reviewed journal psychometric validation. [quora](https://www.quora.com/As-Hassan-s-peer-reviewed-BITE-model-overwhelmingly-implicates-The-Church-of-Jesus-Christ-of-Latter-day-Saints-as-a-destructive-cult-should-an-investigation-into-human-rights-violations-be-opened)
  - Hassan & Shah (2019, *Ethics, Medicine and Public Health*) is mainly conceptual. It reports an online pilot (n = 1,033) described as "preliminary." [dareassociation](https://dareassociation.org/bdev/bdb_archive/BDB_24.1/BDB_2019.24.1_97-107.pdf)
  - Hassan's 2020 Fielding dissertation (n = 1,044) reported high internal consistency. Its principal-component analysis found one factor, "Authoritarian Control," not the four theorised domains. [freedomofmind](https://freedomofmind.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Dr-Hassan-Dissertation-Published.pdf) The sample was self-selected ex-members with no comparison group. [bitterwinter](https://bitterwinter.org/the-truth-please-about-steven-hassan-1-the-bite-model-as-pseudoscience/)
  - Critics such as Introvigne, writing in CESNUR outlets, raise these design issues. [bitterwinter](https://bitterwinter.org/the-truth-please-about-steven-hassan-1-the-bite-model-as-pseudoscience/) [cesnur](https://cesnur.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/tjoc_10_2_2_introvigne.pdf) Those outlets defend new religious movements, so their critiques are partisan, although the design points match the dissertation itself.

  Tier: clinical-theoretical / unpublished dissertation.
- **Lalich (2004), *Bounded Choice*; Stein (2017), *Terror, Love and Brainwashing*.** Lalich drew on case studies of Heaven's Gate and a political cult. Stein frames group control as attachment disorganisation. Tier: scholarly qualitative / theoretical.

**Workplace**
- Einarsen's Negative Acts Questionnaire–Revised (Einarsen, Hoel & Notelaers, 2009) has 22 items in work-related, person-related and physical-intimidation factors. They overlap heavily with isolation, degradation and surveillance tactics.
- Prevalence depends on method (Nielsen, Matthiesen & Einarsen, 2010 meta-analysis): 11.3% with self-labelling against a definition, 14.8% with the behavioural-experience method. Random samples run lower than non-random ones (9.3% vs 15.5% for self-labelling). [springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00420-018-1366-8)
- Tepper's abusive-supervision construct (2000) is the supervisory analogue. Tier: peer-reviewed.

**Institutions.** Smith & Freyd (2013) documented institutional betrayal, in which institutions minimise, cover up or punish reports, as an added layer of harm (see Claim 7). [upenn](https://web.english.upenn.edu/~cavitch/pdf-library/Smith_and_Freyd_Instituional_Betrayal.pdf) [uoregon](https://dynamic.uoregon.edu/jjf/articles/sf2014.pdf)

**Status.** Convergent tactic lists are well documented across settings. No study has used one instrument across partner, family, workplace, group and institutional samples with formal measurement-invariance testing. Cross-setting "transfer" is therefore a strong inference from parallel literatures, not yet a direct finding.

### Claim 3 — Coercive control has survived adversarial legal testing

**England and Wales**
- Serious Crime Act 2015 s.76, in force 29 December 2015. [ons](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/crimeandjustice/articles/domesticabuseprevalenceandtrendsenglandandwales/yearendingmarch2025) Maximum sentence originally 5 years. The Domestic Abuse Act 2021 extended it to post-separation abuse.
- ONS: 49,557 offences recorded in the year ending March 2025, against 45,310 the year before and 33,954 in the year ending March 2021. [womensaid](https://womensaid.org.uk/information-support/what-is-domestic-abuse/coercive-control/) [ons](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/crimeandjustice/articles/domesticabuseprevalenceandtrendsenglandandwales/yearendingmarch2021)
- 1,299 defendants were proceeded against and 853 convicted in calendar 2024; 832 of the 853 were male. [womensaid](https://womensaid.org.uk/information-support/what-is-domestic-abuse/coercive-control/)
- Convictions are about 1.7% of recorded offences. The two figures come from different periods and populations, so this is not a cohort attrition rate.

**Scotland**
- Domestic Abuse (Scotland) Act 2018 s.1, a course-of-behaviour offence covering partners and ex-partners, in force 1 April 2019. [copfs](https://www.copfs.gov.uk/about-copfs/news/domestic-abuse-and-stalking-charges-in-scotland-2024-25) [copfs](https://www.copfs.gov.uk/about-copfs/news/domestic-abuse-and-stalking-charges-in-scotland-2022-23)
- Convictions for a main DASA charge rose from 212 (2019-20) to 897 (2023-24). Prosecutions were 252, 418, 812, 897 and 1,039 over the same years, giving conviction rates of 84%, 91%, 85%, 84% and 86%. [copfs](https://www.copfs.gov.uk/about-copfs/news/domestic-abuse-and-stalking-charges-in-scotland-2023-24) [copfs](https://www.copfs.gov.uk/about-copfs/news/domestic-abuse-and-stalking-charges-in-scotland-2024-25)

**Published evaluations of policing**
- **Barlow, Johnson, Walklate & Humphreys (2020), *British Journal of Criminology*.** In one English force, coercive-control crimes were 0.8% of domestic-abuse crimes (156 cases) by June 2017. They had lower arrest and "solved" rates than other domestic-abuse crimes. - **Brennan, Myhill and colleagues (2021).** Force-wide "Domestic Abuse Matters" training was associated with a 41% increase in arrests for controlling or coercive behaviour, about three additional arrests per force per month. The effect lasted about eight months.

**United States**

**Critiques**
- Goodmark (2018) argues that criminalisation expands the carceral state and may harm marginalised victims.
- Walklate & Fitz-Gibbon (2019) question the "power of law."
- Barlow et al. found that officers still prioritise physical injury. [lancashire](https://knowledge.lancashire.ac.uk/id/eprint/54304/1/54304%20Barlow%20&%20Walklate%20VOR.pdf) [liverpool](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3050204/1/barlow%20et%20al%20author%20accepted%20version.pdf)

**Status.** The offence works in practice in two UK jurisdictions: it survives prosecution, defence and appeal, with high conviction rates once a case is charged. There is no published evidence yet on whether criminalisation reduces victimisation.

### Claim 4 — Psychological and coercive harm produces measurable injury

**Mental health**
- **Dokkedahl, Kirubakaran, Bech-Hansen, Kristensen & Elklit (2022), *Systematic Reviews*.**
  - 194 studies, with k = 149 in random-effects meta-analyses. [nih](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9364557/)
  - Psychological IPV was associated with PTSD, depression and anxiety, most strongly PTSD, in both women and men. [researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362608028_The_psychological_subtype_of_intimate_partner_violence_and_its_effect_on_mental_health_a_systematic_review_with_meta-analyses) [nih](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9364557/)
  - The authors note gender-skewed samples and mostly cross-sectional designs. [nih](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9364557/)
  - An earlier review they cite found psychological violence to be an independent risk factor after controlling for physical and sexual IPV. [nih](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9364557/)

  Tier: meta-analysis.
- **Complex trauma.**
  - Herman (1992) proposed complex PTSD for prolonged captivity-type trauma, including domestic captivity.
  - ICD-11 (WHO) adopted CPTSD as a diagnosis.
  - Cloitre et al. (2019), in a US population sample, estimated PTSD at 3.4% and CPTSD at 3.8%.

  Tier: clinical-theoretical → nosology → epidemiology.
- **Narcissism-linked relationships.**
  - Day, Townsend & Grenyer (2022) found that relatives reported high levels of anxiety, depression, self-aggression and somatic concerns. [cerevity](https://cerevity.com/narcissistic-abuse/)
  - A 2025 study by Day, Kealy, Biberdzic, Green, Denmeade & Grenyer (*Personality and Mental Health*, 19(4), e70038) had n = 135 informants: 71% romantic partners, with an average relationship length of 20 years. Pathological narcissism in the relative was associated with coercive control (r = .18) but not with abuse. Personality-disorder severity was moderately associated with both.
  - In both studies the relative was never clinically assessed. [cerevity](https://cerevity.com/narcissistic-abuse/)

  Tier: peer-reviewed, informant-report.
- **Workplace.** Nielsen & Einarsen (2012) pooled 66 cross-sectional samples (N = 77,721): bullying correlated r = .23–.37 with mental and physical health problems, PTSD symptoms and burnout. In 13 longitudinal samples (N = 62,916), bullying predicted later mental-health problems, and baseline problems predicted later bullying. [semanticscholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Outcomes-of-exposure-to-workplace-bullying:-A-Nielsen-Einarsen/857ec2bc697243606616aca0fbafc8b892681e6d) [researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/326193463_What_we_know_what_we_do_not_know_and_what_we_should_and_could_have_known_about_workplace_bullying_An_overview_of_the_literature_and_agenda_for_future_research) Tier: meta-analysis.
- **Institutional betrayal.** Smith & Freyd (2013), *Journal of Traumatic Stress*:
  - 345 women, 233 of whom reported unwanted sexual experiences; 46% of those 233 reported institutional betrayal. [uoregon](https://dynamic.uoregon.edu/jjf/articles/sf2013.pdf)
  - The interaction between sexual experiences and betrayal predicted trauma-specific sexual symptoms (β = .34) and anxiety (β = .25). [uoregon](https://dynamic.uoregon.edu/jjf/articles/sf2013.pdf)
  - The paper's own table flags only these two interactions as significant, although the text claims four. [uoregon](https://dynamic.uoregon.edu/jjf/articles/sf2013.pdf)
  - The betrayal checklist was new and had not been validated. [uoregon](https://dynamic.uoregon.edu/jjf/articles/sf2013.pdf)

  Tier: peer-reviewed, cross-sectional.

**Economic cost**
- **England and Wales.** Home Office (Oliver et al., 2019) estimated domestic abuse cost about £66 billion in one year (2016/17). Most of that, about £47 billion, was victims' physical and emotional harms. The model does not isolate coercive control.
- **United States.** Peterson et al. (2018, *American Journal of Preventive Medicine*) estimated the lifetime per-victim cost of IPV at $103,767 for women and $23,414 for men. The total is nearly $3.6 trillion (2014 US$) across 43 million US adults with a victimisation history, of which government sources pay about $1.3 trillion (37%).

**Status.** Solidly established that psychological IPV is associated with clinical injury independent of physical violence. Causal direction is better supported in the workplace literature, which has longitudinal designs, than in the IPV literature, which is mostly cross-sectional.

### Claim 5 — The pattern is predictable or forecastable

**Danger Assessment (Campbell)**
- The 11-city case-control study (Campbell et al., 2003, *American Journal of Public Health*) interviewed proxies of 220 femicide victims and 343 abused controls. The final model classified 73% of cases and 93% of controls correctly. [thetrace](https://www.thetrace.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/FemicideDr.Campbell.pdf)
- Validation (Campbell, Webster & Glass, 2009, *Journal of Interpersonal Violence*): 310 femicide cases vs 324 controls. The weighted DA, tested on 194 attempted femicides, gave AUC .90. [johnshopkins](https://pure.johnshopkins.edu/en/publications/the-danger-assessment-validation-of-a-lethality-risk-assessment-i-3/)
- A prospective study of 782 women found AUC .687 for severe re-assault. [sanctuaryforfamilies](https://www.sanctuaryforfamilies.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/The-Danger-Assessment.pdf) [ojp](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/grants/247456.pdf)
- About half of the femicide victims had not accurately perceived their own risk. [sanctuaryforfamilies](https://www.sanctuaryforfamilies.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/The-Danger-Assessment.pdf) [ojp](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/grants/209731.pdf)

**Independent synthesis.** Messing & Thaller (2013) gave sample-weighted AUCs for five tools: [researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/335564185_The_Danger_Assessment_An_Instrument_for_the_Prevention_of_Intimate_Partner_Homicide)

| Tool | AUC | k |
|---|---|---|
| ODARA | .666 | 5 |
| SARA | .628 | 6 |
| DA | .618 | 4 |
| DVSI | .582 | 3 |
| K-SID | .537 | 2 |

**DASH (UK police)**
- Turner, Medina-Ariza & Brown (2019, *British Journal of Criminology*) found DASH grading near chance (AUC ≈ .55). A model built on police records reached about .75. [doi](https://doi.org/10.3390/forensicsci5040064)
- Their 2022 follow-up on a dataset of about 350,000 incidents found AUC .748 for IPV and .763 for non-IPV. The DASH questions added almost nothing. [researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/361342251_Predicting_Domestic_Abuse_Fairly_and_Police_Risk_Assessment)
- A 2025 study in *Forensic Sciences* found DASH AUC .67 for deadly violence and .62 for persistent offending, and .80 for a shortened version. The authors stress that risk tools exist to prevent harm, not to predict it. [researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/345039139_Dashing_Hopes_the_Predictive_Accuracy_of_Domestic_Abuse_Risk_Assessment_by_Police) [cardiff](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/182374/1/forensicsci-05-00064.pdf)

**Homicide timeline**
- Monckton Smith (2020, *Violence Against Women*) used 372 intimate-partner femicides from 575 UK cases (2012–2015) to set out an eight-stage progression: [dvact](https://www.dvact.org/post/do-you-know-the-8-step-timeline-in-domestic-abuse-homicides) [houseofpeacepubs](https://www.houseofpeacepubs.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Femicide-progression-Monckton.pdf)
  - pre-relationship history
  - early commitment
  - controlling relationship
  - trigger
  - escalation
  - change in thinking
  - planning
  - homicide
- Coercive control and stalking recur in the early stages. About 30% of cases were "short-gap," with little time between trigger and homicide. [glos](https://eprints.glos.ac.uk/6896/1/6896%20Monckton-Smith%20%282019%29%20Intimate%20Partner%20Femicide%20using%20Foucauldian......pdf) [researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334972908_Intimate_Partner_Femicide_Using_Foucauldian_Analysis_to_Track_an_Eight_Stage_Progression_to_Homicide)
- The model is descriptive (temporal sequencing). [glos](https://eprints.glos.ac.uk/6896/) It has not been validated prospectively and has no published AUC.

**Status.** Risk can be forecast above chance, with independent AUCs of about .60–.75. That is a "moderate" level. Lethality is rare, so prediction produces many false positives. [springer](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s41887-017-0011-1.pdf) Prior controlling behaviour combined with separation is a consistent risk signal.

### Claim 6 — Trait prevalence is single-digit and spread across populations

**Narcissistic personality disorder**
- Stinson et al. (2008), NESARC Wave 2, face-to-face interviews with n = 34,653. [valleyspringrecovery](https://valleyspringrecovery.com/mental-health-conditions/narcissistic-personality-disorder/)
- Lifetime NPD 6.2%: men 7.7%, women 4.8%. [psychiatrist](https://www.psychiatrist.com/jcp/prevalence-correlates-disability-comorbidity-dsm-iv-narcissistic-personality-disorder-wave-2-nesarc/)
- Prevalence was higher among Black men and women, Hispanic women, younger adults, and adults who were separated, divorced, widowed or never married. [psychiatrist](https://www.psychiatrist.com/jcp/prevalence-correlates-disability-comorbidity-dsm-iv-narcissistic-personality-disorder-wave-2-nesarc/)
- Comorbidity was high: about 64% had a lifetime substance-use disorder. [valleyspringrecovery](https://valleyspringrecovery.com/mental-health-conditions/narcissistic-personality-disorder/)

**Contested NPD figures**
- A systematic review (Dhawan et al., 2010) covered 7 interview-based studies with 49,812 participants. It found community estimates of 0–6.2%, with a mean of 1.06%, or 0.26% if the NESARC study is excluded.
- Trull et al. (2010) reanalysed NESARC requiring distress or impairment for each criterion, which substantially lowered the personality-disorder estimates.
- 6.2% is therefore the upper bound, not the consensus.

**Psychopathy**
- Sanz-García et al. (2021, *Frontiers in Psychology*): 15 studies, 16 samples, 11,497 participants. [frontiersin](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.661044/full)

| Subgroup | Pooled prevalence (95% CI) |
|---|---|
| Overall | 4.5% (1.6–7.9) |
| PCL-R / PCL:SV only | 1.2% |
| Mostly self-report instruments | 5.4% |
| Community samples | 1.9% |
| University students | 8.1% |
| Organisational samples (3 samples, n = 668) | 12.9% |
| Men | 7.9% |
| Women | 2.9% |

- Heterogeneity was extreme (I² = 97.4%). [frontiersin](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.661044/xml)
- Babiak, Neumann & Hare (2010) assessed 203 corporate managers with the PCL-R. 8 of the 203 (3.9%) scored 30 or higher, the common research threshold, against about 1% in community samples. Mean scores were low, and this was a single convenience sample of high-potential managers.

**Dark triad and tetrad**
- Paulhus & Williams (2002) defined the dark triad, and Paulhus (2014) added sadism to make the tetrad. The traits are continuous dimensions with no diagnostic cut-off, so there is no "prevalence" figure.
- Men score higher. A meta-analysis by Grijalva et al. (2015) found a gender difference of d ≈ .26 in narcissism, largest for entitlement and exploitativeness.
- Miller, Lynam, Campbell and Crowe argue that antagonism (callousness, entitlement, manipulation) is the shared core of narcissism and psychopathy. That matters because interpersonal harm is linked to antagonism specifically, not to the grandiosity or self-esteem components.

**Status.** Clinical-threshold prevalence is single-digit, and probably 1–6% depending on the instrument. It is higher in men, younger adults and, for psychopathy, organisational samples. "Single-digit" holds, but the exact figure is instrument-dependent and contested.

### Claim 7 — Third-party recruitment and the production of doubt

**DARVO (Deny, Attack, Reverse Victim and Offender)**
- Freyd introduced the concept in 1997.
- Harsey, Zurbriggen & Freyd (2017): the more DARVO victims encountered when confronting the perpetrator, the more they blamed themselves. [ihavetherightto](https://ihavetherightto.org/blog/darvo-a-sinister-silencing-tactic/) [Substack](https://lucasbean.substack.com/p/maga-is-a-family-of-abusive-people)
- Harsey & Freyd (2020), *Journal of Aggression, Maltreatment & Trauma*, Experiment 1 (n = 316 students): observers exposed to DARVO rated the victim as less believable, more responsible and more abusive, and the perpetrator as less abusive. In Experiment 2, DARVO education reversed part of this effect. [semanticscholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Deny,-Attack,-and-Reverse-Victim-and-Offender-What-Harsey-Freyd/cef325b841f8899577fcaa67ab3662ad007b9bad)
- Harsey & Freyd (2023), *Journal of Interpersonal Violence* (n = 230):
  - DARVO raised perceived victim abusiveness (ηp² = .09) and lowered victim believability (ηp² = .08). [sagepub](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/08862605231169751)
  - It shifted willingness to punish toward the victim. [sagepub](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/08862605231169751)
  - Insincere apologies had minimal effect. [jjfreyd](https://www.jjfreyd.com/darvo)
- A separate replication (Harsey & Freyd, *Journal of Interpersonal Violence*, 2023) largely reproduced the credibility effects but not the victim-responsibility effect. [uoregon](https://dynamic.uoregon.edu/jjf/articles/hfjiv2023.pdf)

Tier: experimental, mostly with student vignette samples.

**Institutional betrayal.** Smith & Freyd (2013, 2014) frame institutions as secondary perpetrators: normalising, covering up, or punishing reporters. [uoregon](https://president.uoregon.edu/sites/default/files/institutional_betrayal-smith_freyd.pdf) [uoregon](https://dynamic.uoregon.edu/jjf/articles/sf2014.pdf) Tier: empirical plus theoretical.

**Gaslighting**
- Sweet (2019), *American Sociological Review*, based on life-story interviews with women in domestic-violence support groups. [cliffsnotes](https://www.cliffsnotes.com/study-notes/29145080)
- She argues gaslighting is sociological rather than psychological. Its power comes from mobilising gender stereotypes and institutional inequalities, such as courts and mental-health labels, to make victims seem "crazy." [sagepub](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0003122419874843) Tier: peer-reviewed qualitative.

**"Flying monkeys" and triangulation.** These are popular and clinical terms with no peer-reviewed operationalisation. The nearest empirical analogues are:
- DARVO's effect on observers
- institutional betrayal
- post-separation, litigation and "legal" abuse, for example concept analyses of post-separation abuse and emerging legal-abuse scales
- Katz's account of children being enlisted in coercive regimes

**Agnotology.** Proctor & Schiebinger (2008) define the deliberate production of ignorance, with tobacco-industry doubt-manufacturing as the paradigm case. Applying it to interpersonal or institutional coercion is analogical. No empirical study links agnotology to interpersonal coercion.

**Status.** Firmly established in experiments that perpetrator rhetoric shifts bystander judgments. Recruitment of specific third parties is documented qualitatively but has not been quantified.

### Claim 8 — Incorrigibility and performed contrition

**Batterer intervention**
- Babcock, Green & Robie (2004), 22 studies: d = 0.18 for police-reported recidivism (95% CI 0.11–0.25) and d = 0.18 for partner reports. That is about a 5% improvement over arrest alone. Duluth and CBT models did not differ. [nih](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK70372/) [york](https://www.crd.york.ac.uk/CRDWeb/ShowRecord.asp?AccessionNumber=12004009226&AccessionNumber=12004009226) [researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/8915306_Does_Batterers%27_Treatment_Work_A_Meta-Analytic_Review_of_Domestic_Violence_Treatment)
- Arce et al. (2020), 62 effect sizes covering 20,860 men, found larger effects (δ = 0.44). These were heavily moderated, and short programmes of under 16 sessions showed no significant effect. [redalyc](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/1798/179864006004/) [isciii](https://scielo.isciii.es/pdf/inter/v29n3/1132-0559-inter-29-3-0153.pdf)

The evidence shows small, real, heterogeneous average change. It supports neither "nothing works" nor "programmes reliably reform."

**Psychopathy**
- Rice, Harris & Cormier (1992) followed a maximum-security therapeutic community. High-PCL participants who went through it had higher violent recidivism than untreated high-PCL comparisons.
- Later reviews, including Salekin's, are more optimistic, especially for youth and for newer intervention models.

The evidence on psychopathy treatment remains mixed.

**NPD**
- No RCTs target NPD specifically. Reviews such as Weinberg & Ronningstam (2022) rely on case series and treatments adapted from BPD.
- Longitudinal data from Ronningstam and others, along with Wetzel et al. (2017), show that narcissistic traits decline on average across adulthood.

**Practitioner accounts.** Bancroft (2002, *Why Does He Do That?*) describes contrition as performed and as part of a cycle, from clinical work with abusers. Tier: popular-practitioner. Walker's "cycle of violence" contrition phase is similar.

**Status.** "Change is rare and small on average" is defensible. "Incorrigible" as a categorical claim is not supported by outcome data. No study directly measures how often contrition is performed rather than genuine.

## (e) Open or Underdetermined Questions

1. **Independence of the repertoire.** How much of the Biderman–Herman–Stark convergence is shared lineage? No study has derived a tactic taxonomy blind to the earlier lists and then compared it.
2. **Cross-setting measurement invariance.** No instrument has been tested for invariance across partner, family, workplace, group and institutional samples. PAEGS and NAQ-R are candidates.
3. **"Narcissistic abuse" as a construct.**
   - Evidence so far comes from informants, with self-selected samples and no clinical assessment of the alleged perpetrator. [simplypsychology](https://www.simplypsychology.org/narcissistic-love-bombing-cycle.html) [cerevity](https://cerevity.com/narcissistic-abuse/)
   - Day et al.'s 2025 finding links pathological narcissism to coercive control but not to abuse once personality-disorder severity is modelled. [cerevity](https://cerevity.com/narcissistic-abuse/) That points to severity and antagonism, not narcissism per se.
4. **Prevalence estimates.** NESARC's 6.2% is contested (Trull et al. 2010). Psychopathy estimates swing fourfold depending on the instrument. [frontiersin](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.661044/text) Dark-trait scores have no clinical cut-off.
5. **Gender patterns.** NISVS shows near-parity in psychological aggression, while 97.5% of E&W CCB convicts are male. [researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/373579861_The_National_Intimate_Partner_and_Sexual_Violence_Survey_Report_on_Intimate_Partner_Violence_The_National_Intimate_Partner_and_Sexual_Violence_Survey_20162017_Report_on_Intimate_Partner_Violence_Ackno) The gap may reflect measurement (acts vs patterns), reporting, or real asymmetry. It is unresolved.
6. **Does criminalisation reduce harm?** There is no outcome evaluation of victimisation or homicide rates after s.76 or DASA. The published evidence covers case-processing only.
7. **Predictive ceiling.** Independent AUCs of about .60–.75 may be close to the ceiling for rare events. The homicide timeline has not been tested prospectively.
8. **Treatment.** There are no NPD-specific RCTs. Psychopathy treatment findings conflict. Batterer programme effects vary widely by design and duration. No study measures performed versus genuine contrition.
9. **BITE model.** It has no peer-reviewed psychometric validation, and its only factor analysis contradicts the four-domain structure. [freedomofmind](https://freedomofmind.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Dr-Hassan-Dissertation-Published.pdf)
10. **Agnotology and "flying monkeys."** Both are analogical or popular. No validated measure of third-party recruitment exists.
11. **Causality in IPV injury.** Most IPV-to-mental-health data are cross-sectional. Workplace longitudinal data show effects running both ways. [semanticscholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Outcomes-of-exposure-to-workplace-bullying:-A-Nielsen-Einarsen/857ec2bc697243606616aca0fbafc8b892681e6d)
12. **Durvasula's framing.** Her clinical claims, such as narcissism being largely untreatable or a societal "epidemic," come from popular books and talks. [ted](https://www.ted.com/talks/ramani_durvasula_narcissism_and_its_discontents) [psychologytoday](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/quantum-leaps/202002/narcissism-and-what-do-about-it) They have not been tested in her peer-reviewed output. [apa](https://www.apa.org/news/podcasts/speaking-of-psychology/narcissism) [grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/Ramani_Durvasula)
